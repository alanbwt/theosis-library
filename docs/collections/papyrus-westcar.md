## Mapping method

1. Column of every section from the TLA column,line numbers; frame of every column from the SMB object titles. Each photograph was opened and checked for the number of columns it shows (3, 2, 4, 3).
2. English to Egyptian: Petrie's text was cut into 32 runs at sentence boundaries (opening words listed in `SECTIONS`) and each run matched to a run of TLA sentences by reading Petrie against the TLA German sentence translations. Sections that cross from one column to the next on the same frame point at that frame; section 16 is entirely in column VI.

## Known gaps and caveats

- Petrie's first paragraph ("One day, when King Khufu reigned ...") is his own invented introduction (stated in his Remarks) and is left out.
- Columns I-III are badly damaged; the TLA shows the restorations in square brackets, while Petrie's English reads a continuous text that includes the restorations of his time. Noted in the scan notes of sections 1-9.
- Petrie's spelling of names is old-fashioned (Dedi, Uba-aner, Rud-didet, Zazamankh) and the Gutenberg text has the misprint "The royal sou Bau-f-ra"; kept as printed.
- Petrie gives Ruddjedet's delivery date as "the 26th of the month Tybi"; the TLA reading differs. Such differences are the translator's and are not changed.

## Open questions

- Column-level crops were not made: the frames show several columns side by side and the boundaries between damaged columns I-III are not clear enough to crop without guessing. Each section points to the whole frame and names the column.
