# Behistun inscription of Darius I (DB, Old Persian) - NOT BUILT

Status: not built (no collection file). Findings for a later attempt.

- Text: ORACC ARIo Q007134 "Darius I 01" (DB) has the full Old Persian text, columns i-v, with line labels ("Persian i 1"
  ...) and English by paragraph (rowspans); page credits CC BY-SA 3.0, JSON metadata CC0. Downloaded:
  `data/sources/behistun/Q007134.html`, `ario.zip`, `ario_json/`.
- Photographs checked (Wikimedia Commons, category "Behistun Inscription" and searches): every usable photograph of the Old
  Persian columns is taken from the ground ~60 m below; they show only the top 14-22 lines of the columns under the relief
  ("Behistun relief.jpg" 7000 px, "39 Bistoun.jpg", "Behistun Inscription in Persia ca. 520 BC ..."), with heavy noise; signs
  cannot be read line by line. Since about 2000 scaffolding hides the lower columns in all recent photographs. Albert Kahn
  autochromes (1927, A53552S-A53563S) and Schwarzenbach (1930s) are distant views. "BehistunInscriptiondetail.jpg" (1967) is a
  close-up of an unidentified patch. "Behistun DB1 1-15.jpg" is Rawlinson's hand copy, not a photograph.
- King & Thompson 1907 (archive.org sculpturesinscri00brituoft): plates I-XVI show the sculptures, rock and map; none shows a
  Persian column whole.
- Livius.org has relief photos only. The ICHHTO photogrammetric survey is not openly available.
- One identification made: in "Behistun relief.jpg" the column under the left half of the relief (x ~550-2700 of 7000) matches
  Rawlinson's copy of DB i 1-15 (water channel near the right end, diagonal crack), so it is probably column i; not used.
- A feasible partial later: the captions DBa-DBk on the relief (ARIo Q007135-Q007145), which sit beside figures that are well
  photographed in close-up on Commons, with King & Thompson's key plates (VI, XIII) for their positions.
