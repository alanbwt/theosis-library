## Mapping method

The lines run along the cylinder and follow one another round it, so each photograph shows a band of about 18 lines. The
bands were fixed by matching the breaks on the photographs with Rawlinson's copy and with ARIo's brackets:
- back view (turned upright): the break running down from the upper edge at about 1/3-2/3 of the line length = Rawlinson's
  lacuna in lines 5-11 (middle of lines 5-8 lost, narrowing in 9-11; ARIo brackets in the middle of lines 6-11); at the top
  edge the preserved start of line 5 (left) and the ends of lines 3-5 (right); about 12 lines below the break (12 to ~22).
- front view: the large break with a crack below it at 57-76 % of the line = Rawlinson's lacuna in lines 21-31 (narrow 21-23,
  widest 24-29; ARIo brackets there in 24-31); its tip touches the upper edge (line 21).
Only the orientation that puts the front view upright and the back view upside down fits Rawlinson's damage map. Lines 20-22
are listed on both photographs. Lines are counted outward from the breaks; no line was read sign by sign on the photographs.

## Gaps and reasons

- Lines 1-5: badly broken (Rawlinson shows only the ends of 1-4); only line 5 and the ends of 3-4 reach the edge of the back view.
- Lines 37-46: on the underside of the cylinder as displayed; no openly available photograph shows them. britishmuseum.org
  (which has full photographs) returns a Cloudflare bot check and was not used. ARIo's text of 36-45 also uses the Yale fragment
  NBC 2504 (not part of BM 90920).

## Open questions

- The line counts at the lower edges of the two views (lines ~20-23 and below 36) are the least certain part; the sections
  were chosen so that each one's lines lie well inside a view, except 20-22, which lists both.
- A third photograph of the underside (e.g. a BM image) would allow lines 37-46 to be added.
