# Hyperborea: the ancient testimonia

Key `hyperborea`, prefix `verified-hyperborea-`, shelf Greece. A collection has one shelf, so the two Latin
entries (Pliny, Mela) sit on the Greece shelf with tradition `roman`. Importer: `scripts/import_hyperborea.py`,
which also holds the shared builder and text helpers used by `scripts/import_atlantis.py`. New author rows
(`pomponius-mela`, `callimachus`) are in the collection file's `authors` list; the other author ids already
exist in `data/authors.json` (`herodotus`, `strabo`, `pliny-elder`, `pindar`, `diodorus`, `pausanias`, `homer`,
`apollonius-rhodes`).

## Method

- **Manuscripts.** For each passage, the oldest manuscript of the work whose page images could be downloaded
  (IIIF). Every section's position was read on the page images: the first and last words with their line
  numbers, and every page break inside a section, are quoted in the section's `scan_note`. Locating the
  passages in the manuscripts was split among helper agents working from the images; each placement was then
  spot-checked on the images (crops viewed) before use. No position was estimated.
- **Original texts.** Perseus canonical-greekLit / canonical-latinLit (CC BY-SA 4.0) for the editions named in
  each entry; Mela from C. Frick's Teubner (1880), transcribed from the printed page images (archive.org scan)
  and checked line by line. These are critical editions, not transcriptions of the manuscripts shown; where the
  manuscript's reading matters, the scan_note says so.
- **English.** Public-domain translations only: Macaulay 1890 (Herodotus; Project Gutenberg 2707, footnote
  numbers removed; the Perseus "Godley" English is a modernized revision and was not used), Hamilton &
  Falconer 1854-57 (Strabo, Perseus), Bostock & Riley 1855 (Pliny, Perseus; divided at Mayhoff's sections by its
  opening words), Arthur Golding 1585 (Mela, EEBO-TCP, CC0), plus the poetry and Diodorus/Pausanias sources
  listed in the table.
- **Copyright check for the 1935 Loeb volumes** (Oldfather's Diodorus vol. II; Jones's Pausanias vol. IV): the
  US renewal lists for 1961-1964 (Project Gutenberg transcriptions 11823-11830, saved in
  `data/sources/hyperborea/renewals/`) contain no Diodorus, Oldfather or Pausanias entry, so neither volume was
  renewed in its 28th year and both are in the US public domain (LacusCurtius states the same for Oldfather).

## Gaps and notes

- **Pindar, Pythian 10**: only lines 25-44 (the whole Hyperborean description) are included. In Vat. gr. 1312 (B), the
  oldest manuscript of the Pythians, the other pages of the ode are too dark in the published images to place line
  by line (164r and 166v-167r could not be read even with contrast enhancement); a later round could map the whole
  ode in Laur. Plut. 32.52 (D), which is legible.
- **Pindar, Olympian 3** is shown on Laur. Plut. 32.52 (D, 14th c.), not on the older Ambrosianus C 222 inf. (A,
  c. 1180) or Vat. gr. 1312 (B): A's images on the Ambrosiana/Unicatt viewer carry the restriction "No
  Downloadable / Printable: No", and B is too dark for a full line-by-line mapping.
- **Callimachus** is shown on Paris gr. 2763 (1470-1500, BnF microfilm); Ambros. B 98 sup. has the same
  no-download restriction.
- **English for Pindar** is D. A. Svarlien's line-grouped translation (Perseus, CC BY-SA 4.0), chosen over Sandys
  (1915) because its line numbers let each part be tied to the manuscript page; Svarlien is openly licensed (spec
  rule 6). Perseus prints "«" for an apostrophe in a few words; restored.
- **Mair's Callimachus** comes from the theoi.com transcription, corrected in five places against the 1921 print
  (archive.org scan; list in the importer, `MAIR_FIXES`).
- **Pausanias 5.7.7-9** (the wild olive brought from the Hyperboreans to Olympia) was not placed on the manuscript in
  time and is not included.
- **Pausanias 1.31.2**: the count of lines 1-20 on fol. 26v is by line detection; lines 21-30, which carry the
  passage, were checked by eye. The folio number 26v is taken from the neighbouring opening (27r/28r).
- **Not included**: Pliny NH 6.34-35 and 6.55 (Hyperboreans placed in Asia; Leiden VLF 4 ends in book 5);
  Bacchylides 3.58-62 (papyrus only); Hesiod and Alcaeus fragments (no manuscript text of their own); Plutarch,
  Camillus 22.2-3 (Heraclides Ponticus' "army from the Hyperboreans" that took Rome; not placed: the Plutarch
  witnesses checked were too costly to search in this round); Aelian, On Animals 11.1; Lucian, Philopseudes 13.
- **Image licences**: BnF NoC-NC; Leiden Public Domain Mark; Vatican Library non-commercial with credit;
  Biblioteca Medicea Laurenziana via Teca digitale (access "Pubblico"), reproduced for non-commercial study with
  credit. The integrator may wish to confirm the Laurenziana's current reuse wording.
- Greek texts are critical editions, not transcriptions of the manuscripts; manuscript readings that differ in
  telling ways are noted in the scan_notes (e.g. Herodotus' "Atlantes" for "Atarantes" in the atlantis entry,
  Pliny's "ex tot", Mela's "olim" corrected to "ulli").
