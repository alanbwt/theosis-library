## Sources

- Manuscript: Florence, Biblioteca Medicea Laurenziana, Plut. 32.16 (paper, late 13th century; a large collection of Greek poetry that also holds letters of Maximus Planudes), Theogony on fols. 240r-247v. BML Teca Digitale IIIF, manifest `https://cdm21059.contentdm.oclc.org/iiif/plutei:596036/manifest.json`; rights CC BY-NC-SA per the record. This is the oldest complete Theogony manuscript with published images; Paris suppl. gr. 663 (11th c.) is older but has only lines 72-145 and 450-504 (and is on Gallica, which refused automated access during this work).
- Greek: H. G. Evelyn-White, Loeb 1914, Perseus `tlg0020.tlg001.perseus-grc2` (CC BY-SA 4.0). Editorial brackets (`<del>`) shown as `[...]`.
- English: H. G. Evelyn-White 1914 (public domain), Perseus `tlg0020.tlg001.perseus-eng2`.

## Mapping method

Each page's first and last verse were read on the photograph (zoomed crops of the top and bottom rows) and matched to the Greek; every page boundary was checked from both sides (page N ends at line k, page N+1 begins at k+1). The scribe writes two verses to a row (left column, then right column), so a page is read across, row by row; fol. 244v is written three verses to a row. Page ranges: 240r 1-42, 240v 43-100, 241r 101-160, 241v 161-222, 242r 223-278, 242v 279-340, 243r 341-409, 243v 410-475, 244r 476-547, 244v 548-668, 245r 669-736, 245v 737-803, 246r 804-867, 246v 868-939, 247r 940-991, 247v 992-1020.

English: Evelyn-White's prose comes in blocks of about five lines; each block is placed in the section holding its middle line, so at a page break the English can run one or two lines past the Greek. Blocks were split at Perseus paragraph (card) starts so that entry boundaries are clean.

## Differences from the edition, gaps

- 929a-929t (Galen's quotation on the birth of Athena) are not in the medieval manuscripts and are left out.
- 1014 (Telegonus) is not on fol. 247v; the manuscript passes from 1013 to 1015.
- 1021-1022 (the transition to the Catalogue of Women) are not in this manuscript: the poem ends at 1020 with the red subscription τέλος τῆς θεογονίας ἡσιόδου. They are printed from the edition with a note.
- fol. 245r: in the falling-anvil passage (722-725) the scribe skipped between the two verses ending χάλκεος ἄκμων and wrote the skipped verses in the lower margin with a reference sign. Which exact verses stand in the margin was not transcribed.
- fol. 243r: the right column starts half a row higher than the left, so the columns are out of step on that page (69 verses).
