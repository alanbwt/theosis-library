# Plato, Critias (Paris gr. 1807)

Key `plato-critias`, prefix `verified-plato-critias-`, shelf Greece. Importer: `scripts/import_plato_critias.py`
(uses the builder in `scripts/import_plato_timaeus.py`).

## Contents

The complete *Critias* as Plato left it, Stephanus 106a-121c: 16 entries (one per Stephanus page,
`verified-plato-critias-106` to `-121`), 76 sections (one per Stephanus section). All entries carry the theme
`atlantis`. The dialogue is unfinished; it stops in mid-sentence at 121c ("kai synageiras eipen").

## Mapping method

As for the Timaeus: the opening words of all 26 columns (145r col. a to 151r col. b) were read on the
full-resolution images and located in Burnet's text; each Stephanus section is linked to the folio side(s) whose
columns carry it, and each `scan_note` quotes the column openings it relies on.

Checked on the images: the Critias begins at the top of 145r col. a, under the title "platonos kritias e
atlantikos" with the number 41 (mu-alpha) of the collection; it ends in 151r col. b with "kai synageiras eipen",
after which the rest of the column is blank (there is no end title). 147r col. a holds about 1,200 letters of
Burnet's text against 900-1,000 for other columns; its foot was read as well ("echousa; geodes d' en pasa kai")
and 147r col. b continues "plen oligon epipedos anothen", so the mapping holds.

## Gaps and notes

- No gaps: all 76 sections have Greek, English and images.
- Burnet's Greek is a critical text and differs in details from the manuscript.
- Bury's footnotes are omitted; quotation marks and italics follow the Perseus markup.
- Speaker labels (ΚΡΙ./Crit., ΕΡ./Herm., ΣΩ./Soc., ΤΙ./Tim.) are shown only where the speaker changes, the same way in
  Greek and English; Perseus' repeated labels at its card breaks inside Critias' narrative are not shown (QC fix
  2026-11-12).
- 110a/110b: Bury turns the sentence round (Solon and the priests first, the names last), so Perseus' 110b milestone
  fell after most of it. His sentence "And for evidence of what I say ... the names of the women." is given whole with
  110b, where most of the Greek sentence stands; both scan_notes say so.
