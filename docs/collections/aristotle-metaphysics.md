## Mapping method

First and last lines of each page read on the images and matched to Ross's Greek (quoted in every scan_note). Sections are whole chapters because the English has no Bekker markers; chapter 8 spans four pages (294r-295v), the others one to three.

## Notes and uncertainties

- Ross's translation starts chapter 8 with "It is clear, then, why these things are as they are", which the Greek edition ends chapter 7 with (1073a13). Noted in translator_notes.
- The MIT text was identified as the 1928 revision (it differs from the 1908 first edition, e.g. "If, then, one form of non-being exists potentially"); it was not collated line by line against a scan of the 1928 printing.
- Vienna, ONB, phil. gr. 100 (J, 9th c.) is older but was not used.
