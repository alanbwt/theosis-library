# Calcutta-Bairat (Bhabru) rock inscription, Hultzsch 1925 (CII I), pp. 172-174. Proofread from archive.org InscriptionsOfAsoka.NewEditionByE.Hultzsch.

1 (A) Pr[i]yadas[i] l[ā]jā Māgadhe saṁghaṁ abhivāde[tū]naṁ āhā ap[ā]bādhataṁ cha phāsu-vihālataṁ chā
2 (B) vidite v[e] bhaṁte āvatake h[a]mā Budhasi dhaṁmasi saṁghasī ti gālave chaṁ prasāde cha (C) e kechi bhaṁte
3 bhagavatā Budhe[na] bhāsite sarve se subhāsite vā (D) e chu kho bhaṁte hamiyāye diseyā hevaṁ sadhaṁme
4 chil[a-ṭhi]tīke hosatī ti alahāmi hakaṁ ta[ṁ] v[ā]tave (E) imāni bhaṁt[e dha]ṁma-paliyāyāni Vinaya-samukase
5 Aliya-vasāṇi Anāgata-bhayāni Muni-gāthā Moneya-sūte Upatisa-pasine e chā Lāghulo-
6 vāde musā-vādaṁ adhigichya bhagavatā Budhena bhāsite etāni bhaṁte dhaṁma-paliyāyāni ichhāmi
7 kiṁti bahuke bhikhu-[p]āye chā bhikhuniye ch[ā] abhikhinaṁ sun[e]yu chā upadhāl[a]yeyū chā
8 (F) hevaṁmevā upāsakā chā upāsikā chā (G) eteni bhaṁte imaṁ likhā[pa]yāmi abhipretaṁ me jānaṁtū ti
@en
(A) The Māgadha king Priyadarśin, having saluted the <em>Saṁgha</em>, hopes they are both well and comfortable.
(B) It is known to you, Sirs, how great is my reverence and faith in the Buddha, the <em>Dharma</em>, (and) the <em>Saṁgha</em>.
(C) Whatsoever, Sirs, has been spoken by the blessed Buddha, all that is quite well spoken.
(D) But, Sirs, what would indeed appear to me (to be referred to by the words of the scripture): ‘thus the true <em>Dharma</em> will be of long duration’, that I feel bound to declare.
(E) The following expositions of the <em>Dharma</em>, Sirs, (viz.) (1) the <em>Vinaya-samukasa</em>, (2) the <em>Aliya-vasas</em>, (3) the <em>Anāgata-bhayas</em>, (4) the <em>Muni-gāthās</em>, (5) the <em>Moneya-sūta</em>, (6) the <em>Upatisa-pasina</em>, and (7) the <em>Lāghulovāda</em> which was spoken by the blessed Buddha concerning falsehood,—I desire, Sirs, that many groups of monks and (many) nuns may repeatedly listen to these expositions of the <em>Dharma</em>, and may reflect (on them).
(F) In the same way both laymen and laywomen (should act).
(G) For the following (purpose), Sirs, am I causing this to be written, (viz.) in order that they may know my intention.
