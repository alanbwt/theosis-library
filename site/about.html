About

The Project

Theosis Library is a verified primary source database for ancient texts. The premise is simple: in an age when AI can generate plausible-sounding ancient writing in seconds, the only durable form of authority is the photograph. You can fake a translation. You cannot fake the parchment.

Every section in this library meets three non-negotiable criteria:

  1. A photograph of the original document. The tablet, stele, papyrus, scroll, wall or manuscript page that carries exactly that section's text, from the holding institution or a published photographic facsimile. Each section says where on the image its text is.
  2. The verbatim original-language text. From a named scholarly transcription or edition (for example the Electronic Babylonian Library, the Thesaurus Linguae Aegyptiae, the Codex Sinaiticus Project, Perseus). No AI generation. No paraphrase. Gaps in the document stay visible as gaps.
  3. A verbatim English translation from a named published source. Public domain or openly licensed (the King James Version for the Bible; scholarly translations such as Budge, Griffith, Legge, Evelyn-White, Lamb or the eBL editors elsewhere). The translator is named on every page.

Nothing else gets in. If we cannot deliver all three for a chapter, that chapter is not in the library.

Why This Matters

AI can generate plausible translations of any ancient text in seconds. What AI cannot generate is a photograph of a manuscript. The scans in this library are the evidence. The original-language texts are verifiable against those scans. The translations are checkable against the originals. Every claim links to a source you can see with your own eyes.

As AI becomes more capable, the need for a verification layer becomes greater, not less. This is that layer.
