Reading Paths

Start with a question

Each path is a short guided sequence through the library that answers one question. Every step opens a real section of an ancient document: the photograph, the original language and the English, so you can check each claim as you read.

11 steps · Uruk to Herodotus

The earliest civilizations on record

From the first written accounts of Uruk to the king lists of Sumer, Babylon and Egypt and the Greek travellers who heard the priests' chronologies: how the oldest civilizations remembered their own beginnings.
9 steps · Plato and his readers

Atlantis: every ancient source

The story of Atlantis as Plato tells it in the Timaeus and Critias, read on the 9th-century Paris manuscript, followed by every ancient writer who answered him.
9 steps · Greek and Latin

Hyperborea: the land beyond the north wind

Every major ancient account of the Hyperboreans, the blessed people of Apollo who lived beyond the north wind, each on its manuscript page.
9 steps · Sumer to Genesis

The Flood before history

The great flood as the oldest written traditions tell it, from the Sumerian king lists to Gilgamesh, Genesis, Enoch and Plato, each on the tablet or manuscript that carries it.
9 steps · Plato to the Gospels

Becoming like God

The ancient sources behind theosis: Plato's "likeness to god", Aristotle's divine life of the mind, the Psalm Jesus quoted, and the Christian promise of the divine nature.
6 steps · Gospels

What did Jesus say about divinity?

The words attributed to Jesus on the divine nature, followed across Mark, Matthew and John in Codex Sinaiticus, the oldest complete New Testament.
