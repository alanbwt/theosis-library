Reading Path

Becoming like God

The ancient sources behind theosis: Plato's "likeness to god", Aristotle's divine life of the mind, the Psalm Jesus quoted, and the Christian promise of the divine nature.

  1. 1
    c. 369 BCE · Greek · Plato, Theaetetus

    Theaetetus 176b: flight is likeness to god

    We must fly from here to there as quickly as we can, and to fly is to become like god as far as possible.

  2. 2
    c. 380 BCE · Greek · Plato, Symposium

    Symposium 212a: beauty itself

    Diotima's ladder ends in the sight of divine beauty; whoever reaches it wins the friendship of heaven and, above all men, is immortal.

  3. 3
    c. 370 BCE · Greek · Plato, Phaedrus

    Phaedrus 246e-247a: the procession of the gods

    The souls follow Zeus and the gods to the place beyond heaven.

  4. 4
    c. 330 BCE · Greek · Aristotle

    Aristotle, Ethics 10.7: make ourselves immortal

    The life of the mind is something divine in us; we should not think mortal thoughts but live by the best thing in us.

  5. 5
    1008 AD manuscript · Hebrew · Psalm 82

    Psalm 82:6: "I have said, Ye are gods"

    The verse at the root of the theosis debate.

  6. 6
    4th c. manuscript · Greek · John 10

    John 10:34: Jesus quotes the Psalm

    "Is it not written in your law, I said, Ye are gods?"

  7. 7
    4th c. manuscript · Greek · 2 Peter 1

    2 Peter 1:4: partakers of the divine nature

    The classic scriptural text for theosis, on the page of Codex Sinaiticus.

  8. 8
    4th c. manuscript · Coptic · Gospel of Thomas

    Thomas 108: "Whoever drinks from my mouth will become like me"

    The saying from Nag Hammadi Codex II, line by line on the papyrus.
