In the beginning: creation accounts

How the oldest texts of Babylon, India, Israel, Greece, China and Phoenicia describe the beginning of the world, ending with the prologue of John.

  1. 1
    c. 1100 BCE (composition); Nineveh tablets · Akkadian · Enuma Elish

    Enuma Elish: "When on high no word was used for heaven"

    Before heaven and earth were named, the fresh waters of Apsu and the salt waters of Tiamat mingled, and the gods were born within them.

  2. 2
    c. 1000 BCE · Vedic Sanskrit · Rigveda 10.129

    Rigveda 10.129: "Then was not non-existent nor existent"

    The Nasadiya hymn: darkness hidden in darkness, and That One breathing by its own power.

  3. 3
    c. 1000 BCE · Vedic Sanskrit · Rigveda 10.129

    Rigveda 10.129.7: "he verily knows it, or perhaps he knows not"

    The hymn ends by doubting whether even the highest overseer knows how creation arose.

  4. 4
    1008 AD manuscript · Hebrew · Genesis 1

    Genesis 1: "In the beginning God created"

    The earth without form and void, darkness on the face of the deep, and the spirit of God moving upon the waters.

  5. 5
    c. 700 BCE · Greek · Hesiod, Theogony

    Theogony 116: "First of all Chaos came to be"

    Then wide-bosomed Earth, Tartarus and Eros; from Chaos came Night and Erebus.

  6. 6
    before 195 BCE manuscript · Classical Chinese · Daodejing 25

    Daodejing 25: "something undefined and complete"

    It came into existence before Heaven and Earth, still and formless; it may be regarded as the mother of all things, and Laozi gives it the designation Tao.

  7. 7
    c. 360 BCE · Greek · Plato, Timaeus

    Timaeus 29e: "He was good"

    The maker of the cosmos was good and without envy, and wished all things to be as like himself as possible.

  8. 8
    c. 290 BCE · Greek · Berossus

    Berossus: darkness and an abyss of waters

    The Babylonian creation as a Greek reader heard it: monstrous beings in the deep, until Belus cuts the woman Thalatth in two to make heaven and earth.

  9. 9
    c. 100 AD (citing an older Phoenician source) · Greek · Philo of Byblos

    Sanchuniathon: dark air and chaos

    The Phoenician cosmogony: a turbid chaos and a wind that loved its own first principles.

  10. 10
    c. 90 AD; 4th c. manuscript · Greek · John 1

    John 1:1: "In the beginning was the Word"

    The Christian answer, on the page of Codex Sinaiticus: the Logos was with God and was God, and all things were made by him.
