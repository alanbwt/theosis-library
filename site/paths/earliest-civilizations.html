Reading Path

The earliest civilizations on record

From the first written accounts of Uruk to the king lists of Sumer, Babylon and Egypt and the Greek travellers who heard the priests' chronologies: how the oldest civilizations remembered their own beginnings.

  1. 1
    c. 3100 BCE · proto-cuneiform · Uruk

    The Kushim tablet: one of the first written records

    A barley account signed by an official named Kushim, possibly the earliest personal name in writing.

  2. 2
    c. 1800 BCE · Sumerian · Sumerian King List

    "After kingship descended from heaven"

    Eridu is the first city; eight kings rule for 241,200 years before the flood.

  3. 3
    c. 2600 BCE (oldest copies) · Sumerian · Instructions of Shuruppak

    Shuruppak instructs his son

    The oldest wisdom literature, set in the days before the flood: the wise man of Shuruppak advises Ziusudra.

  4. 4
    c. 290 BCE · Greek · Berossus

    Berossus: Oannes from the sea

    A being half fish and half man comes out of the Red Sea and teaches the Babylonians letters, sciences, cities, temples, laws and agriculture.

  5. 5
    c. 290 BCE · Greek · Berossus

    Berossus: the ten kings before the flood

    From Alorus to Xisuthrus, ten kings reign for 120 sars before the deluge.

  6. 6
    c. 280 BCE · Greek · Manetho

    Manetho: the reigns of the gods

    Egypt's priest-historian lists dynasties of gods and demigods before the first human king, as reported (and mocked) by George Syncellus.

  7. 7
    c. 50 BCE · Greek · Diodorus

    Diodorus: Osiris ends cannibalism

    Isis discovers wheat and barley and Osiris teaches their cultivation, the Egyptian account of how civilization began.

  8. 8
    c. 100 AD (citing an older Phoenician source) · Greek · Philo of Byblos

    Sanchuniathon: the first inventors

    Light, Fire and Flame discover fire by rubbing wood together; Hypsuranius builds the first huts of reeds at Tyre, and Taautus invents writing.

  9. 9
    c. 700 BCE · Greek · Hesiod, Works and Days

    Hesiod: the golden race

    The first of the five races of mankind lived like gods under Cronos, without toil or old age.

  10. 10
    c. 460 BCE · Greek · Aeschylus, Prometheus Bound

    Prometheus: numbers and letters

    Prometheus lists what he gave mankind: astronomy, number, the combining of letters, the yoke, ships and medicine (Smyth).

  11. 11
    8 AD (11th c. manuscript) · Latin · Ovid, Metamorphoses 1

    Ovid: "First was the Golden Age"

    No laws, no judges, no ships or walls: the Roman picture of the first age before the decline to silver, bronze and iron.

  12. 12
    c. 360 BCE · Greek · Plato, Timaeus

    Timaeus 23e: 8,000 years of records at Sais

    The Egyptian priests' sacred writings go back eight thousand years, and Athens a thousand years further.

  13. 13
    c. 430 BCE · Greek · Herodotus

    Herodotus 2.142: 341 generations

    The priests count 341 generations of kings, 11,340 years, in which the sun four times rose out of its usual place.

  14. 14
    c. 350 BCE · Greek · Plato, Laws

    Laws 3: floods and the survivors

    The world of men has often been destroyed by floods; a few herdsmen on the mountains survive and civilization begins again.
