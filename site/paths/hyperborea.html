Reading Path

Hyperborea: the land beyond the north wind

Every major ancient account of the Hyperboreans, the blessed people of Apollo who lived beyond the north wind, each on its manuscript page.

  1. 1
    498 BCE · Greek · Pindar

    Pindar, Pythian 10: no road by ship or on foot

    Neither by ship nor on foot can anyone find the road to the Hyperboreans.

  2. 2
    498 BCE · Greek · Pindar

    Pindar, Pythian 10: no sickness, no old age

    They wreathe their hair with golden laurel and feast; no sickness or ruinous old age touches that sacred race.

  3. 3
    476 BCE · Greek · Pindar

    Pindar, Olympian 3: the olive from the north

    Heracles brings the wild olive for the Olympic crown from the land of the Hyperboreans.

  4. 4
    c. 430 BCE · Greek · Herodotus

    Herodotus 4.32-36: what is told of the Hyperboreans

    Herodotus weighs the reports, from Hesiod and Homer to the offerings sent from the north to Delos.

  5. 5
    c. 430 BCE · Greek · Herodotus

    Herodotus 4.13: Aristeas and the one-eyed Arimaspians

    The poet Aristeas travels north, possessed by Apollo, and hears of the peoples beyond the griffins.

  6. 6
    c. 50 BCE (citing Hecataeus, c. 300 BCE) · Greek · Diodorus

    Diodorus 2.47: the island and Apollo's round temple

    An island in the ocean beyond the land of the Celts, birthplace of Leto, with a sacred precinct and a temple of Apollo.

  7. 7
    c. 170 AD · Greek · Pausanias

    Pausanias 10.5.8: Delphi founded by Hyperboreans

    The poetess Boeo says the oracle of Delphi was built by men from the Hyperboreans, and that Olen was Apollo's first prophet.

  8. 8
    77 AD · Latin · Pliny the Elder

    Pliny 4.89: a happy race at the hinge of the world

    Beyond the Rhipaean mountains, under the pole, with six months of day: a people who live to extreme old age.

  9. 9
    c. 43 AD · Latin · Pomponius Mela

    Mela 3.36: beyond the Rhipaean mountains

    The Roman geographer places the Hyperboreans first on the coast of Asia, beyond the Rhipaean mountains, under the north pole (English of Arthur Golding, 1585).
