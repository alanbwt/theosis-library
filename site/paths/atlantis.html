Reading Path

Atlantis: every ancient source

The story of Atlantis as Plato tells it in the Timaeus and Critias, read on the 9th-century Paris manuscript, followed by every ancient writer who answered him.

  1. 1
    c. 360 BCE · Greek · Plato, Timaeus

    Timaeus 22b: "You Greeks are always children"

    Solon visits the priests of Sais in Egypt, and an old priest tells him the Greeks have no ancient records.

  2. 2
    c. 360 BCE · Greek · Plato, Timaeus

    Timaeus 22c: destructions by fire and water

    Mankind has been destroyed many times, the priest says, and each time the survivors lose their past.

  3. 3
    c. 360 BCE · Greek · Plato, Timaeus

    Timaeus 24e: a power from the Atlantic ocean

    Nine thousand years before Solon, a great host set out from an island beyond the Pillars of Heracles to conquer Europe and Asia.

  4. 4
    c. 360 BCE · Greek · Plato, Timaeus

    Timaeus 25d: one grievous day and night

    Earthquakes and floods: the island of Atlantis is swallowed by the sea and vanishes.

  5. 5
    c. 360 BCE · Greek · Plato, Critias

    Critias 113c: Poseidon's island

    Poseidon receives Atlantis as his lot and settles there the children he had with a mortal woman.

  6. 6
    c. 360 BCE · Greek · Plato, Critias

    Critias 120e-121c: the divine portion fades

    While the god's nature stayed strong in them they lived nobly; as it was diluted, they grew greedy, and Zeus called the gods together. The dialogue breaks off there.

  7. 7
    c. 20 AD · Greek · Strabo

    Strabo 2.3.6: Posidonius on Atlantis

    The philosopher Posidonius thought the Atlantis story might not be invention, citing real changes of land and sea.

  8. 8
    c. 50 BCE · Greek · Diodorus

    Diodorus 3.56: the Atlantians

    A people on the shore of the ocean, whose myths of the first gods Diodorus records.

  9. 9
    c. 200 AD (citing Theopompus, 4th c. BCE) · Greek · Aelian

    Aelian 3.18: the continent beyond the ocean

    Theopompus' tale of a vast land outside the known world, often read as an answer to Plato.
