Sources

Every section in the Theosis Library is built from three independently verifiable sources: a photograph of the original document, a verbatim original-language text, and a verbatim English translation from a named published source. This page lists exactly what those sources are.

Manuscripts

Codex Sinaiticus (4th c., ~350 AD)

The oldest substantially complete copy of the Christian Bible. Originally one codex containing both the Septuagint Old Testament (in Greek) and the New Testament. Discovered by Constantin von Tischendorf at Saint Catherine's Monastery, Sinai, in the 19th century. Now distributed across the British Library, the Russian National Library, the Leipzig University Library, and Saint Catherine's. Digitized at codexsinaiticus.org with both folio images and verbatim transcription, licensed CC BY-NC-SA 3.0.

This library contains the folio scans for all 260 surviving NT chapters and the 240 surviving LXX/OT chapters of Codex Sinaiticus.

Codex Vaticanus (4th c., ~325-350 AD)

Vaticanus Graecus 1209 at the Vatican Apostolic Library. With Sinaiticus, one of the two earliest near-complete Greek Bibles. Digitized at digi.vatlib.it. Verse-level folio mapping is provided by the New Testament Virtual Manuscript Room (INTF Münster, CC BY 4.0), docID 03.

This library contains 220 New Testament chapters from Codex Vaticanus.

Codex Bezae Cantabrigiensis (5th c., ~400 AD)

Cambridge University Library MS Nn.2.41. A bilingual Greek/Latin codex containing the Gospels and Acts, the most important witness to the Western text tradition. Digitized via the Cambridge Digital Library IIIF. Verse-level page mapping via NTVMR docID 05.

This library contains 45 Gospel and Acts chapters from Codex Bezae (Greek side).

Venetus A (10th c.)

Biblioteca Marciana Marc. gr. Z. 454 (olim 822), the most important complete medieval manuscript of Homer's Iliad, famous for its extensive marginal scholia. Digitized with line-level folio mapping by the Homer Multitext Project (CC BY).

This library contains all 24 books of the Iliad, one section per page of Venetus A.

Codex Regius (GKS 2365 4to, c. 1270 AD)

The unique Icelandic vellum manuscript that preserves the Poetic Edda. Housed at the Árni Magnússon Institute for Icelandic Studies in Reykjavík. Digitized at handrit.is. Every stanza is linked to the page that carries it, following the 1891 diplomatic edition and checked on the photographs.

This library contains 11 mythological poems of the Poetic Edda from Codex Regius.

Codex Alexandrinus (5th c.)

British Library Royal MS 1 D. VIII. With Sinaiticus and Vaticanus, one of the three great uncial Bibles. Page images are the 1909 British Museum reduced photographic facsimile of the New Testament volume (ed. F. G. Kenyon), public domain, scanned by the Getty Research Institute on the Internet Archive. Verse-level page mapping from the NTVMR (docID 20002, CC BY 4.0), confirmed against the verse caption Kenyon printed under every plate. This library contains 228 New Testament chapters; the codex lacks Matthew 1:1-25:5, John 6:50-8:52 and 2 Corinthians 4:13-12:6.

Codex Washingtonianus (4th-5th c.)

Freer Gallery of Art, Smithsonian Institution, F1906.274 (the Freer Gospels, GA 032). Images from the Smithsonian's own IIIF service (asia.si.edu; non-commercial use with credit). Verse-to-page lookup via CSNTM. This library contains 88 Gospel chapters; the codex lacks Mark 15:13-38 and John 14:26-16:7.

Leningrad Codex, Firkovich B19a (1008 AD)

National Library of Russia, St Petersburg. The oldest complete manuscript of the Hebrew Bible and the base text of every modern critical edition. Page images are the 1990 West Semitic Research Project colour photographs as redistributed by the Internet Archive (Public Domain Mark) and Wikimedia Commons; the photographer asserts copyright in the frames, and that residual risk is recorded on every entry. Folio-by-folio verse index from tanach.us, cross-checked against Sefaria for all 929 chapters. This library contains all 929 chapters.

Great Isaiah Scroll, 1QIsa-a (c. 125 BCE)

Israel Museum, Shrine of the Book. The complete Isaiah scroll from Qumran Cave 1. Column images are cropped from the public-domain composite photograph on Wikimedia Commons (Google Art Project); each of the 54 columns was checked against the scroll's own transcription. This library contains all 66 chapters.

Codex Parisino-petropolitanus, BnF Arabe 328 (7th-8th c.)

Bibliothèque nationale de France. One of the oldest surviving Quran manuscripts, in Hijazi script. Images via Gallica IIIF (non-commercial reuse with credit "Source gallica.bnf.fr / BnF"); folio-by-folio sura and verse ranges from Corpus Coranicum (BBAW, CC BY 3.0), each image cross-checked against Corpus Coranicum's image of the same folio. This library contains 33 suras on the 70 Paris leaves.

Epic of Gilgamesh, Nineveh tablets (7th c. BCE)

British Museum, library of Ashurbanipal, including the Flood Tablet K.3375. Photographs are the British Museum's, served by the Electronic Babylonian Library (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0). This library contains 19 tablets of Tablets I, VI and XI.

What's Not Here (Yet)

A text enters the library only when every section has a photograph of the original document that carries it, the verbatim original-language text, and a public-domain or openly licensed English translation of the same passage. These important texts do not yet meet that bar, and why:

Egypt

  • Book of the Dead: the library has spells 1-165 on the Turin papyrus of Iuefankh; the Papyrus of Ani itself has no open digital Egyptian text (TLA holds only BD 58, 59, 185, 186 from it). Spells 20, 111, 121 lack a usable public-domain English; Spell 175 is not on the Turin roll.
  • Shabaka Stone (Memphite Theology), Palermo Stone, Turin King List: no open digital text.
  • Rosetta Stone, hieroglyphic and Demotic versions: no open text (the Greek version is in).
  • Story of Sinuhe, Shipwrecked Sailor: no open photographs of the papyri.
  • Great Hymn to the Aten: no open photograph of the whole inscription.
  • Pyramid Text 271 (Unas): not in the open TLA data.

Mesopotamia and Persia

  • Code of Ur-Nammu, Code of Lipit-Ishtar: no open English translation.
  • Enmerkar and the Lord of Aratta; Gilgamesh, Enkidu and the Netherworld; Nergal and Ereshkigal: too few lines on openly photographed tablets.
  • Atrahasis (most of the Old Babylonian tablets), parts of Gilgamesh, Anzu, Erra: the lines survive only on tablets in Baghdad, Berlin, Istanbul, Ankara, Chicago or Penn without open photographs, or on unphotographed British Museum tablets.
  • Behistun inscription: no photograph shows an Old Persian column legibly (the text and an open translation are ready).
  • Sennacherib's prism: the face holding each column could not be identified on the open photographs.
  • Merneptah Stele: no open line-numbered transliteration reachable.
  • Black Obelisk main inscription (190 lines), Cyrus Cylinder lines 1-5 and 37-46: no source says which lines are on which face, or the lines are not in any open photograph.
  • Avesta: the Gathas and Yasht 19 (no openly usable manuscript images where the chapters could be located).

India and China

  • Ashoka: the Shahbazgarhi, Mansehra, Jaugada and remaining Dhauli rock edicts, the other pillars and the minor rock edicts (all in Hultzsch 1925, not yet transcribed); the Kandahar Greek-Aramaic bilingual (no open English).
  • The Buddha's first sermon (Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta): no openly viewable manuscript found (the British Library EAP and Toronto Myanmar collections refuse automated access).
  • Kena, Mundaka, Taittiriya, Shvetashvatara and Brihadaranyaka Upanishads: no citable Sanskrit e-text (Kena, Mundaka) or no clean public-domain English text (the others).
  • Book of Odes (the Airs of the States), Classic of Changes, Mencius, Zhuangzi, Art of War: not yet built (clean public-domain English and suitable witnesses still being assembled).
  • Shang oracle bones: Britton's 'Fifty Shang Inscriptions' (1940) appears unrenewed but is not online.
  • Dunhuang manuscripts: the Paris (Gallica) and IDP image services refuse automated access.

Greece and Rome

  • Plutarch, Solon 26 and 31-32 (Solon's Atlantis poem); Pausanias 5.7.7-9: manuscripts not yet located on an accessible image service.
  • Orphic gold tablets other than Petelia: no open photographs, or no public-domain edition (tablets found after 1930).
  • Prometheus Bound beyond lines 436-525; Odyssey 24.104-548 (the 10th-century manuscript ends at 24.103); Pindar, Pythian 10 outside lines 25-44 (pages too dark).

Dead Sea Scrolls and Second Temple

  • War Scroll, Thanksgiving Hymns, Genesis Apocryphon, Temple Scroll, Copper Scroll: no public-domain or open English translation.
  • Apocalypse of Peter and Greek 1 Enoch 1-32 from the Akhmim codex: Greek text still to be proofread from the 1893 edition.

New Testament papyri

  • P45 and P47 (Chester Beatty): page labels conflict with the transcription assignments, or the transcription is not openly licensed.
  • P75: 6 of 102 pages; P46: 18 of 172 pages (too damaged to identify, or no image).
