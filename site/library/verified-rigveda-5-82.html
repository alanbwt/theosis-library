Introduction

        
Rigveda 5.82 is a hymn of 9 verses addressed to Savitri. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 5, the family book of the Atri clan. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 396r-397r. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

Vedic Sanskrit · Aufrecht (accented)

                    
1tat sa̍vi̱tur vṛ̍ṇīmahe va̱yaṁ de̱vasya̱ bhoja̍nam | śreṣṭha̍ṁ sarva̱dhāta̍ma̱ṁ tura̱m bhaga̍sya dhīmahi ||
2asya̱ hi svaya̍śastaraṁ savi̱tuḥ kac ca̱na pri̱yam | na mi̱nanti̍ sva̱rājya̍m ||

            
            

              Compare: Padapāṭha (word-by-word text written in the manuscript; Sansknet e-text via GRETIL)
              
        
1tat | savituḥ | vṛṇīmahe | vayam | devasya | bhojanam | śreṣṭham | sarva-dhātamam | turam | bhagasya | dhīmahi
2asya | hi | svayaśaḥ-taram | savituḥ | kat | cana | priyam | na | minanti | sva-rājyam

            

            
          


          

            
English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1WE crave of Savitar the God this treasure much to be enjoyed. The best, all-yielding, conquering gift of Bhaga we would gladly win.
2Savitar's own supremacy, most glorious and beloved of all, No one diminisheth in aught.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
3For Savitar who is Bhaga shall send riches to his worshipper. That wondrous portion we implore.
4Send us this day, God Savitar, prosperity with progeny. Drive thou the evil dream away.
5Savitar, God, send far away all sorrows and calamities, And send us only what is good.
6Sinless in sight of Aditi through the God Savitar's influence, May we obtain all lovely things.
7We with our hymns this day elect the general God, Lord of the good, Savitar whose decrees are true.
8He who for ever vigilant precedes these Twain, the Day and Night, Is Savitar the thoughtful God.
