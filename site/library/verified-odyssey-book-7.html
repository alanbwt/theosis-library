Introduction

        
The Odyssey, composed around 700 BCE and attributed to Homer, tells of Odysseus' ten-year journey home from Troy and his return to Ithaca. Book 7 (347 lines): The palace of Alcinous.
The page photographs are from Florence, Biblioteca Medicea Laurenziana, Plut. 32.24 (parchment, 10th century, 234 folios; the oldest manuscript of the Odyssey with published images), in the Biblioteca Medicea Laurenziana's Teca Digitale. This book is on fol. 62v-69v; each section below is one page, with its first and last line as read on the photograph. Many pages carry interlinear glosses and a few marginal notes in later hands.
The Greek is A. T. Murray's Loeb text (1919) via Perseus, not a transcription of this manuscript, whose wording differs in places. The English is Murray's prose translation (1919), divided every five lines; at a page break a five-line block goes with the page holding most of it.
Where the scribe left out a line, wrote one in the margin, changed the order or added a line not in Murray's text, the section's note on the photograph says so; the Greek and English shown are Murray's full text for the page's range.
