Introduction

        
Rigveda 8.64 is a hymn of 12 verses addressed to Indra. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 8, the book of the Kaṇva and Aṅgiras poets. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 634v-635r. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1MAY our hymns give thee great delight. Display thy bounty, Thunderer. Drive off the enemies of prayer.
2Crush with thy foot the niggard churls who bring no gifts. Mighty art thou There is not one to equal thee.
3Thou art the Lord of Soma pressed, Soma impressed is also thine. Thou art the Sovran of the folk.
4Come, go thou forth, dwelling in heaven and listening to the prayers of men: Thou fillest both the heavens and earth.
5Even that hill with rocky heights, with hundreds, thousands, held within. Thou for thy worshippers brakest through.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
6We call on thee both night and day to taste the flowing Soma juice: Do thou fulfil our heart's desire.
7Where is that ever-youthful Steer, strong-necked and never yet bent down? What Brahman ministers to him?
8To whose libation doth the Steer, betake him with delight therein? Who takes delight in Indra now?
9Whom, Vritra-slayer, have thy gifts and hero powers accompanied? Who is thy dearest in the land?
10For thee among mankind, among the Pûrus is this Soma shed. Hasten thou hither: drink thereof.
11This, growing by Sushomâ and by Ṣaryaṇâvân, dear to thee, In Ârjîkîya, cheers thee best.
12Hasten thou hitherward, and drink this for munificence to-day Delightful for thine eager draught.
