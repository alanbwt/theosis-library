Introduction

        
Rigveda 8.11 is a hymn of 10 verses addressed to Agni. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 8, the book of the Kaṇva and Aṅgiras poets. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 577v-578r. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

            §1-3 · RV 8.11.1-3 (aṣṭaka 5, adhyāya 8, varga 35); Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 fol. 577v
            

              
              
              
            

          


          
          

            
Manuscript

            
            
            
            
Where on the image
Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41, fol. 577v (numbered 98v by the scribe) (OPenn image 578, upper leaf). Checked on the image: the Rigveda text on this page begins in RV 8.10.2 ‹-viśvān devān aham huve›. The next page with Rigveda text, fol. 578r (numbered 99r by the scribe), begins in RV 8.11.4 ‹anti cit santam aha›. Verses 8.11.1-3 end on this page.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1THOU Agni, God mid mortal men, art guard of sacred rites, thou art To be adored at sacrifice.
2O Mighty Agni, thou must be glorified at our festivals, Bearing our offerings to the Gods.
3O Jâtavedas Agni, fight and drive our foes afar from us, Themand their godless enmities.

          

        

        
        


          

            §4-10 · RV 8.11.4-10 (aṣṭaka 5, adhyāya 8, varga 35); Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 fol. 578r

Where on the image

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
4Thou, Jâtavedas, seekest not the worship of a hostile man, However nigh it be to thee.
5We sages, mortals as we are, adore the mighty name of thee, Immortal Jâtavedas' name.
6Sages, we call the Sage to help, mortals, we call the God to aid: We call on Agni with our songs.
7May Vatsa draw- thy mind away even from thy loftiest dwelling-place, Agni, with song that yearns for thee.
8Thou art the same in many a place: mid all the people thou art Lord. In fray and fight we call on thee.
9When we are seeking strength we call Agni to help us in the strife, The giver of rich gifts in war.
10Ancient, adorable at sacrifices, Priest from of old, meet for our praise, thou sittest. Fill full and satisfy thy body, Agni, and win us happiness by offering worship.
