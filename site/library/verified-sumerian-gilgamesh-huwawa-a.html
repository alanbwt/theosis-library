Introduction

        
Gilgameš and Ḫuwawa (Version A, also called 'The lord to the living one's mountain') is one of the Sumerian poems about Gilgameš, king of Uruk, that lie behind the later Babylonian Epic of Gilgamesh. Wishing to make a lasting name, Gilgameš sets out with Enkidu and fifty men to the mountain where the cedars grow. The sun god Utu gives him seven helpers; Gilgameš tricks the guardian Ḫuwawa into giving up his seven auras, and when Ḫuwawa pleads for his life Enkidu kills him. Enlil is angered and redistributes Ḫuwawa's auras. The poem is known from Old Babylonian school tablets (about 1900-1700 BCE).
No single photographed tablet preserves the whole text. 21-33 "Utu, I have something to say to you -- a word in your ear! I greet you -- please pay attention! In my city people are dying, and hearts are full of distress.
