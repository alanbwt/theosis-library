Introduction

        
The Pyramid Texts are the oldest large body of Egyptian religious writing: spells, called utterances, for the resurrection of the dead king, his protection, his meals and his ascent to the sky, cut in vertical columns of hieroglyphs on the walls of the burial apartments of late Old Kingdom pyramids. The pyramid of Unas at Saqqara, the last king of the 5th Dynasty (about 2375-2345 BCE), is the first to carry them. Its sarcophagus chamber, the passage to the antechamber, the antechamber and the corridor hold 649 numbered columns of text; this library gives every column, wall by wall, in seventeen entries (227 different utterances, some of them inscribed more than once).



This entry is the south wall of the passage between sarcophagus chamber and antechamber: 19 columns of hieroglyphs, Sethe's W 360-378, which follow each other from the right-hand (west) edge of the photograph. It holds the utterances 244, 245, 246.



Each section is one utterance. The Egyptian is the transliteration of the Thesaurus Linguae Aegyptiae (edition by Doris Topmann), with Sethe's Unas column number (W) before each column; a number ending in 'a' marks the offering label at the foot of a column. Signs restored by the editor are in square brackets, signs the scribe left out in angle brackets, damaged signs in half brackets. The English is Samuel A. B. Mercer's translation of 1952. Mercer translates Sethe's composite text made from all the pyramids that have an utterance, so his wording can include clauses that are not in the Unas copy, and his section numbers (134a, 134b ...) are Sethe's paragraph numbers, not column numbers; he writes 'N.' where the wall has the king's name. The photograph shows the whole wall; each section's note gives its columns counted from the edge where the text on that wall begins.

            §244 · PT 244 (Sethe §§ 249a-249b); Unas W 360
            

              
              
              
            

          


          
          

            
Manuscript

            
            
            
            
Where on the image
Unas W 360: W 360 is column 1 of the 19 columns on the photograph of the passage, south wall, counted from the right-hand (west) edge. Wall check (south wall): 19 columns (partial ones at both edges), W 360 at the right-hand (west) edge to W 378 at the left. Checked on the image: two cartouches in the second column from the right (W 361 'jyi̯ n=ṯ Wnjs pn Nw,t' twice), the three stars of sbꜣ.pl in the fifth (W 364) and a cartouche in mid-column in the eighth (W 367). Mercer's English renders Sethe's composite text of this utterance from all pyramids; it can contain words that are not in the Unas copy.

          

          

          

            
Egyptian · TLA transliteration

                    
360jr〈,t〉 tw nn ⸢n⸣.[t] [Ḥr,w] [rwḏ.t] di̯ =〈j〉 n =k s〈j〉 〈j〉m〈j〉m =k nri̯ =f n =k
360asḏ dšr〈,t〉.pl

            
          


          

            
English · Samuel A. B. Mercer (1952)

                    
249aTo say: O Osiris N., this here is the [hard] eye of Horus.
249bTake it to thee that thou mayest be strong, (and) that he (Set) may fear thee.
Rubric. Breaking of two red jars.

English · Samuel A. B. Mercer (1952)

                    
250aThis N. comes to thee Nut; this N. comes to thee Nut.
250bHe has thrown his father to the ground; he has left Horus behind him.
250cHis two wings are grown as (those of) a falcon; (his) two feathers as (those of) a gmḥśw-falcon.
250dHis ba has brought him (here); his magic power has equipped him.
251aThou openest thy place in heaven, among the stars of heaven;
251bthou art the only star, the companion of Ḥw; thou lookest down on Osiris,
251cas he commands the spirits. Thou standest there far from him.
251dThou are not of them; thou shalt not be of them.

253bfurious in wrath, whose might no one withstands.
