Introduction

        
Rigveda 1.5 is a hymn of 10 verses addressed to Indra. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 1, a collection of hymns by many families of poets. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 3r-3v. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

            §1-5 · RV 1.5.1-5 (aṣṭaka 1, adhyāya 1, varga 9); Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 fol. 3r
            

              
              
              
            

          


          
          

            
Manuscript

            
            
            
            
Where on the image
Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41, fol. 3r (OPenn image 4, lower leaf). Checked on the image: the Rigveda text on this page begins in RV 1.4.6 ‹uta naḥ subhagān ariḥ›. English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1O COME ye hither, sit ye down: to Indra sing ye forth, your song, companions, bringing hymns of praise.
2To him the richest of the rich, the Lord of treasures excellent, Indra, wi th Soma juice outpoured.
3May he stand by us in our need and in abundance for our wealth: May he come nigh us with his strength.
4Whose pair of tawny horses yoked in battles foemen challenge not: To him, to Indra sing your song.
5Nigh to the Soma-drinker come, for his enjoyment, these pure drops, The Somas mingled with the curd.

          

        

        
        


          

            §6-10 · RV 1.5.6-10 (aṣṭaka 1, adhyāya 1, varga 10); Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 fol. 3v

Where on the image
Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41, fol. 3v (OPenn image 5, upper leaf). Checked on the image: the Rigveda text on this page begins in RV 1.5.6 ‹-jyaiṣṭhyāya sukrato itisu-krato›. The next page with Rigveda text, fol. 4r, begins in RV 1.6.7 ‹indreṇa sam hi dṛkṣase›. Verses 1.5.6-10 end on this page; 1.5.6 begins on fol. 3r.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
6Thou, grown at once to perfect strength, wast born to drink the Soma juice, Strong Indra, for preeminence.
7O Indra, lover of the song, may these quick Somas enter thee: May they bring bliss to thee the Sage.
8Our chants of praise have strengthened thee, O Ṣatakratu, and our lauds So strengthen thee the songs we sing.
9Indra, whose succour never fails, accept these viands thousandfold, Wherein all manly powers abide.
10O Indra, thou who lovest song, let no man hurt our bodies, keep Slaughter far from us, for thou canst.
