Introduction

        
Mark 16 from Codex Washingtonianus (the Freer Gospels, Gregory-Aland 032, Freer Gallery of Art F1906.274), a parchment codex of the four Gospels written in Egypt in the late 4th or early 5th century and bought by Charles Lang Freer from the dealer Ali Arabi at Giza in 1906. It is one of the oldest nearly complete Gospel books in existence. The Gospels stand in the so-called Western order, Matthew, John, Luke, Mark, and the text is famously block-mixed: the copyist drew on several exemplars of different textual character, so the text type changes in blocks within a single Gospel. After Mark 16:14 the codex carries the Freer Logion, a saying found in no other manuscript. Two passages are lost to damage: Mark 15:13-37 and John 14:26-16:6.
  1. Scan: each section is shown with the actual page of the codex that carries those verses, from the Freer Gallery's own photographs of F1906.274 served by the Smithsonian IIIF service (ids.si.edu). Which page holds which verse comes from the verse index of the Center for the Study of New Testament Manuscripts (CSNTM), whose photographs of the codex are the same shots; every CSNTM page was matched to its Smithsonian image by pixel comparison before use. Where a verse begins on one page and ends on the next, both pages are listed. For this chapter the page of each verse is taken from the transcription itself, which records where every page begins; CSNTM's index confirms which photograph shows that page.
  2. Greek: the manuscript's own text, from a diplomatic transcription (see Greek text below), with Tischendorf's 8th critical edition (1869) for comparison.
  3. English: the King James Version (1611, public domain), verbatim from bible-api.com. The KJV translates the later Byzantine text, so it will not always match the Greek shown or the reading of the codex.
The Freer Logion: on the page carrying 16:14 the codex inserts, after that verse, a passage in which the disciples excuse their unbelief and Christ replies that the term of Satan's power is fulfilled. It is not part of Tischendorf's text or of the KJV and so does not appear in either column; it can be read on the scan of that page, where it fills the lines between 16:14 and 16:15.
Greek text: a diplomatic transcription of Codex Washingtonianus made by the Institut für Neutestamentliche Textforschung (INTF), Münster, for the New Testament Virtual Manuscript Room (NTVMR), published under a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 licence. It gives the letters as the first scribe wrote them: no accents or breathings, the scribe's own spelling, and the sacred names in their contracted form (θς for θεος, ις for ιησους, κς for κυριος; the overline is not reproduced). Corrections by later hands are not shown. Letters in square brackets are not legible on the page and were restored by the transcribers, a dot under a letter marks an uncertain reading, and [...] marks text lost to damage. Beneath each section, for comparison, is Tischendorf's 8th critical edition (1869). Where the two differ, the transcription is what the manuscript says. Each section is shown with the page on which the transcription places its verses.
Manuscript note: After verse 14 the codex carries the Freer Logion (κακεινοι απελογουντε λεγοντες ... κληρονομησωσιν), a passage found in no other manuscript; the transcription includes it in verse 14, and the KJV has no English for it.
