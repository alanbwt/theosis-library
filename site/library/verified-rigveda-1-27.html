Introduction

        
Rigveda 1.27 is a hymn of 13 verses addressed to Agni. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 1, a collection of hymns by many families of poets. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 15v-16v. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

Vedic Sanskrit · Aufrecht (accented)

                    
1aśva̱ṁ na tvā̱ vāra̍vantaṁ va̱ndadhyā̍ a̱gniṁ namo̍bhiḥ | sa̱mrāja̍ntam adhva̱rāṇā̍m ||
2sa ghā̍ naḥ sū̱nuḥ śava̍sā pṛ̱thupra̍gāmā su̱śeva̍ḥ | mī̱ḍhvām̐ a̱smāka̍m babhūyāt ||

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1WITH worship will I glorify thee, Agni, like a long-tailed steed, Imperial Lord of sacred rites.
2May the far-striding Son of Strength, bringer of great felicity, Who pours his gifts like rain, be ours.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
3Lord of all life, from near; from far, do thou, O Agni evermore Protect us from the sinful man.
4O Agni, graciously announce this our oblation to the Gods, And this our newest song of praise.
5Give us a share of strength most high, a share of strength that is below, A share of strength that is between.
6Thou dealest gifts, resplendent One; nigh, as with waves of Sindhu, thou Swift streamest to the worshipper.
7That man is lord of endless strength whom thou protectest in the fight, Agni, or urgest to the fray.
8Him, whosoever he may be, no man may vanquish, mighty One: Nay, very glorious power is his.
9May he who dwells with all mankind bear us with war-steeds through the fight, And with the singers win the spoil.
10Help, thou who knowest lauds, this work, this eulogy to Rudra, him Adorable in every house.
11May this our God, great, limitless, smoke-bannered excellently bright, Urge us to strength and holy thought.
12Like some rich Lord of men may he, Agni the banner of the Gods, Refulgent, hear us through our lauds.
