Introduction

        
Rigveda 1.69 is a hymn of 10 verses addressed to Agni. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 1, a collection of hymns by many families of poets. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 48r-48v. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1BRIGHT, splendid, like Dawn's lover, he hath filled the two joined worlds as with the light of heaven.
2When born, with might thou hast encompassed them: Father of Gods, and yet their Son wast thou.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
3Agni, the Sage, the humble, who discerns like the cow's udder, the sweet taste of food,
4Like a bliss-giver to be drawn to men, sits gracious in the middle of the house.
5Born in the dwelling like a lovely son, pleased, like a strong steed, he bears on the folk.
6What time the men and I, with heroes, call, may Agni then gain all through Godlike power.
7None breaks these holy laws of thine when thou hast granted audience to these chieftains here.
8This is thy boast, thou smotest with thy peers, and joined with heroes dravest off disgrace.
9Like the Dawn's lover, spreading light, well-known as hued like morn, may he remember me.
10They, bearing of themselves, unbar the doors: they all ascend to the fair place of heaven.
