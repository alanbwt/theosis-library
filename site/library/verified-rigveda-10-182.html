Introduction

        
Rigveda 10.182 is a hymn of 3 verses addressed to Brihaspati. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 10, the last and latest book. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 48 (University of Pennsylvania), a paper manuscript of the eighth aṣṭaka (RV 10.46-10.191) of the Ṛgveda Saṃhitā in Devanāgarī, 158 leaves, 6 lines to the page, with the accents marked in red; the scribe's colophon on fol. 158 gives Saṃvat 1768 (1711 CE); this hymn stands on fol. 152r-152v. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Like most Rigveda manuscripts, Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 48 writes a repeated refrain in short form after its first occurrence (for example kasmai° for kasmai devāya haviṣā vidhema); the Sanskrit shown is the full text of Aufrecht's edition.

Vedic Sanskrit · Aufrecht (accented)

                    
1bṛha̱spati̍r nayatu du̱rgahā̍ ti̱raḥ puna̍r neṣad a̱ghaśa̍ṁsāya̱ manma̍ | kṣi̱pad aśa̍sti̱m apa̍ durma̱tiṁ ha̱nn athā̍ kara̱d yaja̍mānāya̱ śaṁ yoḥ ||
2narā̱śaṁso̍ no 'vatu prayā̱je śaṁ no̍ astv anuyā̱jo have̍ṣu | kṣi̱pad aśa̍sti̱m apa̍ durma̱tiṁ ha̱nn athā̍ kara̱d yaja̍mānāya̱ śaṁ yoḥ ||
3tapu̍rmūrdhā tapatu ra̱kṣaso̱ ye bra̍hma̱dviṣa̱ḥ śara̍ve̱ hanta̱vā u̍ | kṣi̱pad aśa̍sti̱m apa̍ durma̱tiṁ ha̱nn athā̍ kara̱d yaja̍mānāya̱ śaṁ yoḥ ||

            
          


          

            
English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1Br̥ihaspati lead us safely over troubless and turn his evil thought against the sinner; Repel the curse, and drive away ill-feeling, and give the sacrificer peace and cornfort!
2May Narâṣansa aid us at Prayâja: blest be our Anuyâja at invokings. May he repel the curse, and chase ill-feeling, and give the sacrificer peace and comfort.
3May he whose head is flaming burn the demons, haters of prayer, so that the arrow slay them. May he repel the curse and chase ill-feeling, and give the sacrificer peace and comfort.
