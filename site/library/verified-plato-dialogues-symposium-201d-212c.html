Introduction

        
At the banquet held by the poet Agathon each guest gives a speech in praise of Love (Eros). Socrates does not give his own: he repeats what he says he learned from Diotima, a wise woman of Mantinea. Love, she taught, is not a god but a great spirit (daimon) that carries messages between gods and men; it is the desire to possess the good for ever, and so it is also a desire for immortality, which mortals reach by begetting, in body or in soul. The speech ends with the 'ladder' of love: from one beautiful body to all bodies, to souls, practices, laws and sciences, until the lover sees beauty itself, pure and unmixed; such a man, Diotima says, 'is destined to win the friendship of Heaven; he, above all men, is immortal' (212a).
