Introduction

        
Rigveda 4.11 is a hymn of 6 verses addressed to Agni. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 4, the family book of Vāmadeva. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 266r-267r. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1THY blessed majesty, victorious Agni, shines brightly in the neighbourhood of Sûrya. Splendid to see, it shows even at nighttime, and food is fair to look on in thy beauty.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
2Agni, disclose his thought for him who singeth, the well, Strong God! while thou art praised with fervour. Vouchsafe to us that powerful hymn, O Mighty, which, Radiant One! with all the Gods thou lovest.
3From thee, O Agni, springs poetic wisdom, from thee come thoughts and hymns of praise that prosper; From thee flows wealth, with heroes to adorn it, to the true-hearted man who gives oblation.
4From thee the hero springs who wins the booty, bringer of help, mighty, of real courage. From thee comes wealth, sent by the Gods, bliss-giving; Agni, from thee the fleet impetuous charger.
5Immortal Agni, thee whose voice is pleasant, as first in rank, as God, religious mortals Invite with hyrnns; thee who removest hatred, Friend of the Home, the household's Lord, unerring.

Manuscript

            
            
            
            
            
            
Where on the image
Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41, fol. 267r (numbered 76r by the scribe) (OPenn image 267, lower leaf). Checked on the image: the Rigveda text on this page begins in RV 4.11.6 ‹-amatim āre aṃhaḥ āre›. 6ā̱re a̱smad ama̍tim ā̱re aṁha̍ ā̱re viśvā̍ṁ durma̱tiṁ yan ni̱pāsi̍ | do̱ṣā śi̱vaḥ sa̍hasaḥ sūno agne̱ yaṁ de̱va ā ci̱t saca̍se sva̱sti ||

6āre | asmat | amatim | āre | aṃhaḥ | āre | viśvām | duḥ-matim | yat | ni-pāsi | doṣā | śivaḥ | sahasaḥ | sūno iti | agne | yam | devaḥ | ā | cit | sacase | svasti
