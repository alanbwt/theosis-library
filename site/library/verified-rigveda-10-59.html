Introduction

        
Rigveda 10.59 is a hymn of 10 verses addressed to diverse gods. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 10, the last and latest book. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 48 (University of Pennsylvania), a paper manuscript of the eighth aṣṭaka (RV 10.46-10.191) of the Ṛgveda Saṃhitā in Devanāgarī, 158 leaves, 6 lines to the page, with the accents marked in red; the scribe's colophon on fol. 158 gives Saṃvat 1768 (1711 CE); this hymn stands on fol. 14v-15v. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Like most Rigveda manuscripts, Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 48 writes a repeated refrain in short form after its first occurrence (for example kasmai° for kasmai devāya haviṣā vidhema); the Sanskrit shown is the full text of Aufrecht's edition.

Vedic Sanskrit · Aufrecht (accented)

                    
1pra tā̱ry āyu̍ḥ prata̱raṁ navī̍ya̱ḥ sthātā̍reva̱ kratu̍matā̱ ratha̍sya | adha̱ cyavā̍na̱ ut ta̍vī̱ty artha̍m parāta̱raṁ su nirṛ̍tir jihītām ||
2sāma̱n nu rā̱ye ni̍dhi̱man nv anna̱ṁ karā̍mahe̱ su pu̍ru̱dha śravā̍ṁsi | tā no̱ viśvā̍ni jari̱tā ma̍mattu parāta̱raṁ su nirṛ̍tir jihītām ||

            
          


          

            
English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1His life hath been renewed and carried forward as two men, car-borne, by the skilful driver. One falls, then seeks the goal with quickened vigour. Let Nirr̥iti depart to distant places.
2Here is the psalm for wealth, and food, in plenty: let us do many deeds to bring us glory. All these our doings shall delight the singer. Let Nirr̥iti depart to distant places.

Vedic Sanskrit · Aufrecht (accented)

                    
3a̱bhī ṣv a1̱̍ryaḥ pauṁsyai̍r bhavema̱ dyaur na bhūmi̍ṁ gi̱rayo̱ nājrā̍n | tā no̱ viśvā̍ni jari̱tā ci̍keta parāta̱raṁ su nirṛ̍tir jihītām ||
4mo ṣu ṇa̍ḥ soma mṛ̱tyave̱ parā̍ dā̱ḥ paśye̍ma̱ nu sūrya̍m u̱ccara̍ntam | dyubhi̍r hi̱to ja̍ri̱mā sū no̍ astu parāta̱raṁ su nirṛ̍tir jihītām ||
5asu̍nīte̱ mano̍ a̱smāsu̍ dhāraya jī̱vāta̍ve̱ su pra ti̍rā na̱ āyu̍ḥ | rā̱ra̱ndhi na̱ḥ sūrya̍sya sa̱ṁdṛśi̍ ghṛ̱tena̱ tvaṁ ta̱nva̍ṁ vardhayasva ||
6asu̍nīte̱ puna̍r a̱smāsu̱ cakṣu̱ḥ puna̍ḥ prā̱ṇam i̱ha no̍ dhehi̱ bhoga̍m | jyok pa̍śyema̱ sūrya̍m u̱ccara̍nta̱m anu̍mate mṛ̱ḻayā̍ naḥ sva̱sti ||

            
          


          

            
English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
3May we o'ercome our foes with acts of valour, as heaven is over earth, hills over lowlands. All these our deeds the singer hath considered. Let Nirr̥iti depart to distant places.
4Give us not up as prey to death, O Sorna still let us look upon the Sun arising. Let our old age with passing days be kindly. Let Nirr̥iti depart to distant places.
5O Asuniti, keep the soul within us, and make the days we have to live yet longer. Grant that we still may look upon the sunlight: strengthen thy body with the oil we bring thee.
6Give us our sight again, O Asuniti, give us again our breath and our enjoyment. Long may we look upon the Sun uprising; O Anumati, favour thou and bless us.

Vedic Sanskrit · Aufrecht (accented)

                    
7puna̍r no̱ asu̍m pṛthi̱vī da̍dātu̱ puna̱r dyaur de̱vī puna̍r a̱ntari̍kṣam | puna̍r na̱ḥ soma̍s ta̱nva̍ṁ dadātu̱ puna̍ḥ pū̱ṣā pa̱thyā̱3̱̍ṁ yā sva̱stiḥ ||
8śaṁ roda̍sī su̱bandha̍ve ya̱hvī ṛ̱tasya̍ mā̱tarā̍ | bhara̍tā̱m apa̱ yad rapo̱ dyauḥ pṛ̍thivi kṣa̱mā rapo̱ mo ṣu te̱ kiṁ ca̱nāma̍mat ||
9ava̍ dva̱ke ava̍ tri̱kā di̱vaś ca̍ranti bheṣa̱jā | kṣa̱mā ca̍ri̱ṣṇv e̍ka̱kam bhara̍tā̱m apa̱ yad rapo̱ dyauḥ pṛ̍thivi kṣa̱mā rapo̱ mo ṣu te̱ kiṁ ca̱nāma̍mat ||
10sam i̍ndreraya̱ gām a̍na̱ḍvāha̱ṁ ya āva̍had uśī̱narā̍ṇyā̱ ana̍ḥ | bhara̍tā̱m apa̱ yad rapo̱ dyauḥ pṛ̍thivi kṣa̱mā rapo̱ mo ṣu te̱ kiṁ ca̱nāma̍mat ||

            
          


          

            
English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
7May Earth restore to us our vital spirit, may Heaven the Goddess and mid-air restore it. May Soma give us once again our body, and Pûshan show the Path of peace and comfort.
8May both Worlds bless Subandhu, young Mothers of everlasting Law. May Heaven and Earth uproot and sweep iniquity and shame away: nor sin nor sorrow trouble thee.
9Health-giving medicines descend sent down from heaven in twos and threes, Or wandering singly on the earth. May Heaven and Earth uproot and sweep iniquity and shame away: nor sin nor sorrow trouble thee.
10Drive forward thou the wagon-ox, O Indra, which brought Uṣînarâṇî's wagon hither. May Heaven and Earth uproot and sweep iniquity and shame away: nor sin nor sorrow trouble thee.
