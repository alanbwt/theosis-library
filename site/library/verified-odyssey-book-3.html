Introduction

        
The Odyssey, composed around 700 BCE and attributed to Homer, tells of Odysseus' ten-year journey home from Troy and his return to Ithaca. Book 3 (497 lines): Telemachus at Pylos.
The page photographs are from Florence, Biblioteca Medicea Laurenziana, Plut. 32.24 (parchment, 10th century, 234 folios; the oldest manuscript of the Odyssey with published images), in the Biblioteca Medicea Laurenziana's Teca Digitale. This book is on fol. 19r-29r; each section below is one page, with its first and last line as read on the photograph. Many pages carry interlinear glosses and a few marginal notes in later hands.
The Greek is A. T. Murray's Loeb text (1919) via Perseus, not a transcription of this manuscript, whose wording differs in places. The English is Murray's prose translation (1919), divided every five lines; at a page break a five-line block goes with the page holding most of it.
Where the scribe left out a line, wrote one in the margin, changed the order or added a line not in Murray's text, the section's note on the photograph says so; the Greek and English shown are Murray's full text for the page's range.

And the housewife placed in the car bread and wine 480and dainties, such as kings, fostered of Zeus, are wont to eat. Then Telemachus mounted the beautiful car, and Peisistratus, son of Nestor, a leader of men, mounted beside him, and took the reins in his hands. He touched the horses with the whip to start them, and nothing loath the pair sped on 485to the plain, and left the steep citadel of Pylos. So all day long they shook the yoke which they bore about their necks. Now the sun set and all the ways grew dark. And they came to Pherae, to the house of Diocles, son of Ortilochus, whom Alpheus begot.
