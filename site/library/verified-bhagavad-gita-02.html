Introduction

        
The Bhagavad Gita ("Song of the Lord") is a dialogue of 700 verses between the prince Arjuna and Krishna, set on the battlefield of Kurukshetra in book 6 (Bhishmaparvan) of the Mahabharata. Its date of composition is debated; many scholars place it in the last centuries BCE. This is chapter 2, Yoga by the Sankhya (72 verses as numbered in the manuscript and in Besant's edition).



The pages shown are from a complete Sanskrit manuscript of the Gita at the University of Pennsylvania (Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 559), 90 paper leaves of 7 lines in Devanagari, with speaker headings, colophons and verse-end marks in red. Its final colophon (fol. 90r) dates the copy to Saṃvat 1851, the third day of the bright half of Caitra (1794 CE). Chapter 2 runs from fol. 9v to the chapter colophon on fol. 18r.



Each section below is one manuscript page: the verses whose text ends on that page, located by reading the scribe's verse numbers and the first and last words on the image. When a verse begins at the foot of one page and ends on the next, both pages are shown. The Sanskrit and English are from Annie Besant's edition and translation (4th edition, 1922), which prints each verse in Devanagari with its English; the manuscript's spelling differs from the printed text in small details.

Sanskrit · Devanagari (Besant 1922)

            
2श्रीभगवानुवाच ।
कुतस्त्वा कश्मलमिदं विषमे समुपस्थितम् ।
अनार्यजुष्टमस्वर्ग्यमकीर्तिकरमर्जुन ॥ २ ॥
3क्लैव्यं मा स्म गमः पार्थ नैतत्त्वय्युपपद्यते ।
क्षुद्रं हृदयदौर्बल्यं त्यक्त्वोत्तिष्ट परन्तप ॥ ३ ॥
4अर्जुन उवाच ।
कथं भीष्ममहं सङ्ख्ये द्रोणं च मधुसूदन ।
इषुभिः प्रतियोत्स्यामि पूजार्हावरिसूदन ॥ ४ ॥
5गुरूनहत्वा हि महानुभावान् श्रेयो भोक्तुं भैक्ष्यमपीह लोके ।
हत्वाऽर्थकामांस्तु गुरूनिहैव भुञ्जीय भोगान्रुधिरप्रदिग्धान् ॥ ५ ॥

            
          


          

            
English · Annie Besant (1922)

            
2The Blessed Lord said:
Whence hath this dejection befallen thee in this perilous strait, ignoble, heaven-closing, infamous, O Arjuna?
3Yield not to impotence, O Partha! it doth not befit thee. Shake off this paltry faint-heartedness! Stand up, Parantapa!
4Arjuna said:
How, O Madhusûdana, shall I attack Bhîshma and Drona with arrows in battle, they who are worthy of reverence, O slayer of foes?
5Better in this world to eat even the beggar's crust than to slay these most noble Gurus. Slaying these Gurus, our well-wishers, I should taste of blood-besprinkled feasts.

6न चैतद्विद्मः कतरन्नो गरीयो यद्वा जयेम यदि वा नो जयेयुः ।
यानेव हत्वा न जिजीविषाम: तेऽवस्थिताः प्रमुखे धार्तराष्ट्राः ॥ ६ ॥
7कार्पण्यदोषोपहतस्वभावः पृच्छामि त्वां धर्मसम्मूढचेताः ।
यच्छ्रेयः स्यान्निश्चितं ब्रूहि तन्मे शिष्यस्तेऽहं शाधि मां त्वां प्रपन्नम् ॥ ७ ॥
8न हि प्रपश्यामि ममापनुद्याद्यच्छोकमुच्छोषणमिन्द्रियाणाम् ।
अवाप्य भूमावसपत्नमृद्धं राज्यं सुराणामपि चाधिपत्यम् ॥ ८ ॥
9सञ्जय उवाच ।
एवमुक्त्वा हृषीकेशं गुडाकेशः परन्तपः ।
न योत्स्य इति गोविन्दमुक्त्वा तूष्णीं बभूव ह ॥ ९ ॥

            
          


          

            
English · Annie Besant (1922)

            
6Nor know I which for us be the better, that we conquer them or they conquer us—these, whom having slain we should not care to live, even these arrayed against us, the sons of Dhritarâshtra.
7My heart is weighed down with the vice of faintness; my mind is confused as to duty. I ask thee which may be the better—that tell me decisively. I am thy disciple, suppliant to Thee; teach me.
8For I see not that it would drive away this anguish that withers up my senses, if I should attain unrivalled monarchy on earth, or even the sovereignty of the Shining Ones.
9Sanjaya said:
Gudâkesha, conqueror of his foes, having thus addressed Hrishîkesha and said to Govinda, "I will not fight!", became silent.

सेनयोरुभयोर्मध्ये विषीदन्तमिदं वचः ॥ १० ॥
11श्रीभगवानुवाच ।
अशोच्यानन्वशोचस्त्वं प्रज्ञावादांश्च भाषसे ।

13देहिनोऽस्मिन्यथा देहे कौमारं यौवनं जरा ।

English · Annie Besant (1922)

            
10Then Hrishîkesha, smiling, as it were, O Bhârata, spake these words to him, despondent, in the midst of the two armies:
11The Blessed Lord said:
Thou grievest for those that should not be grieved for, yet speakest words of wisdom. The wise grieve neither for the living nor for the dead.
12Nor at any time verily was I not, nor thou, nor these princes of men, nor verily shall we ever cease to be, hereafter.
13As the dweller in the body experienceth in the body childhood, youth, old age, so passeth he on to another body; the steadfast one grieveth not thereat.

14मात्रास्पर्शास्तु कौन्तेय शीतोष्णसुखदुःखदाः ।
आगमापायिनोऽनित्यास्तांस्तितिक्षस्व भारत ॥ १४ ॥

16नासतो विद्यते भावो नाभावो विद्यते सतः ।

17अविनाशि तु तद्विद्धि येन सर्वमिदं ततम् ।
विनाशमव्ययस्यास्य न कश्चित्कर्तुमर्हति ॥ १७ ॥
18अन्तवन्त इमे देहा नित्यस्योक्ताः शरीरिणः ।
अनाशिनोऽप्रमेयस्य तस्माद्युध्यस्व भारत ॥ १८ ॥

            
          


          

            
English · Annie Besant (1922)

            
14The contacts of matter, O son of Kuntî, giving cold and heat, pleasure and pain, they come and go, impermanent; endure them bravely, O Bhârata.
15The man whom these torment not, O chief of men, balanced in pain and pleasure, steadfast, he is fitted for immortality.
16The unreal hath no being; the real never ceaseth to be; the truth about both hath been perceived by the seers of the essence of things.
17Know That to be indestructible by whom all this is pervaded. Nor can any work the destruction of that imperishable One.
18These bodies of the embodied One, who is eternal, indestructible and immeasurable, are known as finite. Therefore fight, O Bhârata.

19य एनं वेत्ति हन्तारं यश्चैनं मन्यते हतम् ।
उभौ तौ न विजानीतो नायं हन्ति न हन्यते ॥ १९ ॥
20न जायते म्रियते वा कदाचिन्नायं भूत्वा भविता वा न भूयः ।
अजो नित्यः शाश्वतोऽयं पुराणो न हन्यते हन्यमाने शरीरे ॥ २० ॥
21वेदाऽविनाशिनं नित्यं य एनमजमव्ययम् ।
कथं स पुरुषः पार्थ कं घातयति हन्ति कम् ॥ २१ ॥
22वासांसि जीर्णानि यथा विहाय नवानि गृह्णाति नरोऽपराणि ।
तथा शरीराणि विहाय जीर्णान्यन्यानि संयाति नवानि देही ॥ २२ ॥

            
          


          

            
English · Annie Besant (1922)

            
19He who regardeth this as a slayer, and he who thinketh he is slain, both of them are ignorant. He slayeth not, nor is he slain.
20He is not born, nor doth he die; nor having been, ceaseth he any more to be; unborn, perpetual, eternal and ancient, he is not slain when the body is slaughtered.
21Who knoweth him indestructible, perpetual, unborn, undiminishing, how can that man slay, O Pârtha, or cause to be slain?
22As a man, casting off worn-out garments, taketh new ones, so the dweller in the body, casting off worn-out bodies, entereth into others that are new.

23Weapons cleave him not, nor fire burneth him, nor waters wet him, nor wind drieth him away.

25Unmanifest, unthinkable, immutable, he is called; therefore knowing him as such, thou shouldst not grieve.
26Or if thou thinkest of him as being constantly born and constantly dying, even then, O mighty-armed, thou shouldst not grieve.

English · Annie Besant (1922)

            
27For certain is death for the born, and certain is birth for the dead; therefore over the inevitable thou shouldst not grieve.

30This dweller in the body of everyone is ever invulnerable, O Bhârata; therefore thou shouldst not grieve for any creature.

32Happy the Kshattriyas, O Pârtha, who obtain such a fight, offered unsought as an open door to heaven.

34Men will recount thy perpetual dishonour, and, to one highly esteemed, dishonour exceedeth death.
35The great car-warriors will think thee fled from the battle from fear, and thou, that wast highly thought of by them, wilt be lightly held.

            The last section of each chapter is the chapter colophon. Besant prints most Sanskrit colophons in shortened form (marked with ॰) while her English gives the full formula; the manuscript writes the colophon out in full, and its chapter names differ in places from Besant's (see the scan note).
