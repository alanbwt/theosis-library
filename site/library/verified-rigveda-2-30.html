Introduction

        
Rigveda 2.30 is a hymn of 11 verses addressed to Indra and other gods. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 2, the family book of Gṛtsamada. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 172v-173v. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1THE streams unceasing flow to Indra, slayer of Ahi, Savitar, God, Law's fulfiller, Day after day goes on the sheen of waters. What time hath past since they were first set flowing?
2His Mother-for she knew-spake and proclaimed him who was about to cast his bolt at Vr̥itra. Cutting their paths according to his pleasure day after day flow to their goal the rivers.
3Aloft he stood above the airy region, and against Vr̥itra shot his deadly missile. Enveloped in a cloud he rushed upon him. Indra subdued the foe with sharpened weapons.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
4As with a bolt, Br̥ihaspati, fiercely flaming, pierce thou Vr̥ikadvaras', the Asura's, heroes. Even as in time of old with might thou slewest, so slay even now our enemy, O Indra.
5Cast down from heaven on high thy bolt of thunder wherewith in joy thou smitest dead the foeman. For gain of children make us thine, O Indra, of many children's children and of cattle.
6Whomso ye love, his power ye aid and strengthen; ye Twain are the rich worshipper's advancers. Graciously favour us, Indra and Soma; give us firm standing in this time of danger.
7Let it not vex me, tire me, make me slothful, and never let us say, Press not the Soma; For him who cares for me, gives gifts, supports me, who comes with kine to me who pour libations.
8Sarasvatî, protect us: with the Maruts allied thou boldly conquerest our foemen, While Indra does to death the daring chieftain of Ṣaṇḍikas exulting in his prowess.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
9Him who waylays, yea, him who would destroy us,– aim at him, pierce him with thy sharpened weapon. Br̥ihaspati, with arms thou slayest foemen O King, give up the spoiler to destruction.
10Perform, O Hero, with our valiant heroes the deeds heroic which thou hast to finish. Long have they been inflated with presumption: slay them, and bring us hither their possessions.
11I craving joy address with hymn and homage your heavenly host, the company of Maruts, That we may gain wealth with full store of heroes, each day more famous, and with troops of children.
