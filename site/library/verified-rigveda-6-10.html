Introduction

        
Rigveda 6.10 is a hymn of 7 verses addressed to Agni. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 6, the family book of Bharadvāja. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 412r-412v. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

Manuscript

            
            
            
            
Where on the image

Checked on the image: the Rigveda text on this page begins in RV 6.9.7 ‹devāḥ anamasyan bhiyānāḥ tvām›. Vedic Sanskrit · Aufrecht (accented)

                    
1pu̱ro vo̍ ma̱ndraṁ di̱vyaṁ su̍vṛ̱ktim pra̍ya̱ti ya̱jñe a̱gnim a̍dhva̱re da̍dhidhvam | pu̱ra u̱kthebhi̱ḥ sa hi no̍ vi̱bhāvā̍ svadhva̱rā ka̍rati jā̱tave̍dāḥ ||
2tam u̍ dyumaḥ purvaṇīka hota̱r agne̍ a̱gnibhi̱r manu̍ṣa idhā̱naḥ | stoma̱ṁ yam a̍smai ma̱mate̍va śū̱ṣaṁ ghṛ̱taṁ na śuci̍ ma̱taya̍ḥ pavante ||

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1INSTALL at sacrifice, while the rite advances, your pleasant, heavenly Agni, meet for praises. With hymns-for he illumines us-install him. He, Jâtavedas, makes our rites successful.
2Hear this laud, Radiant Priest of many aspects, O Agni with the fires of man enkindled, Laud which bards send forth pure as sacred butter, strength to this man, as 'twere for selfadvantage.

4ā yaḥ pa̱prau jāya̍māna u̱rvī dū̍re̱dṛśā̍ bhā̱sā kṛ̱ṣṇādhvā̍ | adha̍ ba̱hu ci̱t tama̱ ūrmyā̍yās ti̱raḥ śo̱ciṣā̍ dadṛśe pāva̱kaḥ ||
5nū na̍ś ci̱tram pu̍ru̱vājā̍bhir ū̱tī agne̍ ra̱yim ma̱ghava̍dbhyaś ca dhehi | ye rādha̍sā̱ śrava̍sā̱ cāty a̱nyān su̱vīrye̍bhiś cā̱bhi santi̱ janā̍n ||
6i̱maṁ ya̱jñaṁ cano̍ dhā agna u̱śan yaṁ ta̍ āsā̱no ju̍hu̱te ha̱viṣmā̍n | bha̱radvā̍jeṣu dadhiṣe suvṛ̱ktim avī̱r vāja̍sya̱ gadhya̍sya sā̱tau ||

7vi | dveṣāṃsi | inuhi | vardhaya | iḷām | madema | śata-himāḥ | su-vīrāḥ

            

            
          


          

            
English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
3Mid mortal men that singer thrives in glory who offers gifts with hymns of praise to Agni, And the God, wondrous bright, with wondrous succours helps him to win a stable filled with cattle.
4He, at his birth, whose path is black behind him, filled heaven and earth with far-apparent splendour: And he himself hath been. through night's thick darkness, made manifest by light, the Purifier.
5With thy most mighty aid, confer, O Agni, wonderful wealth on us and on our princes, Who stand preeminent, surpassing others in liberal gifts, in fame, and hero virtues.
6Agni, accept this sacrifice with gladness, which, seated here, the worshipper presenteth. Fair hymns hadst thou among the Bharadvâjas, and holpest them to gain abundant vigour.
7Scatter our foes, increase our store. May we he glad a hundred winters with brave sons.
