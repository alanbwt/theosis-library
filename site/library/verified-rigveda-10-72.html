Introduction

        
Rigveda 10.72 is a hymn of 9 verses addressed to the gods / hymn of creation. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 10, the last and latest book. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 48 (University of Pennsylvania), a paper manuscript of the eighth aṣṭaka (RV 10.46-10.191) of the Ṛgveda Saṃhitā in Devanāgarī, 158 leaves, 6 lines to the page, with the accents marked in red; the scribe's colophon on fol. 158 gives Saṃvat 1768 (1711 CE); this hymn stands on fol. 37v-38v. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Like most Rigveda manuscripts, Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 48 writes a repeated refrain in short form after its first occurrence (for example kasmai° for kasmai devāya haviṣā vidhema); the Sanskrit shown is the full text of Aufrecht's edition.

Vedic Sanskrit · Aufrecht (accented)

                    
1de̱vānā̱ṁ nu va̱yaṁ jānā̱ pra vo̍cāma vipa̱nyayā̍ | u̱ktheṣu̍ śa̱syamā̍neṣu̱ yaḥ paśyā̱d utta̍re yu̱ge ||
2brahma̍ṇa̱s pati̍r e̱tā saṁ ka̱rmāra̍ ivādhamat | de̱vānā̍m pū̱rvye yu̱ge 'sa̍ta̱ḥ sad a̍jāyata ||
3de̱vānā̍ṁ yu̱ge pra̍tha̱me 'sa̍ta̱ḥ sad a̍jāyata | tad āśā̱ anv a̍jāyanta̱ tad u̍ttā̱napa̍da̱s pari̍ ||

            
          


          

            
English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1LET US with tuneful skill proclaim these generations of the Gods, That one may see them when these hymns are chanted in a future age.
2These Brahmaṇaspati produced with blast and smelting, like a Smith, Existence, in an earlier age of Gods, from Non-existence sprang.
3Existence, in the earliest age of Gods, from Non-existence sprang. Thereafter were the regions born. This sprang from the Productive Power.

Vedic Sanskrit · Aufrecht (accented)

                    
4bhūr ja̍jña uttā̱napa̍do bhu̱va āśā̍ ajāyanta | adi̍te̱r dakṣo̍ ajāyata̱ dakṣā̱d v adi̍ti̱ḥ pari̍ ||
5adi̍ti̱r hy aja̍niṣṭa̱ dakṣa̱ yā du̍hi̱tā tava̍ | tāṁ de̱vā anv a̍jāyanta bha̱drā a̱mṛta̍bandhavaḥ ||
6yad de̍vā a̱daḥ sa̍li̱le susa̍ṁrabdhā̱ ati̍ṣṭhata | atrā̍ vo̱ nṛtya̍tām iva tī̱vro re̱ṇur apā̍yata ||
7yad de̍vā̱ yata̍yo yathā̱ bhuva̍nā̱ny api̍nvata | atrā̍ samu̱dra ā gū̱ḻham ā sūrya̍m ajabhartana ||
8a̱ṣṭau pu̱trāso̱ adi̍te̱r ye jā̱tās ta̱nva1̱̍s pari̍ | de̱vām̐ upa̱ prait sa̱ptabhi̱ḥ parā̍ mārtā̱ṇḍam ā̍syat ||

            
          


          

            
English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
4Earth sprang from the Productive Power the regions from the earth were born. Daksha was born of Aditi, and Aditi was Daksha's Child.
5For Aditi, O Daksha, she who is thy Daughter, was brought forth. After her were the blessed Gods born sharers of immortal life.
6When ye, O Gods, in yonder deep closeclasping one another stood, Thence, as of dancers, from your feet a thickening cloud of dust arose.
7When, O ye Gods, like Yatis, ye caused all existing things to grow, Then ye brought Sûrya forward who was lying hidden in the sea.
8Eight are the Sons of Adid who from her body sprang to life. With seven she went to meet the Gods she cast Mârtâṇḍa far away.
