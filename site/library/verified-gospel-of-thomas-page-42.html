Introduction

        
The Gospel of Thomas is a collection of 114 sayings attributed to Jesus, introduced as the hidden sayings written down by Didymos Judas Thomas. The only complete copy is the second tractate of Nag Hammadi Codex II, a papyrus book in Coptic copied in the 4th century and found near Nag Hammadi in Upper Egypt in 1945; it is now in the Coptic Museum, Cairo (inv. 10544). Greek fragments of the work survive in three Oxyrhynchus papyri (P. Oxy. 1, 654 and 655), which are not shown here.
This entry covers the sayings 51-57, which begin on codex page 42; the end of saying 57 is on page 43. The Coptic is given line by line; the small numbers are codex page and line (for example 33.5), so each line can be found on the photograph. Page and line breaks follow the Coptic SCRIPTORIUM edition by Paul Dilley, which he made from the photographs of The Facsimile Edition of the Nag Hammadi Codices, the negatives shown here.
Photograph: Codex II page 42, A-Series negative by Basile Psiroukis, Facsimile Edition p. 52, CCDL nha 2871; Nag Hammadi Archive of the Institute for Antiquity and Christianity, Claremont Colleges Digital Library. Checked on the photograph: page 42 line 1 begins 'ⲁⲩⲱ ⲁϥⲟⲩⲱⲛϩ ⲉ[ⲃ]ⲟⲗ ϩⲛ̄ⲧⲟⲩϩⲓⲕⲱⲛ ⲉⲩ' and line 35 reads 'ⲉ̣ⲛ̣[ⲁⲛⲟ]ⲩϥ⳿ ⲁⲡⲉϥϫⲁϫⲉ ⲉⲓ ⲛ̄ⲧⲟⲩϣⲏ⳿' (the top line is damaged at the left; the last line begins after a break).

  1. 
            Coptic: Coptic SCRIPTORIUM edition (Paul C. Dilley), version 6.2.0, CC BY 4.0, shown in the manuscript's own spelling with words grouped as bound groups. Editorial signs are the edition's: square brackets mark letters lost in a gap (restored by the editor, or dots for the number of lost letters), a dot under a letter marks an uncertain reading, round brackets write out a letter abbreviated by a stroke at a line end, angle brackets mark letters added by the editor, curly brackets letters the editor removes, double square brackets letters erased by the scribe.

Where a word is divided between two lines on the papyrus, the edition sometimes keeps the word whole on one line, so a line can begin a few letters earlier or later on the photograph (for example 39.33-34, and the page change inside saying 13).
