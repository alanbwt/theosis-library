Introduction

        
P66 is a papyrus codex of the Gospel of John copied in Egypt around 200, the most complete early copy of John. Most of it is Papyrus Bodmer II in the Fondation Martin Bodmer, Cologny (Geneva); small pieces are in the Chester Beatty Library, Dublin, and the Institut für Altertumskunde, Cologne. The first five quires (John 1:1-14:26) survive as a bound book of numbered pages; later leaves, from John 14:29 to 21:9, are damaged and kept between glass. The scribe made many corrections, some of them his own; only the first hand is shown here.



Each section below is one page: the photograph, the NTVMR diplomatic transcription line by line, and the King James Version of every verse on the page. Pages of the bound book are shown on the photographic plates published by Victor Martin and J. W. B. Barns in 1962 (scanned by the Bodmer Lab), the glass-mounted leaves on colour photographs. Lost: John 6:12-34 (pages 35-38) and parts of chapters 14-21; the transcription ends at 21:9. Where P66 lacks a verse that the KJV has (John 5:4, the account of the adulteress at 7:53-8:11, 16:15), the section says so.



This entry covers pages 18-25 (John 4:6-5:2).

English · King James Version (1611)

                    
4:50Jesus saith unto him, Go thy way; thy son liveth. And the man believed the word that Jesus had spoken unto him, and he went his way. 51And as he was now going down, his servants met him, and told him, saying, Thy son liveth. 52Then enquired he of them the hour when he began to amend. And they said unto him, Yesterday at the seventh hour the fever left him. 53So the father knew that it was at the same hour, in the which Jesus said unto him, Thy son liveth: and himself believed, and his whole house. 54This is again the second miracle that Jesus did, when he was come out of Judæa into Galilee. 5:1After this there was a feast of the Jews; and Jesus went up to Jerusalem. 2Now there is at Jerusalem by the sheep market a pool, which is called in the Hebrew tongue Bethesda, having five porches.

          

        

        
      


    

    

    

      
      

        
Notes

        
    
          
          
  1. 
            Greek: the NTVMR diplomatic transcription, line by line as on the papyrus. Square brackets enclose letters that are lost or illegible and were supplied by the transcribers; a dot under a letter marks it as uncertain; [...] marks a gap. Nomina sacra and numerals keep the scribe's overline. Spelling is the scribe's (no accents or breathings). Words broken at a line end stay broken as on the papyrus. Corrections are not shown: the text is the first hand.
          
    2. 
          
          
  2. 
            English: the King James Version (1611), whole verses, for every verse that has text on the page. A verse that begins on one page and ends on the next is given in full on both. The KJV translates a later form of the Greek text, so its wording does not always match the papyrus; material differences (such as verses the papyrus does not have) are noted in the section.
