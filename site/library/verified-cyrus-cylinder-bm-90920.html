Cyrus Cylinder, lines 6-36 (BM 90920)

        
        
Cyrus II (the Great), king of Persia (559-530 BCE)

        
British Museum BM 90920, photographs by Prioryman on Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 3.0) + ORACC ARIo text and translation (CC BY-SA 3.0) · ORACC ARIo Cyrus II 1 (after I. Finkel)

        
      


      

      

        
Introduction

        
The Cyrus Cylinder is a barrel-shaped clay cylinder inscribed in Babylonian cuneiform for Cyrus II (the Great), king of Persia, after he took Babylon in 539 BCE. It was buried in the foundations of the city wall of Babylon, where it was found in 1879 by Hormuzd Rassam; it is in the British Museum (BM 90920). The text says that Nabonidus, the last king of Babylon, neglected the cult of Marduk, that Marduk chose Cyrus, who entered Babylon without a battle, and that Cyrus returned the gods of many cities to their shrines and gathered their peoples back to their settlements. This policy is often compared with the decree of Cyrus in the Old Testament that allowed the Jews to return to Jerusalem and rebuild the temple (Ezra 1:1-4; 2 Chronicles 36:22-23), although the cylinder names only Mesopotamian cities and gods.
This entry is partial. It gives lines 6-36, which the two available photographs show: the side with lines 5-22 and the side with lines 20-36. Lines 1-5 and 37-46 are not included, because no openly available photograph shows them (the beginning of the text is badly broken, and the last lines are on the side that rests on the stand). The Akkadian and English are ARIo's edition (after I. Finkel), divided into ARIo's paragraphs.

(As for) the black-headed people whom he allowed his hand to conquer, he was assiduous towards their welfare in truth and justice. The god Marduk, the great lord, the one who nurtures his people, look[ed] with pleasure upon his good deeds and righteous heart.

33Moreover, (as for) the deities of the land of Sumer and Akkad who Nabonidus, incurring the wrath of the lord of the gods (Marduk), had brought into Šuanna (Babylon), at the command of the god Marduk, the great lord, I allowed (them) to live in peace in their abodes, (in) the dwelling(s) of (their) heart’s content. May all of the deities whom I made enter into their cult-centers speak daily about my life being long (and) may they mention favorable words about me in the presence of the gods Bēl (Marduk) and Nabû. Furthermore, may they say this to the god Marduk, my lord: “[(As for) the ...] of Cyrus (II), the king who reveres you, and Cambyses (II), his son, [...] may they be the ones who provision our shrines until distant days.” Also, the people of Babylon blessed (my) kingship. I allowed the lands, all of them, to live undisturbed.

          

        

        
      


    

    

    

      
      

        
Notes

        
    
          
          
  1. 
            ARIo's English is divided into paragraphs that do not always begin at a line start; a number such as 23b marks a paragraph that begins inside line 23. ARIo translates the first part of such a line (23a) at the end of the previous paragraph; that clause is moved to the section that carries the line, so each section's English covers the same lines as its Akkadian. Lines 9-14 form one section because ARIo's English for lines 10 and 11 is interwoven.
          
    2. 
          
          
  2. 
            The transliteration follows ARIo: Sumerian word-signs in capitals, determinatives raised (m before a man's name, d before a god's name), half brackets for damaged signs, square brackets for restorations.
