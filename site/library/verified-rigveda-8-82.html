Introduction

        
Rigveda 8.82 is a hymn of 9 verses addressed to Indra. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 8, the book of the Kaṇva and Aṅgiras poets. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 649r-649v. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

Manuscript

            
            
            
            
Where on the image
Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41, fol. 649r (numbered 71r by the scribe) (OPenn image 649, lower leaf). Checked on the image: the Rigveda text on this page begins in RV 8.82.1 ‹ā pra drava parāvataḥ›. Lines 1-9 carry oṃ and the anukramaṇī for aṣṭaka 6, adhyāya 6; 8.82.1 (ā pra drava parāvataḥ) begins in line 9. The next page with Rigveda text, fol. 649v (numbered 71v by the scribe), begins in RV 8.82.2 ‹-sutāsaḥ mādayiṣṇavaḥ piba dadhṛk›. Verse 8.82.1 ends on this page.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1HASTE forward to us from afar, or, Vritra-slayer, from anear, To meet the offering to the meath.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
2Strong are the Soma-draughts; come nigh: the juices fill thee with delight: Drink boldly even as thou art wont'.
3Joy, Indra, in the strengthening food: let it content thy wish and thought, And be delightful to thine heart.
4Come to us thou who hast no foe: we call thee down to hymns of praise, In heaven's sublimest realm of light.
5This Soma here expressed with stones and dressed with milk for thy carouse, Indra, is offered up to thee.
6Graciously, Indra, hear my call. Come and obtain the draught, and sate Thyself with juices blent with milk.
7The Soma, Indra, which is shed in chalices and vats for thee, Drink thou, for thou art Lord thereof.
8The Soma seen within the vats, as in the flood the Moon is seen, Drink thou, for thou art Lord thereof.
9That which the Hawk brought in his claw, inviolate, through the air to thee, Drink thou, for thou art Lord thereof.
