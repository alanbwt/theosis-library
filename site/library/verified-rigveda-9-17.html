Introduction

        
Rigveda 9.17 is a hymn of 8 verses addressed to Soma. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 9, the hymns to Soma Pavamāna. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 674v-675r. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1LIKE rivers down a steep descent, slaying the Vr̥itras, full of zeal, The rapid Soma-streams have flowed.
2The drops of Soma juice effused fall like the rain upon the earth: To Indra flow the Soma-streams.
3With swelling wave the gladdening drink, the Soma, flows into.the sieve, Loving the Gods and slaying fiends.
4It hastens to the pitchers, poured upon the sieve it waxes strong At sacrifices through the lauds.
5Soma, thou shinest mounting heaven as 'twere above light's triple realm, And moving secm'st to speed the Sun.
6To him, the head of sacrifice, singers and bards have sung their songs, Offering what he loves to see.
7The men, the sages with their hymns, eager for help, deck thee strong steed, Deck thee for service of the Gods.
8Flow onward to the stream of meath rest efficacious in thy home, Fair, to be drunk at sacrifice.
