Introduction

        
Rigveda 9.102 is a hymn of 8 verses addressed to Soma. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 9, the hymns to Soma Pavamāna. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 733v-734r. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1THE Child, when blended with the streams, speeding the plan of sacrifice, Surpasses all things that are dear, yea, from of old.
2The place, near the two pressing-stones of Trita, hath he occupied, Secret and dear through seven lights of sacrifice.
3Urge to three courses, on the heights of Trita, riches in a stream. He who is passing wise measures his courses out.
4Even at his birth the Mothers Seven taught him, for glory, like a sage, So that he, firm and sure, hath set his mind on wealth.
5Under his sway, of one accord, are all the guileless Deities: Warriors to be envied, they, when they are pleased.
6The Babe whom they who strengthen Law have generated fair to see, Much longed for at the sacrifice, most liberal Sage,–

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
7To him, united, of themselves, come the young Parents of the rite, When they adorn him, duly weaving sacrifice.
8With wisdom and with radiant eyes unbar to us the stall of heaven, Speeding at solemn rite the plan of Holy Law.
