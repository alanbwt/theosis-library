Introduction

        
Rigveda 3.25 is a hymn of 5 verses addressed to Agni. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 3, the family book of Viśvāmitra. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 205r-205v. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

            §1-2 · RV 3.25.1-2 (aṣṭaka 3, adhyāya 1, varga 25); Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 fol. 205r
            

              
              
              
            

          


          
          

            
Manuscript

            
            
            
            
Where on the image
Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41, fol. 205r (numbered 15r by the scribe) (OPenn image 206, lower leaf). Checked on the image: the Rigveda text on this page begins in RV 3.24.2 ‹-idhyase vīti-hotraḥ amartyaḥ juṣasva›. The next page with Rigveda text, fol. 205v (numbered 15v by the scribe), begins in RV 3.25.3 ‹-bhāti devī iti amṛteiti›. Verses 3.25.1-2 end on this page.

2a̱gniḥ sa̍noti vī̱ryā̍ṇi vi̱dvān sa̱noti̱ vāja̍m a̱mṛtā̍ya̱ bhūṣa̍n | sa no̍ de̱vām̐ eha va̍hā purukṣo ||

2agniḥ | sanoti | vīryāṇi | vidvān | sanoti | vājam | amṛtāya | bhūṣan | saḥ | naḥ | devān | ā | iha | vaha | purukṣo itipuru-kṣo

            

            
          


          

            
English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1THOU art the sapient Son of Dyaus, O Agni, yea, and the Child of Earth, who knowest all things. Bring the Gods specially, thou Sage, for worship.
2Agni the wise bestows the might of heroes grants strengthening food, preparing it for nectar. Thou who art rich in food bring the Gods hither.

          

        

        
        


          

            §3-5 · RV 3.25.3-5 (aṣṭaka 3, adhyāya 1, varga 25); Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 fol. 205v
            

              
              
              
            

          


          
          

            
Manuscript

            
            
            
            
            
            
Where on the image
Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41, fol. 205v (numbered 15v by the scribe) (OPenn image 207, upper leaf). Checked on the image: the Rigveda text on this page begins in RV 3.25.3 ‹-bhāti devī iti amṛteiti›. The next page with Rigveda text, fol. 206r (numbered 16r by the scribe), begins in RV 3.26.3 ‹-krandan jani-bhiḥ sam idhyate›. Verses 3.25.3-5 end on this page; 3.25.3 begins on fol. 205r (numbered 15r by the scribe).

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
3Agni, infallible, lights Earth and Heaven, immortal Goddesses gracious to all men,– Lord through his strength, splendid through adorations.
4Come to the sacrifice, Agni and Indra come to the offerer's house who hath the Soma. Come, friendly-minded, Gods, to drink the Soma.
5In the floods' home art thou enkindled, Agni, O Jâtavedas, Son of Strength, eternal, Exalting with thine help the gatheringplaces.
