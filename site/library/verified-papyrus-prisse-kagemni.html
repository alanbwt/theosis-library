Introduction

        
The Papyrus Prisse, bought at Thebes in 1843 by Emile Prisse d'Avennes and now in the Bibliotheque nationale de France (Egyptien 183-194), is one of the oldest surviving literary papyri. It was written in the 12th Dynasty, around 1850 BCE, in a large hieratic book hand. The roll begins with the end of a wisdom text addressed to Kagemni; the beginning of that text is lost.



The instruction is set in the Old Kingdom: a vizier gives rules of modest conduct (silence, restraint at table, no boasting) to his children, who read them aloud; then King Huni dies, Sneferu becomes king, and Kagemni is made vizier. The two columns of Kagemni (12 and 9 lines) are on frame Egyptien 183; the papyrus then has a stretch of erased writing (frames 184-185) before the Instruction of Ptahhotep begins.



Each section below gives the Thesaurus Linguae Aegyptiae transliteration with the papyrus page and line numbers (1,1 to 2,9), the English of Battiscombe Gunn (1906), and the photograph of the column that carries it. Signs supplied by the editor are in angle brackets, superfluous signs in braces, damaged signs in half brackets, and the lost end of the colophon is shown as [...].

Egyptian · TLA transliteration

                    
1,1wḏꜣ snḏ,w ḥzi̯ mt〈j〉 wn ẖn n gr,w 1,2wsḫ s,t n.t hru̯ m mdwi̯.w spd ds.w.pl 1,3r thi̯-mtn nn ḥn n js ḥr zp =f

            
          


          

            
English · Battiscombe G. Gunn (1906)

                    
The cautious man flourisheth, the exact one is praised; the innermost chamber openeth unto the man of silence. Wide is the seat of the man gentle of speech; but knives are prepared against one that forceth a path, that he advance not, save in due season.

Egyptian · TLA transliteration

                    
1,3jr ḥmsi̯ =k ḥnꜥ ꜥšꜣ,t 1,4msdi̯ tʾ mrr =k ꜣ,t pw kt.t dꜣjr-jb ḫw pw ꜣfꜥ 1,5jw ḏbꜥ =t〈w〉 jm jw jkn n mw ꜥḫm =f jb,t jw mḥ,{t}〈w〉 rʾ m šw,w 1,6smn =f jb jw nfr,t jdn bw-nfr jw nh〈j〉 n ktt jdn wr ẖz 1,7pw ḥnti̯ n ẖ,t =f swꜣ tr smḫ.n =f wstn ẖ,t m pr =sn

            
          


          

            
English · Battiscombe G. Gunn (1906)

                    
If thou sit with a company of people, desire not the bread that thou likest: short is the time of restraining the heart, and gluttony is an abomination; therein is the quality of a beast. A cup of water quencheth the thirst, and a mouthful of melon supporteth the heart. A good thing standeth for goodness, but some small thing standeth for plenty. A base man is he that is governed by his belly; he departeth only when he is no longer able to fill full his belly in men's houses.

Egyptian · TLA transliteration

                    
1,7jr 1,8ḥmsi̯ =k ḥnꜥ ꜣfꜥ wnm =k ꜣḫf =f swꜣ jr zw{r}j =k ḥnꜥ 1,9tḫ,w šzp =k jw jb =f ḥtp.w m ꜣt.w r jwf r-gs skn 1,10šzp ḏi̯ =f n =k m wjn st kꜣ szf.t pw

            
          


          

            
English · Battiscombe G. Gunn (1906)

                    
If thou sit with a glutton, eat with him, then depart (?).

        
If thou drink with a drunkard, accept [drink], and his heart shall be satisfied.

        
Refuse not meat when with a greedy man. Take that which he giveth thee; set it not on one side, thinking that it will be a courteous thing.

Egyptian · TLA transliteration

                    
2,3rḏi̯.jn ṯꜣ,t〈j〉 njs.t〈w〉 nꜣy =f n ẖrd.w.pl m-ḫt ꜥrq =f sḫr 2,4r〈m〉ṯ bj〈ꜣ〉,t =sn m jyi̯.t ḥr =f ḏr.n ḏd.n =f n =sn jr n,t.t nb.t m zẖꜣw ḥr 2,5pꜣ šfd,w sḏm st mj ḏd =j st m zni̯ ḥꜣw-ḥr šꜣꜣ.t wn.jn =sn 2,6ḥr rḏi̯.t st ḥr ẖ,t.pl =sn wn.jn =sn ḥr šdi̯.t st mj n,t.t m zẖꜣw wn.jn nfr st ḥr jb =sn 2,7r ḫ,t nb.t n,t{j}〈.t〉 m tꜣ pn r-ḏr =f wn.jn ꜥḥꜥ =sn ḥmsi̯ =sn ḫft ꜥḥꜥ.n ḥm n nsw,t-bj,tj Ḥwnj 2,8mnj.n =f ꜥḥꜥ.n sꜥḥꜥ ḥm n nsw,t-bj,tj Snfr,w m nsw,t mnḫ m tꜣ pn r-ḏr =f ꜥḥꜥ.n rḏi̯ 2,9Kꜣ=〈j〉-gmi̯.n=j r 〈j〉m〈,j〉-rʾ-nʾ,t ṯꜣ,t〈j〉

            
          


          

            
English · Battiscombe G. Gunn (1906)

                    
The Vizier caused his sons and daughters to be summoned, when he had finished the rules of the conduct of men. And they marvelled when they came to him. Then he said unto them, 'Hearken unto everything that is in writing in this book, even as I have said it in adding unto profitable sayings.' And they cast themselves on their bellies, and they read it, even as it was in writing. And it was better in their opinion than any thing in this land unto its limits.

        
Now they were living when His Majesty, the King of Upper and Lower Egypt, HEUNI, departed, and His Majesty, the King of Upper and Lower Egypt, SENFÔRU, was enthroned as a gracious king over the whole of this land.

        
Then was Ke'gemni made Governor of his City and Vizier.
