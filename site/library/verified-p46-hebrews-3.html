Introduction

        
P46 is a papyrus codex of the letters of Paul copied in Egypt around 200, the oldest surviving collection of Paul's letters. 86 of its 104 leaves survive: 56 in the Chester Beatty Library, Dublin (Biblical Papyrus II, published by Frederic Kenyon in 1934-1937) and 30 in the University of Michigan Library, Ann Arbor (P.Mich. inv. 6238, published by Henry Sanders in 1935). The letters run in the order Romans, Hebrews, 1 and 2 Corinthians, Ephesians, Galatians, Philippians, Colossians and 1 Thessalonians; the first seven leaves (Romans 1:1-5:17) and the end of the book are lost. Hebrews standing after Romans, and the doxology of Romans (16:25-27) standing after 15:33, are among its best-known features.



Each section below is one page side: the holding library's photograph, the NTVMR diplomatic transcription line by line, and the King James Version of every verse that has text on the page. The lower lines of many leaves are lost; the transcribers' restorations are in square brackets.



This entry covers fols. 23v-24r (Hebrews 3:3-4:3).

English · King James Version (1611)

                    
3:3For this man was counted worthy of more glory than Moses, in asmuch as he who hath builded the house hath more honour than the house. 4For every house is builded by some man; but he that built all things is God. 5And Moses verily was faithful in all his house, as a servant, for a testimony of those things which were to be spoken after; 6But Christ as a son over his own house; whose house are we, if we hold fast the confidence and the rejoicing of the hope firm unto the end. 7Wherefore (as the Holy Ghost saith, To day if ye will hear his voice, 8Harden not your hearts, as in the provocation, in the day of temptation in the wilderness: 9When your fathers tempted me, proved me, and saw my works forty years. 10Wherefore I was grieved with that generation, and said, They do alway err in their heart; and they have not known my ways. 11So I sware in my wrath, They shall not enter into my rest.) 12Take heed, brethren, lest there be in any of you an evil heart of unbelief, in departing from the living God. 13But exhort one another daily, while it is called To day; lest any of you be hardened through the deceitfulness of sin.

English · King James Version (1611)

                    
3:13But exhort one another daily, while it is called To day; lest any of you be hardened through the deceitfulness of sin. 14For we are made partakers of Christ, if we hold the beginning of our confidence stedfast unto the end; 15While it is said, To day if ye will hear his voice, harden not your hearts, as in the provocation. 16For some, when they had heard, did provoke: howbeit not all that came out of Egypt by Moses. 17But with whom was he grieved forty years? was it not with them that had sinned, whose carcases fell in the wilderness? 18And to whom sware he that they should not enter into his rest, but to them that believed not? 19So we see that they could not enter in because of unbelief. 4:1Let us therefore fear, lest, a promise being left us of entering into his rest, any of you should seem to come short of it. 2For unto us was the gospel preached, as well as unto them: but the word preached did not profit them, not being mixed with faith in them that heard it. 3For we which have believed do enter into rest, as he said, As I have sworn in my wrath, if they shall enter into my rest: although the works were finished from the foundation of the world.

          

        

        
      


    

    

    

      
      

        
Notes

        
    
          
          
  1. 
            Greek: the NTVMR diplomatic transcription, line by line as on the papyrus. Square brackets enclose letters that are lost or illegible and were supplied by the transcribers; a dot under a letter marks it as uncertain; [...] marks a gap. Nomina sacra and numerals keep the scribe's overline. Spelling is the scribe's (no accents or breathings). Words broken at a line end stay broken as on the papyrus. Corrections are not shown: the text is the first hand.
          
    2. 
          
          
  2. 
            English: the King James Version (1611), whole verses, for every verse that has text on the page. A verse that begins on one page and ends on the next is given in full on both. The KJV translates a later form of the Greek text, so its wording does not always match the papyrus; material differences (such as verses the papyrus does not have) are noted in the section.
