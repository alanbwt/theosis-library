Introduction

        
The Book of the Dead, in Egyptian the spells for going forth by day, is a collection of funerary spells written on papyrus rolls and placed in burials from the New Kingdom to the Roman period. Each roll was made for one person, with spells taken from a large repertoire and illustrated with drawings (vignettes). This library gives one complete roll: the papyrus of Iuefankh in the Museo Egizio, Turin (Cat. 1791), written in the Ptolemaic period (332-30 BCE) and coming from Thebes. Richard Lepsius published it in 1842 and divided it into 165 chapters; his numbering is still the standard numbering of the spells, and it is used here.



Spell 71 (Birch: 'The chapter of Coming forth as the Day, of stopping the Robber, of how a Person is not taken in Hades, and his Soul is sound in [escapes from] Taser.') is on Lepsius's plates XXVI-XXVII and on photographs 10, 11 of the 31 Museo Egizio photographs of the roll.



The Egyptian is the transliteration of the Thesaurus Linguae Aegyptiae (edition by Burkhard Backes), with the column number of the spell before each column; the numbers are those of Lepsius's plates. On the papyrus the text runs in vertical columns of cursive hieroglyphs from right to left along the roll; titles and some words are written in red. Signs restored by the editor are in square brackets, signs the scribe left out in angle brackets, superfluous signs in braces and damaged signs in half brackets. The English is the translation by Samuel Birch, published in 1867 and made from Lepsius's copy of this papyrus, so the English and the Egyptian come from the same manuscript. It was the first complete English translation of the Book of the Dead; many passages are now understood differently. Birch's own square brackets give his alternatives and explanations. The photographs are by the Museo Egizio; each shows a stretch of the roll, and the note of each section says where the spell stands on it.

Object: Turin, Museo Egizio, Cat. 1791 (papyrus of Iuefankh), Book of the Dead, Ptolemaic period (332-30 BCE), from Thebes

            
Images: Museo Egizio, Turin, photographs of Cat. 1791 on Wikimedia Commons (https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Book_of_the_Dead_of_Iuefankh_-_Museo_Egizio_(Turin)_C._1791), e.g. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Book_of_the_Dead_of_Iuefankh,_papyrus_-_Museo_Egizio_Turin_C_1791_p034.jpg (CC0 1.0 (Museo Egizio, as stated on Wikimedia Commons))

            
Text-to-image mapping: Spell order, plate and column numbers from Lepsius, Das Todtenbuch der Aegypter (1842), whose plates reproduce this papyrus and are headed by their chapter numbers; the TLA uses the same numbers. The 31 Museo Egizio photographs (Commons p034 to p04, from the start to the end of the roll) were each compared by eye with Lepsius's plates: vignettes, red titles and framed compartments identify the spells on each photograph, and the overlapping edges of neighbouring photographs were compared side by side. Positions in the notes are measured from the left edge of the photograph.

English · Samuel Birch (1867)

            
LXXI. The chapter of Coming forth as the Day, of stopping the Robber, of how a Person is not taken in Hades, and his Soul is sound in [escapes from] Taser.
[Vignette. — A mummied Cow with Plumes and Hawk.]
On Hawk coming forth from Nu! Lord of the Great Cow [the Flood, Meh-hul']. I am sound as thou thyself art well, I net, I enwrap, I have placed myself on the earth as I wished, [said] by the Lord doing this to me only. I am the Hawk within the bandages, passing through the earth out of the case or the doors, or the gates, [said] by Horus son of Isis [repeated]. I am the Hawk of the Southern heaven, the Ibis of the Northern heaven, pacifying destructive fire, passing truth to the Lord of Truth [said] by Thoth. — Then, “I am sound,” &c., to “doing this to me only.”
I am the Unbu [flower] of the region where nought grows [Anrutf], the flower fiabhu of the hidden place, [said] by Osiris. — Then follows, “ I am sound,” &c.
Oh “Burning [Fuming] with his foot!” reckoning the moment, the Viper, Lord of Souls, dwelling in his halves! sound, &c., to “ doing this to me only.”
Oh Sun! I am well, &c. Sebak stands on his floor, Nit stands in the fountain. I am well, &c.
Oh seven Chief Powers at the arm of the Balance! the day of judgment, cutting off heads, breaking necks taking hearts, destroying hearts, making blows in the Pool of Fire! I knew ye, I knew your name I have known ye, each of ye: I approach to ye, approach ye to me. I live like you, ye live as I do — impart ye me the life in your hands, the health in your fists — impart ye the life of your lips, give ye to me many years for my years of life. He gave me many days for the days of my life, many nights for the nights of my life, to be well and shine or to breathe as the types; breath to thy nostril, thy eyes see as if they were those of the horizon, the day of reckoning the transgressions.
