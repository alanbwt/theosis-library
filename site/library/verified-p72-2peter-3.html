Introduction

        
P72 is a papyrus codex copied in Egypt in the 3rd or early 4th century. It holds the oldest known copies of 1 Peter, 2 Peter and Jude. The pages are numbered by the scribe. 1-2 Peter (papyrus pages 1-36) are Papyrus Bodmer VIII, given by Martin Bodmer to Pope Paul VI in 1969 and now in the Vatican Library; Jude (pages 62-68) is Papyrus Bodmer VII, still at the Fondation Martin Bodmer, Cologny (Geneva). Both belong to the Bodmer "Miscellaneous" or "Composite" codex, which also contains the Nativity of Mary, the apocryphal correspondence of Paul with the Corinthians, the 11th Ode of Solomon, Melito's sermon On the Pascha, a hymn fragment and other texts. 1-2 Peter seem to have formed a separate booklet before being bound with the rest.



Each section below is one papyrus page: the photograph, the NTVMR diplomatic transcription of that page line by line, and the King James Version of every verse on it. The scribe's spelling is irregular (ει for ι, ε for αι, and so on) and is kept as written.



This entry covers papyrus pages 33-36 (2 Peter 3:3-18). The photographs are from the Vatican Library (Pap. Bodmer VIII).

Where on the image
Vatican Library, Pap. Bodmer VIII, DigiVatLib image 18 (https://digi.vatlib.it/view/MSS_Pap.Bodmer.VIII/0018), the right-hand page of the opening, cropped at the fold. English · King James Version (1611)

                    
3:3Knowing this first, that there shall come in the last days scoffers, walking after their own lusts, 4And saying, Where is the promise of his coming? for since the fathers fell asleep, all things continue as they were from the beginning of the creation. 5For this they willingly are ignorant of, that by the word of God the heavens were of old, and the earth standing out of the water and in the water: 6Whereby the world that then was, being overflowed with water, perished: 7But the heavens and the earth, which are now, by the same word are kept in store, reserved unto fire against the day of judgment and perdition of ungodly men. 8But, beloved, be not ignorant of this one thing, that one day is with the Lord as a thousand years, and a thousand years as one day.

            §p36 · P72 page 36: 2 Peter 3:16-18
            

              
              
              
            

          


          
          

            
Manuscript

            
            
            
            
Where on the image
Vatican Library, Pap. Bodmer VIII, DigiVatLib image 20 (https://digi.vatlib.it/view/MSS_Pap.Bodmer.VIII/0020), the single leaf in the frame. The typed label on the glass frame reads "X verso = p. 36 (Λς) 2 Petri 3, 16-18". 3:16 begins on the previous page. The page also carries a book title, shown in italics in the Greek (πετρου επιστολη β); the KJV has no English for it. Checked on the image: 14 lines in the transcription; the first legible line (line 1) reads ‹περι τουτων εν αις εστιν›, the last legible line (line 14) ‹πετρου επιστολη β›; both match the page. As on page 22, the title is followed by ειρηνη τω γραψαντι και τω αναγινωσκοντι between two ornaments, recorded by NTVMR only in a note.

English · King James Version (1611)

                    
3:16As also in all his epistles, speaking in them of these things; in which are some things hard to be understood, which they that are unlearned and unstable wrest, as they do also the other scriptures, unto their own destruction. 17Ye therefore, beloved, seeing ye know these things before, beware lest ye also, being led away with the error of the wicked, fall from your own stedfastness. 18But grow in grace, and in the knowledge of our Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ. To him be glory both now and for ever. Amen.

          

        

        
      


    

    

    

      
      

        
Notes

        
    
          
          
  1. 
            Greek: the NTVMR diplomatic transcription, line by line as on the papyrus. Square brackets enclose letters that are lost or illegible and were supplied by the transcribers; a dot under a letter marks it as uncertain; [...] marks a gap. Nomina sacra and numerals keep the scribe's overline. Spelling is the scribe's (no accents or breathings). Words broken at a line end stay broken as on the papyrus. Corrections are not shown: the text is the first hand.
          
    2. 
          
          
  2. 
            English: the King James Version (1611), whole verses, for every verse that has text on the page. A verse that begins on one page and ends on the next is given in full on both. The KJV translates a later form of the Greek text, so its wording does not always match the papyrus; material differences (such as verses the papyrus does not have) are noted in the section.
