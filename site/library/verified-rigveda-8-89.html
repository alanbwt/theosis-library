Introduction

        
Rigveda 8.89 is a hymn of 7 verses addressed to Indra. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 8, the book of the Kaṇva and Aṅgiras poets. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 652r-652v. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1To Indra sing the lofty hymn, Maruts that slays the Vr̥itras best. Whereby the Holy Ones created for the God the light divine that ever wakes.
2Indra who quells the curse blew curses far away, and then in splendour came to us. Indra, refulgent with thy Marut host! the Gods strove eagerly to win thy love.
3Sing to your lofty Indra, sing, Maruts, a holy hymn of praise. Let Ṣatakratu, Vr̥itra-slayer, kill the foe with hundred-knotted thunderbolt.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
4Aim and fetch boldly forth, O thou whose heart is bold: great glory will be thine thereby. In rapid torrent let the mother waters spread. Slay Vr̥itra, win the light of heaven.
5When thou, unequalled Maghavan, wast born to smite the Vr̥itras dead, Thou spreadest out the spacious earth and didst support and prop the heavens.
6There was the sacrifice produced for thee, the laud, and song of joy, Thou in thy might surpassest all, all that now is and yet shall be.
7Raw kine thou filledst with ripe milk. Thou madest Sûrya rise to heaven. Heat him as milk is heated with pure Sâma hymns, great joy to him who loves the song.
