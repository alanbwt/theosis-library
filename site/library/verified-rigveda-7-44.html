Introduction

        
Rigveda 7.44 is a hymn of 5 verses addressed to Dadhikra. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 7, the family book of Vasiṣṭha. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 523r-523v. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1I CALL on Dadhikrâs, the first, to give you aid, the Aṣvins, Bhaga, Dawn, and Agni kindled well, Indra, and Vishṇu, Pûshan, Brahmaṇaspati, Âdityas, Heaven and Earth, the Waters, and the Light.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
2When, rising, to the sacrifice we hasten, awaking Dadhikrâs with adorations. Seating on sacred grass the Goddess Iḷâ. let us invoke the sage swift-hearing Aṣvins.
3While I am thus arousing Dadhikrâvan I speak to Agni, Earth, and Dawn, and Sûrya, The red, the brown of Varuṇa ever mindful: may they ward off from us all grief and trouble.
4Foremost is Dadhikrâvan, vigorous courser; in forefront of the cars, his way he knoweth, Closely allied with Sûrya and with Morning, Âdityas, and Angirases, and Vasus.
5May Dadhikrâs prepare the way we travel that we may pass along the path of Order. May Agni hear us, and the Heavenly Army: hear us all Mighty Ones whom none deceiveth.
