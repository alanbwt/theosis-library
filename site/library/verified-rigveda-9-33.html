Introduction

        
Rigveda 9.33 is a hymn of 6 verses addressed to Soma. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 9, the hymns to Soma Pavamāna. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 680r. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1LIKE waves of waters, skilled in song the juices of the Soma speed Onward, as buffaloes to woods.
2With stream of sacrifice the brown bright drops have flowed with strength in store Of kine into the wooden vats.
3To Indra, Vâyu, Varuṇa, to Vishṇu, and the Maruts, flow The drops of Soma juice effused.
4Three several words are uttered: kine are lowing, cows who give their milk: The Tawny-hued goes bellowing on.
5The young and sacred mothers of the holy rite have uttered praise: They decorate the Child of Heaven.
6From every side, O Soma, for our profit, pour thou forth four seas Filled full of riches thousandfold.
