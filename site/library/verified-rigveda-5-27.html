Introduction

        
Rigveda 5.27 is a hymn of 6 verses addressed to Agni. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 5, the family book of the Atri clan. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 337v-338v. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1THE Godlike hero, famousest of nobles, hath granted me two oxen with a wagon. Trivr̥ishan's son Tryaruṇa hath distinguished himself, Vaiṣvânara Agni! with ten thousands.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
2Protect Tryaruṇa, as thou art waxing strong and art highly praised, Vaiṣvânara Agni! Who granteth me a hundred kine and twenty, and two bay horses, good at draught, and harnessed.
3So Trasadasyu served thee, God Most Youthful, craving thy favour for the ninth time, Agni; Tryaruṇa who with attentive spirit accepteth many a song from me the mighty.
4He who declares his wish to me, to Aṣvamedha, to the Prince, Pays him who with his verse seeks gain, gives power to him who keeps the Law.
5From whom a hundred oxen, all of speckled hue, delight my heart, The gifts of Aṣvamedha, like thrice-mingled draughts of Soma juice.
