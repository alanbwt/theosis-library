Introduction

        
Rigveda 7.74 is a hymn of 6 verses addressed to Asvins. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 7, the family book of Vasiṣṭha. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 539v-540r. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1THESE morning sacrifices call you, Aṣvins, at the break of day. For help have I invoked you rich in power and might: for, house by house ye visit all.
2O Heroes, ye bestow wonderful nourishment. send it to him whose songs are sweet Accordant, both of you, drive your car down to us, and drink the savoury Soma juice.
3Approach ye and be near to us. drink, O ye Aṣvins, of the meath. Draw forth the milk, ye Mighty, rich in genuine wealth: injure us not, and come to us.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
4The horses that convey you in their rapid flight down to the worshipper's abode, With these your speedy coursers, Heroes, Aṣvins, come, ye Gods, come well-inclined to us.
5Yea, verily, our princes seek the Aṣvins in pursuit of food. These shall give lasting glory to our liberal lords, and, both Nâsatyas, shelter us.
6Those who have led the way, like cars, offending none, those who are guardians of the men- Also through their own might the heroes have grown strong, and dwell in safe and happy homes.
