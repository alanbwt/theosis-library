Introduction

        
Rigveda 4.35 is a hymn of 9 verses addressed to the Ribhus. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 4, the family book of Vāmadeva. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 296r-297r. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1Come hither, O ye Sons of Strength, ye R̥ibhus; stay not afar, ye Children of Sudhanvan. At this libation is your gift of treasure. Let gladdening draughts approach you after Indra's.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
2Hither is come the R̥ibhus' gift of riches; here was the drinking of the well-pressed Soma, Since by dexterity and skill as craftsmen ye made the single chalice to be fourfold
3Ye made fourfold the chalice that was single: ye spake these words and said, O Friend, assist us; Then, Vâjas! gained the path of life eternal, deft-handed R̥ibhus, to the Gods' assembly.
4Out of what substance was that chalice fashioned which ye made fourfold by your art and wisdom? Now for the gladdening draught press out the liquor, and drink, O R̥ibhus, of die meath of Soma.
5Ye with your cunning made your Parents youthful; the cup, for Gods to drink, ye formed with cunning; With cunning, R̥ibhus, rich in treasure, fashioned the two swift Tawny Steeds who carry Indra.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
6Whoso pours out for you, when days are closing, the sharp libation for your joy, O Vâjas, For him, O mighty R̥ibhus, ye, rejoicing, have fashioned wealth with plenteous store of heroes.
7Lord of Bay Steeds, at dawn thejuice thou drankest: thine, only thine, is the noonday libation. Now drink thou with the wealth-bestowing R̥ibhus, whom for their skill thou madest friends, O Indra.
8Ye, whom your artist skill hath raised to Godhead have sat you down above in heaven like falcons. So give us riches, Children of Sudhanvan, O Sons of Strength; ye have become immortal.
9The third libation, that bestoweth treasure, which ye have won by skill, ye dexterous-handed,– This drink hath been effused for you, O R̥ibhus . drink it with high delight, with joy like Indra's.
