Introduction

        
Rigveda 8.55 is a hymn of 5 verses addressed to thanksgiving. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 8, the book of the Kaṇva and Aṅgiras poets. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 628r-628v. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

Manuscript

            
            
            
            
Where on the image
Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41, fol. 628r (numbered 50r by the scribe) (OPenn image 628, lower leaf). Checked on the image: the Rigveda text on this page begins in RV 8.54.3 ‹naḥ vasavaḥ rudrāḥ avase›. The next page with Rigveda text, fol. 628v (numbered 50v by the scribe), begins in RV 8.55.2 ‹ukṣaṇaḥ divi tāraḥ na›. Verse 8.55.1 ends on this page.

          

          

          

            
Vedic Sanskrit · Aufrecht (accented)

                    
1bhūrīd indra̍sya vī̱rya1̱̍ṁ vy akhya̍m a̱bhy āya̍ti | rādha̍s te dasyave vṛka ||

1bhūri | it | indrasya | vīryam | vi | akhyam | abhi | ā | ayati | rādhaḥ | te | dasyave | vṛka

            

            
          


          

            
English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1GREAT, verily, is Indra's might. I have beheld, and hither comes Thy bounty, Dasyave-vr̥ika!

Vedic Sanskrit · Aufrecht (accented)

3śa̱taṁ ve̱ṇūñ cha̱taṁ śuna̍ḥ śa̱taṁ carmā̍ṇi mlā̱tāni̍ | śa̱tam me̍ balbajastu̱kā aru̍ṣīṇā̱ṁ catu̍ḥśatam ||
4su̱de̱vāḥ stha̍ kāṇvāyanā̱ vayo̍-vayo vica̱ranta̍ḥ | aśvā̍so̱ na ca̍ṅkramata ||
5ād it sā̱ptasya̍ carkira̱nn ānū̍nasya̱ mahi̱ śrava̍ḥ | śyāvī̍r atidhva̱san pa̱thaś cakṣu̍ṣā ca̱na sa̱ṁnaśe̍ ||

5āt | it | sāptasya | carkiran | na | anūnasya | mahi | śravaḥ | śyāvīḥ | ati-dhvasan | pathaḥ | cakṣuṣā | cana | sam-naśe

            

            
          


          

            
English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
2A hundred oxen white of hue are shining like the stars in heaven, So tall, they seem to prop the sky.
3Bamboos a hundred, a hundred dogs, a hundred skins of beasts well-tanned, A hundred tufts of Balbaja, four hundred red-hued mares are mine.
4Blest by the Gods, Kinvayanas! be ye who spread through life on life: Like horses have ye stridden forth.
5Then men extolled the team of seven not yet full-grown, its fame is great. The dark mares rushed along the paths, so that no eye could follow them.
