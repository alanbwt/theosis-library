Introduction

        
Rigveda 1.9 is a hymn of 10 verses addressed to Indra. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 1, a collection of hymns by many families of poets. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 5r-5v. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1COME, Indra, and delight thee with the juice at all the Soma feasts, Protector, mighty in thy strength.
2To Indra pour ye forth thejuice, the active gladdening juice to him Ile gladdening, omnific God.
3O Lord of all men, fair of cheek, rejoice thee in the gladdening lauds, Present at these drink-offerings.
4Songs have outpoured themselves to thee, Indra, the strong, the guardian Lord, And raised themselves unsatisfied.
5Send to us bounty manifold, O Indra, worthy of' our wish, For power supreme is only thine.
6O Indra, stimulate thereto us emulously fain for wealth, And glorious, O most splendid One.
7Give, Indra, wide and lofty fame, wealthy in cattle and in strength, Lasting our life-time, failing not.
8Grant us high fame, O Indra, grant riches bestowing thousands, those Fair fruits of earth borne home in wains.

          

        

        
        


          

            §9-10 · RV 1.9.9-10 (aṣṭaka 1, adhyāya 1, varga 18); Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 fol. 5v
            

              
              
              
            

          


          
          

            
Manuscript

            
            
            
            
            
            
Where on the image
Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41, fol. 5v (OPenn image 7, upper leaf). Checked on the image: the Rigveda text on this page begins in RV 1.9.9 ‹-ṛgmiyam homa gantāram ūtaye›. The next page with Rigveda text, fol. 6r, begins in RV 1.10.7 ‹-gavām apa vrajam vṛdhi›. Verses 1.9.9-10 end on this page; 1.9.9 begins on fol. 5r.

10su̱te-su̍te̱ nyo̍kase bṛ̱had bṛ̍ha̱ta ed a̱riḥ | indrā̍ya śū̱ṣam a̍rcati ||

10sute--sute | ni-okase | bṛhat | bṛhate | ā | it | ariḥ | indrāya | śūṣam | arcati

            

            
          


          

            
English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
9Praising with songs the praise-worthy who cometh to our aid, we call Indra, the Treasure-Lord of wealth.
10To lofty Indra, dweller by each libation, the pious man Sings forth aloud a strengthening hymn.
