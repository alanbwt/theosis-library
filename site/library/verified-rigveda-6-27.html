Introduction

        
Rigveda 6.27 is a hymn of 8 verses addressed to Indra. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 6, the family book of Bharadvāja. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 438r-439r. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1WHAT deed hath Indra done in the wild transport, in quaffing or in friendship with, the Soma? What joys have men of ancient times or recent obtained within the chamber of libation?
2In its wild joy Indra hath proved him faithful, faithful in quaffing, faithful in its friendship. His truth is the delight that in this chamber the men of old and recent times have tasted.
3All thy vast power, O Maghavan, we know not, know not the riches of thy full abundance. No one hath seen that might of thine, productive of bounty every day renewed, O Indra.
4This one great power of thine our eyes have witnessed, wherewith thou slewest Varaṣikha's children, When by the force of thy descending thunder, at the mere sound, their boldest was demolished.
5In aid of Abhyâvartin Châyamâna, Indra destroyed the seed of Varaṣikha. At Hariyûpîyâ he smote the vanguard of the Vr̥ichîvans, and the rear fled frighted.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
6Three thousand, mailed, in quest of fame, together, on the Yavyâvatî, O much-sought Indra, Vr̥ichîvan's sons, falling before the arrow, like bursting vessels went to their destruction.
7He, whose two red Steers, seeking goodly pasture, plying their tongues move on 'twixt earth and heaven, Gave Turvaṣa to Sr̥injaya, and, to aid him, gave the Vr̥ichîvans up to Daivavata.
8Two wagon-teams, with damsels, twenty oxen, O Agni, Abhyâvartin Châyamâna, The liberal Sovran, giveth me. This guerdon of Pr̥ithu's seed is hard to win from others.
