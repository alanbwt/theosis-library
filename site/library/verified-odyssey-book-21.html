Introduction

        
The Odyssey, composed around 700 BCE and attributed to Homer, tells of Odysseus' ten-year journey home from Troy and his return to Ithaca. Book 21 (434 lines): The bow.
The page photographs are from Florence, Biblioteca Medicea Laurenziana, Plut. 32.24 (parchment, 10th century, 234 folios; the oldest manuscript of the Odyssey with published images), in the Biblioteca Medicea Laurenziana's Teca Digitale. This book is on fol. 205v-214r; each section below is one page, with its first and last line as read on the photograph. Many pages carry interlinear glosses and a few marginal notes in later hands.
The Greek is A. T. Murray's Loeb text (1919) via Perseus, not a transcription of this manuscript, whose wording differs in places. The English is Murray's prose translation (1919), divided every five lines; at a page break a five-line block goes with the page holding most of it.
Where the scribe left out a line, wrote one in the margin, changed the order or added a line not in Murray's text, the section's note on the photograph says so; the Greek and English shown are Murray's full text for the page's range.

1But the goddess, flashing-eyed Athena, put it into the heart of the daughter of Icarius, wise Penelope, to set before the wooers in the halls of Odysseus the bow and the gray iron, to be a contest and the beginning of death. 5She climbed the high stairway to her chamber, and took the bent key in her strong hand—a goodly key of bronze, and on it was a handle of ivory. And she went her way with her handmaidens to a store-room, far remote, where lay the treasures of her lord, 10bronze and gold and iron, wrought with toil. And there lay the back-bent bow and the quiver that held the arrows, and many arrows were in it, fraught with groanings—gifts which a friend of Odysseus had given him when he met him once in Lacedaemon, even Iphitus, son of Eurytus, a man like unto the immortals. 15They two had met one another in Messene in the house of wise Ortilochus. Odysseus verily had come to collect a debt which the whole people owed him, for the men of Messene had lifted from Ithaca in their benched ships three hundred sheep and the shepherds with them.
