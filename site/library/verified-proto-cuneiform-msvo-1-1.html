Introduction

        
This clay tablet (Ashm 1926-0561, Ashmolean Museum, Oxford, UK) is a proto-cuneiform document of the Uruk III period, about 3200-3000 BCE, from Jemdet Nasr (CDLI catalogues the provenience as uncertain, attributed to Jemdet Nasr). It is a survey of fields in barley, fields assigned to laborers and irrigated fields belonging to officials of the settlement NI.RU (Jemdet Nasr): the chief exchange agent, the equid herder, the chief justice, the wife of the lord and the priest of NI.RU, with totals on the reverse.
Proto-cuneiform is the earliest stage of cuneiform writing, used for bookkeeping in southern Mesopotamia. It records numbers in several counting systems (shown in the transliteration as signs N01, N14, N34 and so on) together with signs for goods, offices and places. It does not yet write full sentences, so the English is an interpretation of the entries rather than a translation of connected language.
The sections follow the faces and columns of the tablet. The transliteration and the English are CDLI's, line by line with the same line numbers.

Notes

        
    
          
          
  1. 
            Transliteration: CDLI ATF (proto-cuneiform). Numerals are written as counts of numeral signs, e.g. 4(N14) = four N14 signs; ~a, ~b mark sign variants; [ ] broken, # damaged; sz is shown as š.
          
    2. 
          
          
  2. 
            English: CDLI's line-by-line translation (#tr.en lines of the ATF).
          
    3. 
          
          
  3. 
            CDLI notes: reverse, column 1, line 1: calculation: total of all “fields in barley”; reverse, column 1, line 2: calculation: total of all “fields of laborers”; reverse, column 1, line 3: calculation: total of all “irrigated fields”; reverse, column 2, line 1: calculation in bur3: 110(+) + 12(+) + 205(+) = 334 bur3 3 iku (only 7 bur3 missing)
