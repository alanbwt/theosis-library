Introduction

        
Rigveda 7.22 is a hymn of 9 verses addressed to Indra. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 7, the family book of Vasiṣṭha. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 507v-508v. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1DRINK Soma, Lord of Bays, and let it cheer thee: Indra, the stone, like a well guided courser, Directed by the presser's arms hath pressed it.
2So let the draught of joy, thy dear companion, by which, O Lord of Bays, thou slayest foemen, Delight thee, Indra, Lord of princely treasures.
3Mark closely, Maghavan, the words I utter, this eulogy recited by Vasishṭha: Accept the prayers I offer at thy banquet.
4Hear thou the call of the juice-drinking press-stone: hear thou the Brahman's hymn who sings and lauds thee. Take to thine inmost self these adorations.
5I know and ne'er forget the hymns and praises of thee, the Conqueror, and thy strength immortal. Thy name I ever utter. Self-Refulgent
6Among mankind many are thy libations, and many a time the pious sageinvokes thee. O Maghavan, be not long distant from us.
7All these libations are for thee, O Hero: to thee I offer these my prayers. that strengthen. Ever, in every place, must men invoke thee.
8Never do men attain, O Wonder-Worker, thy greatness, Mighty One, who must be lauded, Nor, Indra, thine heroic power and bounty.

Manuscript

            
            
            
            
            
            
Where on the image
Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41, fol. 508v (numbered 29v by the scribe) (OPenn image 509, upper leaf). Checked on the image: the Rigveda text on this page begins in RV 7.22.9 ‹ye ca nūtnāḥ indra›. English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
9Among all R̥ishis, Indra, old and recent, who have engendered hymns as sacred singers, Even with us be thine auspicious friendships. Ye Gods, preserve us evermore with blessings.
