Introduction

        
John 20 from Codex Washingtonianus (the Freer Gospels, Gregory-Aland 032, Freer Gallery of Art F1906.274), a parchment codex of the four Gospels written in Egypt in the late 4th or early 5th century and bought by Charles Lang Freer from the dealer Ali Arabi at Giza in 1906. It is one of the oldest nearly complete Gospel books in existence. The Gospels stand in the so-called Western order, Matthew, John, Luke, Mark, and the text is famously block-mixed: the copyist drew on several exemplars of different textual character, so the text type changes in blocks within a single Gospel. After Mark 16:14 the codex carries the Freer Logion, a saying found in no other manuscript. Two passages are lost to damage: Mark 15:13-37 and John 14:26-16:6.

It gives the letters as the first scribe wrote them: no accents or breathings, the scribe's own spelling, and the sacred names in their contracted form (θς for θεος, ις for ιησους, κς for κυριος; the overline is not reproduced). A stroke ¯ after a letter (for example μενω¯, το¯) is the scribe's abbreviation for a final ν, kept as the transcription gives it. Corrections by later hands are not shown. Letters in square brackets are not legible on the page and were restored by the transcribers, a dot under a letter marks an uncertain reading, and [...] marks text lost to damage. Beneath each section, for comparison, is Tischendorf's 8th critical edition (1869). Where the two differ, the transcription is what the manuscript says.
