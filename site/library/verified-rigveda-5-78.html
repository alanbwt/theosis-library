Introduction

        
Rigveda 5.78 is a hymn of 9 verses addressed to Asvins. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 5, the family book of the Atri clan. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 392v-393v. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1YE Aṣvins, hither come to us: Nâsatyas, be not disinclined. Fly hither like two swans unto the juice we shed.
2O Aṣvins, like a pair of deer, like two wild cattle to the mead: Fly hither like two swans unto the juice we shed.
3O Aṣvins rich in gifts, accept our sacrifice to prosper it: Fly hither like two swans unto the juice we shed.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
4As Atri when descending to the cavern called on you loudly like a wailing woman. Ye came to him, O Aṣvins, with the freshest and most auspicious fleetness of a falcon.
5Tree, part asunder like the side of her who bringeth forth a child. Ye Aṣvins, listen to my call: loose Saptavadhri from his bonds.
6For Saptavadhri, for the seer affrighted when he wept and wafled, Ye, Aṣvins, with your magic powers rent up the tree and shattered it.
7Like as the wind on every side ruffles a pool of lotuses, So stir in thee the babe unborn, so may the ten-month babe descend.
