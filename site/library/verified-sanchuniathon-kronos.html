Introduction

        
Philo of Byblos, a Phoenician grammarian writing in Greek around 100 AD, claimed to have translated the Phoenician History of Sanchuniathon of Berytus, a priestly writer said to have lived before the Trojan War, who drew on the city records and the temple inscriptions of Phoenicia and on the writings of Taautus (Thoth). Philo's work survives only in the long quotations that Eusebius of Caesarea copied into book 1 of his Preparation for the Gospel (c. 313 AD), taken partly through the philosopher Porphyry. It is the fullest account of Phoenician myth in Greek, and since the discovery of the Ugaritic tablets (14th-13th c. BCE) several of its gods and stories, such as El, Dagon, the succession of divine kings and the castration of the sky god, are known to go back to Bronze Age Canaan.



The manuscript shown is Paris, Bibliothèque nationale de France, grec 451, written in 914 AD by the scribe Baanes for Arethas, archbishop of Caesarea in Cappadocia; it is the oldest manuscript of books 1-5 of the Preparation. The passage runs from fol. 204v to fol. 213v. Each section is one of Dindorf's numbered sections and points at the page(s) that carry it; the page breaks were read on the images and are recorded in each note.



This entry covers sections 1.10.15 to 1.10.30, on fols. 208v to 210r.

English · E. H. Gifford (1903)

                    
10.15'And from them is born Epigeius or Autochthon, whom they afterwards called Uranus; so that from him they named the element above us Uranus because of the excellence of its beauty. And he has a sister born of the aforesaid parents, who was called Gé (earth), and from her, he says, because of her beauty, they called the earth by the same name. And their father, the Most High, died in an encounter with wild beasts, and was deified, and his children offered to him libations and sacrifices.

10.18'To Kronos are born children, Persephone and Athena. The former died a virgin: but by the advice of Athena and Hermes Kronos made a sickle and a spear of iron. Then Hermes talked magical words to the allies of Kronos, and inspired them with a desire of fighting against Uranus on behalf of Gé. And thus Kronos engaged in war, and drove Uranus from his government, and succeeded to the kingdom. Also there was taken in the battle the beloved concubine of Uranus, being great with child, whom Kronos gave in marriage to Dagon.

10.20'Soon after this he became suspicious of his own brother Atlas, and, with the advice of Hermes, threw him into a deep pit and buried him. And the allies of Elus, who is Kronos, were surnamed Eloim, as these same, who were surnamed after Kronos, would have been called Kronii.
