Introduction

        
Rigveda 6.61 is a hymn of 14 verses addressed to Sarasvati. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 6, the family book of Bharadvāja. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 478v-479v. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1To Vadhryaṣva when. be worshipped her with gifts she gave fierce Divodâsa, canceller of debts. Consumer of the churlish niggard, one and all, thine, O Sarasvatî, are these effectual boons.
2She with her might, like one who digs for lotus-stems, hath burst with her strong waves the ridges of the hills. Let us invite with songs and holy hymns for help Sarasvatî who slayeth the Pârâvatas.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
3Thou castest down, Sarasvatî, those who scorned the Gods, the brood of every Br̥isaya skilled in magic arts. Thou hast discovered rivers for the tribes of men, and, rich in wealth! made poison flow away from them.
4May the divine Sarasvatî, rich in her wealth, protect us well, Furthering all our thoughts with might
5Whoso, divine Sarasvatî, invokes thee where the prize is set, Like Indra when he smites the foe.
6Aid us, divine Sarasvatî, thou who art strong in wealth and power Like Pûshan, give us opulence.
7Yea, this divine Sarasvatî, terrible with her golden path, Foe-slayer, claims our eulogy.
8Whose limitless unbroken flood, swift-moving with a rapid rush, Comes onward with tempestuous roar.
9She hath spread us beyond all foes, beyond her Sisters, Holy One, As Sûrya spreadeth out the days.
10Yea, she most dear amid dear stream, Seven-sistered, graciously inclined, Sarasvatî hath earned our praise.

14sarasvati | abhi | naḥ | neṣi | vasyaḥ | mā | apa | spharīḥ | payasā | mā | naḥ | ā | dhak | juṣasva | naḥ | sakhyā | veśyā | ca | mā | tvat | kṣetrāṇi | araṇāni | ganma

            

            
          


          

            
English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
11Guard us from hate Sarasvatî, she who hath filled the realms of earth, And that wide tract, the firmament!
12Seven-sistered, sprung from threefold source, the Five Tribes' prosperer, she must be Invoked in every deed of might.
13Marked out by majesty among the Mighty Ones, in glory swifter than the other rapid Streams, Created vast for victory like a chariot, Sarasvatî must be extolled by every sage.
14Guide us, Sarasvatî, to glorious treasure: refuse us not thy milk, nor spurn us from thee. Gladly accept our friendship and obedience: let us not go from thee to distant countries.
