Introduction

        
Rigveda 1.158 is a hymn of 6 verses addressed to Asvins. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 1, a collection of hymns by many families of poets. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 120r-120v. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1YE Vasus Twain, ye Rudras full of counsel, grant us, Strong Strengtheners, when ye stand beside us, What wealth Aucathya craves of you, great Helpers when ye come forward with no niggard succour.
2Who may give you aught, Vasus, for your favour, for what, at the Cow's place, ye grant through worship? Wake for us understanding full of riches, come with a heart that will fulfil our longing.
3As erst for Tugra's son your car, sea-crossing, strong, was equipped and set amid the waters, So may I gain your shelter and protection as with winged course a hero seeks his army.
4May this my praise preserve Ucathya's offspring: let not these Twain who fly with wings exhaust me. Let not the wood ten times up-piled consume me, when fixed for you it bites the ground it stands on.
5The most maternal streams, wherein the Dâsas cast me securely bound, have not devoured me. When Traitana would cleave my head asunder, the Dâsa wounded his own breast and shoulders.

          

        

        
        


          

            §6 · RV 1.158.6 (aṣṭaka 2, adhyāya 3, varga 1); Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 fol. 120v
            

              
              
              
            

          


          
          

            
Manuscript

            
            
            
            
            
            
Where on the image
Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41, fol. 120v (numbered 1v by the scribe) (OPenn image 122, upper leaf). Checked on the image: the Rigveda text on this page begins in RV 1.158.6 ‹māmateyaḥ jujurvān daśame yuge›. The next page with Rigveda text, fol. 121r (numbered 2r by the scribe), begins in RV 1.159.5 ‹rayim dhattam vasu-mantam śata-gvinam›. Verse 1.158.6 ends on this page; 1.158.6 begins on fol. 120r (numbered 1r by the scribe).

          

          

          

            
Vedic Sanskrit · Aufrecht (accented)

                    
6dī̱rghata̍mā māmate̱yo ju̍ju̱rvān da̍śa̱me yu̱ge | a̱pām artha̍ṁ ya̱tīnā̍m bra̱hmā bha̍vati̱ sāra̍thiḥ ||

            
            

              Compare: Padapāṭha (word-by-word text written in the manuscript; Sansknet e-text via GRETIL)
              
        
6dīrgha-tamāḥ | māmateyaḥ | jujurvān | daśame | yuge | apām | artham | yatīnām | brahmā | bhavati | sārathiḥ

            

            
          


          

            
English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
6Dîrghatamas the son of Mamatâ hath come to length of days in the tenth age of human kind. He is the Brahman of the waters as they strive to reach their end and aim: their charioteer is he.
