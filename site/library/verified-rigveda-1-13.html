Introduction

        
Rigveda 1.13 is a hymn of 12 verses addressed to Apri hymn. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 1, a collection of hymns by many families of poets. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 7r-7v. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

            §1-3 · RV 1.13.1-3 (aṣṭaka 1, adhyāya 1, varga 24); Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 fol. 7r
            

              
              
              
            

          


          
          

            
Manuscript

            
            
            
            
Where on the image
Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41, fol. 7r (OPenn image 8, lower leaf). Checked on the image: the Rigveda text on this page begins in RV 1.12.5 ‹-dīdi-vaḥ prati sma riṣataḥ›. The next page with Rigveda text, fol. 7v, begins in RV 1.13.4 ‹devān iḷitaḥ ā vaha›. Verses 1.13.1-3 end on this page.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1AGNI, well-kindled, bring the Gods for him who offers holy gifts. Worship them, Purifier, Priest.
2Son of Thyself, present, O Sage, our sacrifice to the Gods today. Sweet to the taste, that they may feast.
3Dear Narâṣansa, sweet of tongue, the giver of oblations, I Invoke to this our sacrifice.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
4Agni, on thy most easy car, glorified, hither bring the Gods: Manu appointed thee as Priest.
5Strew, O ye wise, the sacred grass that drips with oil, in order due, Where the Immortal is beheld.
6Thrown open be the Doors Divine, unfailing, that assist the rite, For sacrifice this day and now.
7I call the lovely Night and Dawn to seat them on the holy grass At this our solemn sacrifice.
8The two Invokers I invite, the wise, divine and sweet of tongue, To celebrate this our sacrifice.
9Iḷâ, Sarasvatî, Mahi, three Goddesses who bring delight, Be seated, peaceful, on the grass.
10Tvashṭar I call, the earliest born, the wearer of all forms at will: May he be ours and ours alone.
11God, Sovran of the Wood, present this our oblation to the Gods, And let the giver be renowned.
12With Svâhâ pay the sacrifice to Indra in the offerer's house: Thither I call the Deities.
