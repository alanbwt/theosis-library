Introduction

        
Rigveda 5.26 is a hymn of 9 verses addressed to Agni. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 5, the family book of the Atri clan. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 337r-337v. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1O AGNI, Holy and Divine, with splendour and thy pleasant tongue Bring hither and adore the Gods.
2We pray thee, thou who droppest oil, bright-rayed! who lookest on the Sun, Bring the Gods hither to the feast.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
3We have enkindled thee, O Sage, bright caller of the Gods to feast. O Agni, great in Sacrifice.
4O Agni, come with all the Gods, come to our sacrificial gift: We choose thee as Invoking Priest.
5Bring, Agni, to the worshipper who pours the juice, heroic strength: Sit with the Gods upon the grass.
6Victor of thousands, Agni, thou, enkindled, cherishest the laws, Laud-worthy, envoy of the Gods.
7Set Agni Jâtavedas down, the bearer of our sacred gifts, MostYouthful, God and Minister.
8Duly proceed our sacrifice, comprising all the Gods, to-day: Strew holy grass to be their seat.
9So may the Maruts sit thereon, the Aṣvins, Mitra, Varuṇa: The Gods with all their company.
