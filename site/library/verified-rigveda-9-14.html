Introduction

        
Rigveda 9.14 is a hymn of 8 verses addressed to Soma. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 9, the hymns to Soma Pavamāna. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 673v-674r. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

Manuscript

            
            
            
            
Where on the image
Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41, fol. 673v (numbered 95v by the scribe) (OPenn image 674, upper leaf). Checked on the image: the Rigveda text on this page begins in RV 9.13.5 ‹-sahasriṇam rayim pavantām ā›. The next page with Rigveda text, fol. 674r (numbered 96r by the scribe), begins in RV 9.14.6 ‹ati śritī tiraścatā gavyā›. Verses 9.14.1-5 end on this page.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1REPOSING on the river's wave the Sage hath widely flowed around, Bearing the hymn which many love.
2When the Five kindred Companies, active in duty, with the song Establish him, the Powerful,
3Then in his juice whose strength is great, have all the Gods rejoiced themselves, When he hath clothed him in the milk.
4Freeing himself he flows away, leaving his body's severed limbs, And meets his own Companion here.
5He by the daughters of the priest, like a fair youth, hath been adorned, Making the milk, as 'twere, his robe.

          

        

        
        


          

            §6-8 · RV 9.14.6-8 (aṣṭaka 6, adhyāya 8, varga 4); Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 fol. 674r
            

              
              
              
            

          


          
          

            
Manuscript

            
            
            
            
Where on the image
Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41, fol. 674r (numbered 96r by the scribe) (OPenn image 674, lower leaf). Checked on the image: the Rigveda text on this page begins in RV 9.14.6 ‹ati śritī tiraścatā gavyā›. The next page with Rigveda text, fol. 674v (numbered 96v by the scribe), begins in RV 9.15.8 ‹iti tyam daśa kṣipaḥ›. Verses 9.14.6-8 end on this page.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
6O'er the fine fingers, through desire of milk, in winding course he goes, And utters voice which he hath found.
7The nimble fingers have approached, adorning him the Lord of Strength: They grasp the vigorous Courser's back.
8Comprising all the treasures that are in the heavens and on the earth, Come, Soma, as our faithful Friend.
