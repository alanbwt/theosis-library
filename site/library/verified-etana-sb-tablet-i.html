Introduction

        
The gods plan the city of Kish and seek a king; Ishtar and Enlil choose Etana.
The Legend of Etana tells of an early king of Kish, chosen by the gods, who has no son. An eagle that betrayed its friend the serpent is thrown into a pit; Etana rescues it, and the eagle carries him up towards heaven to find the plant of birth. The story is known from Old Babylonian, Middle Assyrian and Standard Babylonian versions; the end is lost. This page follows the line numbering of the electronic Babylonian Library edition.
How this page is built. Each section is a run of consecutive lines as written on one photographed clay tablet. For every line the eBL edition (https://www.ebl.lmu.de/corpus/L/1/9/SB/I) lists the manuscripts that carry it, with each tablet's own column and line numbers; the tablet chosen for each run is the photographed manuscript with the most signs preserved there. The original text is eBL's transliteration of that tablet's signs (not a modern composite text): square brackets mark broken signs, x an illegible sign, # a damaged sign, ? an uncertain reading, and words in {braces} are determinatives. Superscript numbers are the line numbers of the eBL edition, used in both the Akkadian and the English. The English is B. R. Foster's translation in the eBL edition (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), one line of English per line of the text; italics mark uncertain renderings, as in the source.
Finding a line on the photograph. Each section names the tablet, the face and column, and the tablet's own line numbers (a prime, as in 5', counts from the first preserved line of a broken column). On a tablet with six columns the obverse carries columns i-iii from left to right; the reverse, turned over from bottom to top, carries columns iv-vi from right to left. The photographs often show the obverse above the reverse with the edges around them; a fragment preserves only part of this layout.
This page shows 40 of the 40 lines of the eBL edition (100%), from 2 tablets in the British Museum: K.2606 (Nineveh), K.8563 (Nineveh).

Manuscript

            
            
            
            
Where on the image
British Museum K.2606: column i, the tablet's own lines 1 to 30. eBL's corpus edition (https://www.ebl.lmu.de/corpus/L/1/9/SB/I) attributes SB I 1-29 to this manuscript at these column and line numbers. Photograph checked: obverse, reverse and edges, ink number K.2606 on the edge; a column of about 29 lines on the obverse and a few lines on the reverse, consistent with eBL's attribution. The English translates eBL's composite text: words in square brackets are restored, partly from other manuscripts, and may not be legible on this tablet.

1They planned the city [...],

2[The gods] laid [its foundations],

3They planned the city [Kish],

4[The Anunna-gods] laid its foundations.

5The Igigi-gods founded its brickwork [...]

6“Let [a ma]n be their (the people’s) shepherd,

7“Let Etana be their master builder ...

8The great Anunna-gods, or[dainers of destinies],

9[Sa]t holding their counsel [concerning the land],

10Those who formed the four world regions, the right and [left] (banks of the rivers),

11By command of all of them, the Igigi-gods [ordained the holy day for] the peop[le].

12No [king] had they established [over the teeming peoples].

13At that time, [no headdress had been put together, nor crown],

14Nor yet scepter [had been set] with lapis,

15No [throne daises] whatsoever had been constructed.

16The seven gods barred the [gates] against the multitude,

18The Igigi-gods surrounded the city [with ramparts].
