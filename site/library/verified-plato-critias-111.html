Introduction

        
Stephanus page 111 of Plato's Critias (111a-111e): the fertility of ancient Attica and the soil carried off by floods.



The Critias is Plato's full account of Atlantis and of the Athens that fought it: the land and city of ancient Athens (109b-112e), then the island of Atlantis, Poseidon's kingdom beyond the Pillars of Heracles, its royal city, plain, army and laws (113b-120d), and the moral decline of its kings (120e-121c). Plato left the dialogue unfinished; it stops in mid-sentence as Zeus begins to speak, and the manuscript shown here stops at the same words.



The manuscript is Paris, Bibliothèque nationale de France, grec 1807, known to editors as Plato A. It was copied in Constantinople around 850-875 by the scribe of the so-called philosophical collection, and is the oldest surviving medieval manuscript of the Timaeus and Critias (tetralogies VIII-IX: Clitophon, Republic, Timaeus, Critias, Minos, Laws, Epinomis, Letters). Each page has two columns of 44 lines. The Timaeus fills fol. 114r-144v and the Critias fol. 145r-151r.



On the manuscript this page of text runs over fol. 146v col. b to fol. 147r col. a. Each section below is linked to the folio side(s) that carry it. The mapping comes from reading the first words of every column on the images and finding them in Burnet's text: fol. 146v col. b begins ‹-ρωπίαν ἔχοντας, ἐν ἀριστερᾷ δὲ πρὸς› (Crit. 110e); fol. 147r col. a begins ‹ἐκέκτητο, καὶ πολλὴν ἐν τοῖς ὄρεσιν› (Crit. 111c).



The Greek is John Burnet's critical text (Oxford, 1902), not a transcription of this manuscript; the scribe's spelling, accents and occasional readings differ from it. The English is R. G. Bury's Loeb translation (1929).
