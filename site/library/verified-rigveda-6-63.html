Introduction

        
Rigveda 6.63 is a hymn of 11 verses addressed to Asvins. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 6, the family book of Bharadvāja. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 482r-483r. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1WHERE hath the hymn with reverence, like an envoy, found both fair Gods to-day, invoked of many- Hymn that hath brought the two Nâsatyas hither? To this man's thought be ye, both Gods, most friendly.
2Come readily to this mine invocation, lauded with songs, that ye may drink the juices. Compass this house to keep it from the foeman, that none may force it, either near or distant.
3Juice in wide room hath been prepared to feast you: for you the grass is strewn, most soft to tread on. With lifted hands your servant hath adored you. Yearningfor you the press-stones shed the liquid.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
4Agni uplifts him at your sacrifices: forth goes the oblation dropping oil and glowing. Up stands the grateful-minded priest, elected, appointed to invoke the two Nâsatyas.
5Lords of great wealth! for glory, Sûrya's Daughter mounted your car that brings a hundred succours. Famed for your magic arts were ye, magicians! amid the race of Gods, ye dancing Heroes!
6Ye Twain, with these your glories fair to look on, brought, to win victory, rich gifts for Sûryâ. After you flew your birds, marvels of beauty: dear to our hearts! the song, well lauded, reached you.
7May your winged coursers, best to draw. Nâsatyas! convey you to the object of your wishes. Swift as the thought, your car hath been sent onward to food of many a sort and dainty viands.
8Lords of great wealth, manifold is your bounty: ye filled our cow with food that never faileth. Lovers of sweetness! yours are praise and singers, and poured libations which have sought your favour.
9Mine were two mares of Puraya, brown, swift-footed; a hundred with Sumîḍha, food with Peruk. Ṣâṇḍa gave ten gold-decked and well-trained horses, tame and obedient and of lofty stature.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
10Nâsatyas! Purupanthas offered hundreds, thousands of steeds to him who sang your praises, Gave, Heroes! to the singer Bharadvâja. Ye-Wonder-Workers, let the fiends be slaughtered.
11May I with princes share your bliss in freedom.
