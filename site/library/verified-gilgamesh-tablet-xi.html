Introduction

        
Uta-napishti tells how the god Ea warned him of the Flood, how he built a boat, survived the deluge, sent out birds, and was granted eternal life. Gilgamesh fails a test of sleep, obtains a plant of rejuvenation, loses it to a snake, and returns to Uruk.
The Standard Babylonian version of the epic, traditionally ascribed to the scholar Sin-leqi-unninni, was written on twelve tablets. Most surviving copies come from the libraries of Ashurbanipal at Nineveh (7th century BCE) and from Babylon and other Babylonian cities (7th-2nd century BCE). This page gives Tablet XI in the line numbering of the electronic Babylonian Library edition by A. R. George (2022 and later revisions).
How this page is built. Each section is a run of consecutive lines as written on one photographed clay tablet. For every line the eBL edition (https://www.ebl.lmu.de/corpus/L/1/4/SB/XI) lists the manuscripts that carry it, with each tablet's own column and line numbers; the tablet chosen for each run is the photographed copy of the work with the most signs preserved there. Commentaries and texts that only quote a line are never chosen. School and excerpt tablets are used only for lines that no photographed library copy preserves as well, and only where they carry the line as text; their sections say so. The original text is eBL's transliteration of that tablet's signs (not a modern composite text): square brackets mark broken signs, x an illegible sign, # a damaged sign, ? an uncertain reading, and words in {braces} are determinatives; the notes give a key to every sign used on this page, and eBL's layout codes are written out in words (for example '(line break)' or '(blank space)'). Superscript numbers are the line numbers of the eBL edition, used in both the Akkadian and the English. The English is A. R. George's translation in the eBL edition (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), one line of English per line of the poem; italics mark uncertain renderings, as in the source. Where the tablet is broken, the English still translates the whole line of eBL's composite text; each section's note names the lines that are broken on its tablet.
Finding a line on the photograph. Each section names the tablet, the face and column, and the tablet's own line numbers (a prime, as in 5', counts from the first preserved line of a broken column). On a tablet with six columns the obverse carries columns i-iii from left to right; the reverse, turned over from bottom to top, carries columns iv-vi from right to left. The photographs often show the obverse above the reverse with the edges around them; a fragment preserves only part of this layout.
This page shows 329 of the 329 lines of the eBL edition (100%), from 5 tablets in the British Museum: K.2252 (Nineveh), K.8517 (Nineveh), Sm.2131 (Nineveh), K.7752 (Nineveh), K.3375 (Nineveh).

English · A. R. George (eBL edition)

                    
5I was fully intent on doing battle with you,

6[but] in your presence my hand is stayed.

7How was it you attended the gods’ assembly, and found life?’

8Ūta-napišti spoke to him, to Gilgameš:

9‘I will disclose to you, Gilgameš, a secret matter,

10and I will tell you a mistery of the gods.

11The city of Šuruppak – a city you yourself know,

12the [city that] is situated on the [banks] of the Euphrates–

13that city was old and the gods were within it,

14(when) the great gods decided to cause the Deluge.

15Their father Anu took the oath,

16their counsellor, the hero Enlil;

17their chamberlain, Ninurta,

18their inspector of waterways, Ennugi

19With them the Prince Ea was under oath likewise,

20(but) repeated their words to a reed fence:

21“Reed fence, reed fence! Brick wall, brick wall!

22Listen, O reed fence! Pay heed, O brick wall!

23O man of Šuruppak, son of Ubār-Tutu,

24demolish the house, build a boat!

25Abandon riches and seek survival!

26Spurn property and save life!

27Put on board the boat the seed of all living creatures!

28The boat that you are going to build,

29her dimensions should all be matching:

30her breadth and length should be the same,

31cover her with a roof, like the Apsû”.

32I understood and spoke to Ea my master:

33“I hereby concur, my lord, with what you told me thus.

English · A. R. George (eBL edition)

                    
312for whom of my (kind) ran dry the blood of my heart?

313Not for myself did I establish a bounty,

314[for] the “Lion of the Earth” I have done a favour.

315Now for twenty leagues the tide has been rising!

316When I opened the channel I abandoned the tools:

317what thing would I find that was placed (to serve) for my landmark? Had I only turned away,

318and left the boat on the shore!'

319At twenty leagues they broke bread,

320at thirty leagues they pitched camp.

321They arrived in Uruk the Sheepfold.

322Gilgameš spoke to him, to Ur-šanābi the boatman:

323‘Go up, Ur-šanābi, on to the wall of Uruk and walk around,

324survey the foundation platform, inspect the brickwork!

325(See) if its brickwork is not kiln-fired brick,

326and if the Seven Sages did not lay its foundations!

327One šār is city, one šār date-grove, one šār is clay-pit, half a šār the temple of Ištar:

328three šār and a half (is) Uruk, (its) measurement.’
