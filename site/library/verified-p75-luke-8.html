Introduction

        
P75 is a papyrus codex of Luke and John copied in Egypt around 175-225, the oldest known copy of Luke and one of the oldest of John. It was bought by Martin Bodmer in the 1950s and published as Papyrus Bodmer XIV-XV; in 2006 it was given to the Vatican Library, where it is kept as Papyrus Hanna 1 (Mater Verbi). Its text is very close to that of Codex Vaticanus, written some 150 years later. Where Luke ends and John begins (folio 44r) both titles stand on the same page.



About 51 leaves survive, many damaged at the top and edges, especially in Luke 3-7. Lost: Luke 1:1-3:17, most of 3:23-4:33, 5:11-36, 6:5-10, 7:33-34 and 7:44-45 (and parts of 7:36-49), 17:16-19, 18:19-22:3; John 11:46-47, 12:1-2, 13:11-14:7 and everything after 15:10 except small fragments. Each section below is one page: the photograph, the NTVMR diplomatic transcription line by line, and the King James Version of every verse on the page. Where P75 lacks a verse or words that the KJV has (for example John 5:4 or the account of the adulteress, John 7:53-8:11), the section says so. Six damaged pages (folios 7v, 11r, 39v, 58r, 58v and 64v) are left out because their text could not be matched to the photographs with certainty.



This entry covers fols. 16r-18r (Luke 8:5-9:10), shown on the Vatican Library's photographs (frames 1A.8r to 1A.10r).

English · King James Version (1611)

                    
8:5A sower went out to sow his seed: and as he sowed, some fell by the way side; and it was trodden down, and the fowls of the air devoured it. 6And some fell upon a rock; and as soon as it was sprung up, it withered away, because it lacked moisture. 7And some fell among thorns; and the thorns sprang up with it, and choked it. 8And other fell on good ground, and sprang up, and bare fruit an hundredfold. And when he had said these things, he cried, He that hath ears to hear, let him hear. 9And his disciples asked him, saying, What might this parable be? 10And he said, Unto you it is given to know the mysteries of the kingdom of God: but to others in parables; that seeing they might not see, and hearing they might not understand. 11Now the parable is this: The seed is the word of God. 12Those by the way side are they that hear; then cometh the devil, and taketh away the word out of their hearts, lest they should believe and be saved. 13They on the rock are they, which, when they hear, receive the word with joy; and these have no root, which for a while believe, and in time of temptation fall away. 14And that which fell among thorns are they, which, when they have heard, go forth, and are choked with cares and riches and pleasures of this life, and bring no fruit to perfection. 15But that on the good ground are they, which in an honest and good heart, having heard the word, keep it, and bring forth fruit with patience. 16¶ No man, when he hath lighted a candle, covereth it with a vessel, or putteth it under a bed; but setteth it on a candlestick, that they which enter in may see the light.

And they being afrai d wondered, saying one to another, What manner of man is this! for he commandeth even the winds and water, and they obey him. 26¶ And they arrived at the country of the Gadarenes, which is over against Galilee. 27And when he went forth to land, there met him out of the city a certain man, which had devils long time, and ware no clothes, neither abode in any house, but in the tombs. 28When he saw Jesus, he cried out, and fell down before him, and with a loud voice said, What have I to do with thee, Jesus, thou Son of God most high? I beseech thee, torment me not.

Notes

        
    
          
          
  1. 
            Greek: the NTVMR diplomatic transcription, line by line as on the papyrus. Square brackets enclose letters that are lost or illegible and were supplied by the transcribers; a dot under a letter marks it as uncertain; [...] marks a gap. Nomina sacra and numerals keep the scribe's overline. Spelling is the scribe's (no accents or breathings). Words broken at a line end stay broken as on the papyrus. Corrections are not shown: the text is the first hand.
          
    2. 
          
          
  2. 
            English: the King James Version (1611), whole verses, for every verse that has text on the page. A verse that begins on one page and ends on the next is given in full on both. The KJV translates a later form of the Greek text, so its wording does not always match the papyrus; material differences (such as verses the papyrus does not have) are noted in the section.
