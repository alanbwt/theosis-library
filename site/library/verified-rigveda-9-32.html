Introduction

        
Rigveda 9.32 is a hymn of 6 verses addressed to Soma. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 9, the hymns to Soma Pavamāna. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 679v-680r. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

2ād ī̍ṁ tri̱tasya̱ yoṣa̍ṇo̱ hari̍ṁ hinva̱nty adri̍bhiḥ | indu̱m indrā̍ya pī̱taye̍ ||
3ād ī̍ṁ ha̱ṁso yathā̍ ga̱ṇaṁ viśva̍syāvīvaśan ma̱tim | atyo̱ na gobhi̍r ajyate ||

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1THE rapture-shedding Soma-drops, effused in our assembly, have Flowed forth to glorify our prince.
2Then Trita's Maidens onward urge the Tawny-coloured with the stones, Indu for Indra, for his drink.
3Now like a swan he maketh all the company sing each his hymn: He, like a steed, is bathed in milk.
4O Soma, viewing heaven and earth, thou runncst like a darting deer Set in the place of sacrifice.

Manuscript

            
            
            
            
            
            
Where on the image
Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41, fol. 680r (numbered 102r by the scribe) (OPenn image 680, lower leaf). Checked on the image: the Rigveda text on this page begins in RV 9.32.5 ‹yathā hitam›. 6a̱sme dhe̍hi dyu̱mad yaśo̍ ma̱ghava̍dbhyaś ca̱ mahya̍ṁ ca | sa̱nim me̱dhām u̱ta śrava̍ḥ ||

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
5The cows have sung with joy to him, even as a woman to her love He came as to a settled race.
6Bestow illustrious fame on us, both on our liberal lords and me, Glory, intelligence, and wealth.
