Introduction

        
Rigveda 8.80 is a hymn of 10 verses addressed to Indra. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 8, the book of the Kaṇva and Aṅgiras poets. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 648r-648v. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1O Ṣatakratu! truly I have made none else my Comforter. Indra; be gracious unto us.
2Thou who hast ever aided us kindly of old to win the spoil, As such, O Indra, favour us.
3What now? As prompter of the poor thou helpest him who sheds the juice. Wilt thou not, Indra, strengthen us?
4O Indra, help our chariot on, yea, Thunderer, though it lag behind: Give this my car the foremost place.
5Ho there! why sittest thou at case? Make thou my chariot to be first And bring the fame of victory near.
6Assist our car that seeks the prize. What can be easier for thee? So make thou us victorious.
7Indra, be firm: a fort art thou. To thine appointed place proceeds The auspicious hymn in season due.
8Let not our portion be disgrace. Broad is the course, the prize is set, The barriers are opened wide.
9This thing we wish. that thou mayst take thy fourth, thy sacrificial name. So art thou held to be our Lord.

Manuscript

            
            
            
            
            
            
Where on the image
Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41, fol. 648v (numbered 70v by the scribe) (OPenn image 649, upper leaf). Checked on the image: the Rigveda text on this page begins in RV 8.80.10 ‹-avīvṛdhat vaḥ amṛtāḥ amandīt›. English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
10Ekadyû hath exalted you, Immortals: both Goddesses and Gods hath he delighted. Bestow upon him bounty meet for praises. May he, enriched with prayer, come soon and early.
