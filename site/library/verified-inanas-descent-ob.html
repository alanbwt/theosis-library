Introduction

        
In Inana's Descent to the Netherworld the goddess Inana leaves her temples and goes down to the underworld, ruled by her sister Ereškigala. At each of its seven gates she must give up one of her garments and ornaments, and she arrives naked. Ereškigala kills her and her corpse is hung on a hook. Her minister Ninšubur goes to the gods for help; Enki makes two beings who revive her with the food and water of life. Inana may leave only if someone takes her place, and she hands over her husband Dumuzid, who is carried off to the underworld; at the end Dumuzid and his sister Ĝeštin-ana are to spend half of each year there in turn. The poem is known from Old Babylonian school tablets (about 1900-1700 BCE); a later Akkadian version is Ištar's Descent.
No single photographed tablet preserves the whole text. Each section below is one paragraph of the ETCSL translation, shown with the photographed tablet or tablets that carry those lines and their own transliterations.
