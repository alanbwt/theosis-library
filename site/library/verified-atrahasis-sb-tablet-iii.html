Introduction

        
The later version of the Flood episode.
Atrahasis ('Exceedingly Wise') is the Babylonian story of humanity from its creation to the Flood. The lesser gods rebel against their labour; humankind is created from clay and the flesh and blood of a slain god to do the work instead; when people multiply and their noise disturbs Enlil, he sends plague, drought and famine, and finally the Flood, which Atrahasis survives in a boat on the advice of Enki (Ea). The oldest near-complete copy was written at Sippar by the scribe Ipiq-Aya in the 17th century BCE. This page follows the line numbering of the electronic Babylonian Library edition.
How this page is built. Each section is a run of consecutive lines as written on one photographed clay tablet. For every line the eBL edition (https://www.ebl.lmu.de/corpus/L/1/1/SB/III) lists the manuscripts that carry it, with each tablet's own column and line numbers; the tablet chosen for each run is the photographed manuscript with the most signs preserved there. The original text is eBL's transliteration of that tablet's signs (not a modern composite text): square brackets mark broken signs, x an illegible sign, # a damaged sign, ? an uncertain reading, and words in {braces} are determinatives. Superscript numbers are the line numbers of the eBL edition, used in both the Akkadian and the English. The English is B. R. Foster's translation in the eBL edition (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), one line of English per line of the text; italics mark uncertain renderings, as in the source.
Finding a line on the photograph. Each section names the tablet, the face and column, and the tablet's own line numbers (a prime, as in 5', counts from the first preserved line of a broken column). On a tablet with six columns the obverse carries columns i-iii from left to right; the reverse, turned over from bottom to top, carries columns iv-vi from right to left. The photographs often show the obverse above the reverse with the edges around them; a fragment preserves only part of this layout.
This page shows 54 of the 60 lines of the eBL edition (90%), from 1 tablet in the British Museum: K.4539 (Nineveh).
Lines not shown. 6 of the 60 lines of the eBL edition have no witness here with an open photograph: 1 (only on Ist-B.116, IM.124649); 2 (only on Ist-B.116); 66-68 (no manuscript preserves it); 132 (no manuscript preserves it). Lines that survive only on tablets whose photographs are not openly licensed (for example in the Iraq Museum, Berlin, Istanbul, Ankara, Philadelphia or private collections), or only on tablets whose eBL photograph does not show the lines, are omitted.

Manuscript

            
            
            
            
            
            
            
            
            
            
Where on the image
British Museum K.4539: obverse, column ii, the tablet's own lines 1' to 19'. eBL's corpus edition (https://www.ebl.lmu.de/corpus/L/1/1/SB/III) attributes SB III 3-21 to this manuscript at these column and line numbers. Photograph checked: one face only, a fragment about 3.5 cm wide with about 9 lines; no legible ink number. Alone it cannot carry eBL's 54 lines (obverse column ii 37, reverse column iii 17), but with the three linked pieces (K.5902, K.7816+K.13863, K.17752) the set shows about 45 to 50 lines on both faces. eBL treats K.4539 + K.5902 + K.7816 + K.17752 as one manuscript and holds a separate photograph of each of these pieces; it does not say which piece carries which line, so all of these photographs are linked, and the lines are on one or more of them. The English translates eBL's composite text: words in square brackets are restored, partly from other manuscripts, and may not be legible on this tablet.
