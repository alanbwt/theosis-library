Introduction

        
Rigveda 4.37 is a hymn of 8 verses addressed to the Ribhus. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 4, the family book of Vāmadeva. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 298v-299r. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1COME to our sacrifice, Vâjas, R̥ibhukshans, Gods, by the paths which Gods are wont to travel, As ye, gay Gods, accept in splendid weather the sacrifice among these folk of Manus.
2May these rites please you in your heart and spirit; may the drops clothed in oil this day approach you. May the abundant juices bear you onward to power and strength, and, when imbibed, delight you.
3Your threefold going near is God-appointed, so praise is given you, Vâjas and R̥ibhukshans. So, Manus-like, mid younger folk I offer, to you who are aloft in heaven, the Soma.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
4Strong, with fair chains of gold and jaws of iron, ye have a splendid car and well-fed horses. Ye Sons of Strength, ye progeny of Indra, to you the best is offered to delight you.
5R̥ibhukshans! him, for handy wealth, the mightiest comrade in the fight, Him, Indra's equal, we invoke, most bounteous ever, rich in steeds.
6The mortal man whom, R̥ibhus, ye and Indra favour with your help, Must be successful, by his thoughts, at sacrifice and with the steed.
7O Vâjas and R̥ibhukshans, free for us the paths to sacrifice, Ye Princes, lauded, that we may press forward to each point of heaven.
8O Vâjas and R̥ibhukshans, ye Nâsatyas, Indra, bless this wealth, And, before other men's, the steed, that ample riches may be won.
