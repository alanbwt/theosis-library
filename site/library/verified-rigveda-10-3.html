Introduction

        
Rigveda 10.3 is a hymn of 7 verses addressed to Agni. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 10, the last and latest book. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 743v-744v. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1O KING, the potent and terrific envoy, kindled for strength, is manifest in beauty. He shines, all-knowing, with his lofty splendour: chasing black Night he comes with white-rayed Morning.
2Having o'ercome the glimmering Black with beauty, and bringing forth the dame the Great Sire's Daughter, Holding aloft the radiant light of Sûrya, as messenger of heaven he shines with treasures.
3Attendant on the Blessed Dame the Blessed hath come: the Lover followeth his Sister. Agni, far-spreading with conspicuous lustre, hath compassed Night with whitelyshining garments.
4His goings-forth kindle as 'twere high voices the goings of the auspicious Friend of Agni. The rays, the bright beams of the strong-jawed, mighty, adorable Steer are visible as he cometh.
5Whose radiant splendours flow, like sounds, about us, his who is lofty, brilliant, and effulgent, Who reaches heaven with best and brightest lustres, sportive and piercing even to the summit.

          

        

        
        


          

            §6-7 · RV 10.3.6-7 (aṣṭaka 7, adhyāya 5, varga 31); Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 fol. 744v
            

              
              
              
            

          


          
          

            
Manuscript

            
            
            
            
            
            
Where on the image
Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41, fol. 744v (numbered 61v by the scribe) (OPenn image 745, upper leaf). Checked on the image: the Rigveda text on this page begins in RV 10.3.6 ‹-jehamānasya svanayan niyut-bhiḥ pratnebhiḥ›. English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
6His powers, whose chariot fellies gleam and glitter have loudly roared while, as with teams, he hasted. He, the most Godlike, far-extending envoy, shines with flames ancient, resonant, whitely-shining.
7So bring us ample wealth: seat thee as envoy of the two youthful Matrons, Earth and Heaven. Let Agni rapid with his rapid, horses, impetuous with impetuous Steeds, come hither.
