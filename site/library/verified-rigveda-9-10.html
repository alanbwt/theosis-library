Introduction

        
Rigveda 9.10 is a hymn of 9 verses addressed to Soma. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 9, the hymns to Soma Pavamāna. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 671v-672r. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

            §1-5 · RV 9.10.1-5 (aṣṭaka 6, adhyāya 7, varga 34); Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 fol. 671v
            

              
              
              
            

          


          
          

            
Manuscript

            
            
            
            
Where on the image
Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41, fol. 671v (numbered 93v by the scribe) (OPenn image 672, upper leaf). Checked on the image: the Rigveda text on this page begins in RV 9.9.5 ‹mahe yuvānam ā dadhuḥ›. English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1LIKE cars that thunder on their way, like coursers eager for renown, Have Soma-drops flowed forth for wealth.
2Forth have they rushed from holding hands, like chariots that are urged to speed, Like joyful songs of singing-men.
3The Somas deck themselves with milk, as Kings are graced with eulogies, And, with seven priests, the sacrifice.
4Pressed for the gladdening draught, the drops flow forth abundantly with song, The Soma juices in a stream.
5Winning Vivasvan's glory and producing Morning's light, the Suns Pass through the openings of the cloth.

          

        

        
        


          

            §6-9 · RV 9.10.6-9 (aṣṭaka 6, adhyāya 7, varga 35); Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 fol. 672r
            

              
              
              
            

          


          
          

            
Manuscript

            
            
            
            
            
            
Where on the image
Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41, fol. 672r (numbered 94r by the scribe) (OPenn image 672, lower leaf). Checked on the image: the Rigveda text on this page begins in RV 9.10.6 ‹āyavaḥ›. The next page with Rigveda text, fol. 672v (numbered 94v by the scribe), begins in RV 9.11.8 ‹-sicyase manaḥ-cit manasaḥ patiḥ›. Verses 9.10.6-9 end on this page; 9.10.6 begins on fol. 671v (numbered 93v by the scribe).

7samīcīnāsaḥ | āsate | hotāraḥ | sapta-jāmayaḥ | padam | ekasya | piprataḥ

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
6The singing-men of ancient time open the doors of sacred songs,– Men, for the mighty to accept.
7Combined in close society sit the seven priests, the brother-hood, Filling the station of the One.
8He gives us kinship with the Gods, and with the Sun unites our eye: The Sage's offspring hath appeared.
9The Sun with his dear eye beholds that quarter of the heavens which priests Have placed within the sacred cell.
