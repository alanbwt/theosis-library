Introduction

        
The eagle and the serpent swear friendship before Shamash. The eagle eats the serpent's young; on Shamash's advice the serpent traps the eagle in a pit. Etana prays for a son, and Shamash sends him to the eagle.
The Legend of Etana tells of an early king of Kish, chosen by the gods, who has no son. An eagle that betrayed its friend the serpent is thrown into a pit; Etana rescues it, and the eagle carries him up towards heaven to find the plant of birth. The story is known from Old Babylonian, Middle Assyrian and Standard Babylonian versions; the end is lost. This page follows the line numbering of the electronic Babylonian Library edition.
How this page is built. Each section is a run of consecutive lines as written on one photographed clay tablet. For every line the eBL edition (https://www.ebl.lmu.de/corpus/L/1/9/SB/II) lists the manuscripts that carry it, with each tablet's own column and line numbers; the tablet chosen for each run is the photographed copy of the work with the most signs preserved there. Commentaries and texts that only quote a line are never chosen. School and excerpt tablets are used only for lines that no photographed library copy preserves as well, and only where they carry the line as text; their sections say so. The original text is eBL's transliteration of that tablet's signs (not a modern composite text): square brackets mark broken signs, x an illegible sign, # a damaged sign, ? an uncertain reading, and words in {braces} are determinatives; the notes give a key to every sign used on this page, and eBL's layout codes are written out in words (for example '(line break)' or '(blank space)'). Superscript numbers are the line numbers of the eBL edition, used in both the Akkadian and the English. The English is B. R. Foster's translation in the eBL edition (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), one line of English per line of the text; italics mark uncertain renderings, as in the source. Where the tablet is broken, the English still translates the whole line of eBL's composite text; each section's note names the lines that are broken on its tablet.
Finding a line on the photograph. Each section names the tablet, the face and column, and the tablet's own line numbers (a prime, as in 5', counts from the first preserved line of a broken column). On a tablet with six columns the obverse carries columns i-iii from left to right; the reverse, turned over from bottom to top, carries columns iv-vi from right to left. The photographs often show the obverse above the reverse with the edges around them; a fragment preserves only part of this layout.
This page shows 98 of the 152 lines of the eBL edition (64%), from 3 tablets in the British Museum: K.2527 (Nineveh), K.1547 (Nineveh), K.2606 (Nineveh).
Lines not shown. 54 of the 152 lines of the eBL edition are not shown, because no photographed copy of the work used here carries them: 1-43 (photograph does not show these lines (visual check): K.8572); 120-127 (photograph does not show these lines (visual check): K.8572); 148-150 (photograph does not show these lines (visual check): K.8572). A line is shown only on a photographed tablet that carries it as text. Commentaries and other texts that quote a line (letters, rituals, god lists) are not used as witnesses, because they carry it with explanations or inside another text; tablets without a photograph in eBL are not used, because the line could not be shown on its tablet.

130“You committed an abomination of the gods, a forbidden act.

131“Were you (not) under oath? I will not come near you.

132“There, there! A man I will send you will help you.”

133Etana kept on beseeching Shamash day after day,

134“O Shamash, you have dined from my fattest sheep!

135“O Netherworld, you have drunk of the blood of my (sacrificed) lambs!

136“I have honored the gods, revered the spirits,

137“Dream interpreters have used up my incense,

138“Gods have used up my lambs in slaughter.

139“O lord, give the command!

140“Grant me the plant of birth!

141“Reveal to me the plant of birth!

142“Relieve me of my disgrace, grant me an heir!”

143Shamash made ready to speak and said to Etana,

144“Go (your) way, cross the mountain,

145“Find a pit, look inside,

146“An eagle is cast within it.

147“He will reveal to you the plant [of birth].”

Where on the image
British Museum K.2606: column iv, the tablet's own lines 1' to 2'. eBL's corpus edition (https://www.ebl.lmu.de/corpus/L/1/9/SB/II) attributes SB II 151-152 to this manuscript at these column and line numbers. Photograph checked: obverse, reverse and edges, ink number K.2606 on the edge; a column of about 29 lines on the obverse and a few lines on the reverse, consistent with eBL's attribution. On this tablet of line 151 only one to three signs survive and line 152 is partly broken (square brackets and x in the Akkadian). The English of these lines translates eBL's composite text, so it includes words that are not preserved on this tablet but are restored from other manuscripts or by the editors. Square brackets in the English mark eBL's restorations in its composite text; words that survive on other tablets carry no brackets even where this tablet is broken.
