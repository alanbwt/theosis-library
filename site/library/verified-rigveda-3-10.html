Introduction

        
Rigveda 3.10 is a hymn of 9 verses addressed to Agni. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 3, the family book of Viśvāmitra. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 195v-196v. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
2They laud thee in their solemn rites, Agni, as Minister and Priest, Shine forth in thine own home as guardian of the Law.
3He, verily, who honours thee with fuel, Knower of all life, He, Agni! wins heroic might, he prospers well.
4Ensign of sacrifices, he, Agni, with Gods is come to us, Decked by the seven priests, to him who bringeth gifts.
5ToAgni, the Invoking Priest, offer your best, your lofty speech, To him Ordainer-like who brings the light of songs.
6Let these our hymns make Agni grow, whence, meet for laud, he springs to life, To mighty strength and great possession, fair to see.
7Best Sacrificer, bring the Gods, O Agni, to the pious man: A joyful Priest, thy splendour drive our foes afar
8As such, O Purifier, shine on us heroic glorious might: Be nearest Friend to those who laud thee, for their weal.

Manuscript

            
            
            
            
            
            
Where on the image
Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41, fol. 196v (numbered 6v by the scribe) (OPenn image 198, upper leaf). Checked on the image: the Rigveda text on this page begins in RV 3.10.9 ‹viprāḥ vipanyavaḥ jāgṛ-vāṃsaḥ sam›.
