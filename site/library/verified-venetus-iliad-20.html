Introduction

        
Book 20 of the Iliad (503 lines in Monro and Allen's edition) as written in Venetus A (Venice, Biblioteca Nazionale Marciana, Marc. gr. Z. 454 = 822), the oldest complete manuscript of the Iliad, copied in the 10th century with the text in the centre of each page and the scholia around it. This book is on fol. 260r-269v; each section below is one page of the manuscript and links that page.
The page of every line comes from the Homer Multitext Project's index of the manuscript (release 2024c), which records where each line stands on the page photographs.
Greek: the manuscript's own text, from the Homer Multitext diplomatic edition of Venetus A, with its accents, punctuation and signs as given there. The Oxford text of Monro and Allen (1920) is given for comparison.
English: A. T. Murray's translation (Loeb Classical Library, 1924). Murray numbers every fifth line and starts a new paragraph at many other lines; the English is divided only at those points, and a passage that runs across a page break goes with the page that holds most of its lines. The English of a section can therefore begin or end a few lines away from the Greek; each section gives its exact English line range.
Not in Venetus A: line 20.312 of the standard text is not in this manuscript. It is shown in the Monro and Allen text and in the English of the page where it would stand.

English · A. T. Murray (1924)

            
1So by the beaked ships around thee, O son of Peleus, insatiate of fight, the Achaeans arrayed them for battle; and likewise the Trojans over against them on the rising ground of the plain. But Zeus bade Themis summon the gods to the place of gathering from the 5brow of many-ribbed Olympus; and she sped everywhither, and bade them come to the house of Zeus. There was no river that came not, save only Oceanus, nor any nymph, of all that haunt the fair copses, the springs that feed the rivers, and the grassy meadows. 10And being come to the house of Zeus they sate them down within the polished colonnades which for father Zeus Hephaestus had builded with cunning skill. Thus were they gathered within the house of Zeus; nor did the Shaker of Earth fail to heed the call of the goddess, but came forth from the sea to join their company; 15and he sate him in the midst, and made question concerning the purpose of Zeus: “Wherefore, thou lord of the bright lightning, hast thou called the gods to the place of gathering? Is it that thou art pondering on somewhat concerning the Trojans and Achaeans? for now is their battle and fighting kindled hard at hand.” Then Zeus, the cloud-gatherer, answered him, and said: 20“Thou knowest, O Shaker of Earth, the purpose in my breast, for the which I gathered you hither; I have regard unto them, even though they die. Yet verily, for myself will I abide here sitting in a fold of Olympus, wherefrom I will gaze and make glad my heart; but do ye others all go forth till ye be come among the Trojans and Achaeans, and bear aid to this side or that, even as the mind of each may be.
