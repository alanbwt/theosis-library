Introduction

        
The Book of the Dead, in Egyptian the spells for going forth by day, is a collection of funerary spells written on papyrus rolls and placed in burials from the New Kingdom to the Roman period. Each roll was made for one person, with spells taken from a large repertoire and illustrated with drawings (vignettes). This library gives one complete roll: the papyrus of Iuefankh in the Museo Egizio, Turin (Cat. 1791), written in the Ptolemaic period (332-30 BCE) and coming from Thebes. Richard Lepsius published it in 1842 and divided it into 165 chapters; his numbering is still the standard numbering of the spells, and it is used here.



Spell 122 (Birch: 'The chapter of Going in after coming out from Hades.') is on Lepsius's plate XLV and on photograph 18 of the 31 Museo Egizio photographs of the roll.



The Egyptian is the transliteration of the Thesaurus Linguae Aegyptiae (edition by Burkhard Backes), with the column number of the spell before each column; the numbers are those of Lepsius's plates. On the papyrus the text runs in vertical columns of cursive hieroglyphs from right to left along the roll; titles and some words are written in red. Signs restored by the editor are in square brackets, signs the scribe left out in angle brackets, superfluous signs in braces and damaged signs in half brackets. The English is the translation by Samuel Birch, published in 1867 and made from Lepsius's copy of this papyrus, so the English and the Egyptian come from the same manuscript. It was the first complete English translation of the Book of the Dead; many passages are now understood differently. Birch's own square brackets give his alternatives and explanations. The photographs are by the Museo Egizio; each shows a stretch of the roll, and the note of each section says where the spell stands on it.

English · Samuel Birch (1867)

            
CXXII. The chapter of Going in after coming out from Hades.
[Vignette. — Deceased approaching a Gate.]
I HAVE opened: it has rendered divine by me. Thou art the passer away of what thou hast made to grow. I am one of ye: who is with thee.(?) The serpent is approaching thee. Let him come head to head, entering the place of regeneration. I go to the boat of the Finder of Faces. Collector of Souls, that is the name of thy vessel. Curler of Locks is the name of the oars. Exciter is the name of the forepart. Doing them is the name of the hatches (or oarblades). Twister of the Centre [or fingers] is the name of the great paddle. As it has been figured at my going so may it be for thee I have been presented with jars of milk, cakes, liquids in the temple of Anupu [Anubis], or, thou hast been given what they have not done to Osiris. He goes in as a hawk, he comes out like a phoenix. He has made way, he goes in peace to the West behind the Pool of Osiris. The Osiris makes way, he goes, he glorifies Osiris, the Lord of Life.
