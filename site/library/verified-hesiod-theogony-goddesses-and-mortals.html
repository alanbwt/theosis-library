Introduction

        
Hesiod's Theogony (c. 700 BCE) is the oldest Greek account of the origin of the world and the gods, from Chaos and Earth through the reign of Cronos to the victory of Zeus. This part: Goddesses who lay with mortal men (lines 963-1022, 60 lines). The children born to goddesses by mortal men, from Demeter and Iasion to Calypso and Odysseus.
The page photographs are from Florence, Biblioteca Medicea Laurenziana, Plut. 32.16 (paper, late 13th century; a large collection of Greek poetry that also holds letters of Maximus Planudes), Theogony on fols. 240r-247v, the oldest complete manuscript of the poem with published images, in the Biblioteca Medicea Laurenziana's Teca Digitale. This part is on fol. 247r, 247v. The scribe writes two verses to a row, one in each column, so a page is read across the columns, row by row. Each section below is one page; its note gives the page's first and last verse as read on the photograph.
The Greek is Evelyn-White's Loeb text (1914) via Perseus, not a transcription of this manuscript, whose wording differs here and there. Verses the editor brackets as later additions are shown in square brackets. The English is Evelyn-White's prose translation (1914); it runs in blocks of about five lines, so at a page break a block can start a line or two before or after the page.

English · H. G. Evelyn-White (1914)

            
963And now farewell, you dwellers on Olympus, and you islands and continents, and you briny sea within. 965Now sing the company of goddesses, sweet-voiced Muses of Olympus, daughter of Zeus who holds the aegis,—even those deathless ones who lay with mortal men and bore children like gods. Demeter, bright goddess, was joined in sweet love 970with the hero Iasion in a thrice-ploughed fallow in the rich land of Crete, and bore Plutus, a kindly god who goes everywhere over land and the sea's wide back, and he makes rich the man who finds him and into whose hands he comes, bestowing great wealth upon him. 975And Harmonia, the daughter of golden Aphrodite, bore to Cadmus Ino and Semele and fair-cheeked Agave and Autonoe whom long haired Aristaeus wedded, and Polydorus also in rich-crowned Thebes. And the daughter of Ocean, Callirrhoe 980was joined in the love of rich Aphrodite with stout-hearted Chrysaor and bore a son who was the strongest of all men, Geryones, whom mighty Heracles killed in sea-girt Erythea for the sake of his shambling oxen. And Eos bore to Tithonus brazen-crested Memnon, 985king of the Ethiopians, and the Lord Emathion. 990seized and caught up and made a keeper of her shrine by night, a divine spirit. And the son of Aeson by the will of the gods led away from Aeetes the daughter of Aeetes the heaven-nurtured king, when he had finished the many grievous labours 995which the great king, overbearing Pelias, that outrageous and presumptuous doer of violence, put upon him. But now, sweet-voiced Muses of Olympus, daughters of Zeus who holds the aegis, sing of the company of women.
