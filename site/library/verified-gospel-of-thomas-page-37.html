Introduction

        
The Gospel of Thomas is a collection of 114 sayings attributed to Jesus, introduced as the hidden sayings written down by Didymos Judas Thomas. The only complete copy is the second tractate of Nag Hammadi Codex II, a papyrus book in Coptic copied in the 4th century and found near Nag Hammadi in Upper Egypt in 1945; it is now in the Coptic Museum, Cairo (inv. 10544). Greek fragments of the work survive in three Oxyrhynchus papyri (P. Oxy. 1, 654 and 655), which are not shown here.
This entry covers saying 22, which begins on codex page 37. The Coptic is given line by line; the small numbers are codex page and line (for example 33.5), so each line can be found on the photograph. Page and line breaks follow the Coptic SCRIPTORIUM edition by Paul Dilley, which he made from the photographs of The Facsimile Edition of the Nag Hammadi Codices, the negatives shown here.
Photograph: Codex II page 37, A-Series negative by Basile Psiroukis, Facsimile Edition p. 47, CCDL nha 2898; Nag Hammadi Archive of the Institute for Antiquity and Christianity, Claremont Colleges Digital Library. Checked on the photograph: page 37 line 1 begins 'ⲛ̄ϩⲛ̄ϣⲏⲣⲉ ϣⲏⲙ⳿ ⲉⲩϭⲉⲗⲓⲧ⳿ ⲁⲩⲥⲱϣⲉ ⲉⲧ' and line 35 reads 'ⲧⲟⲧⲉ ⲧⲉⲧⲛⲁⲃⲱⲕ̂ ⲉϩⲟⲩⲛ [ⲉⲧ]ⲙⲛ̄[ⲧⲉⲣ]ⲟ⳿' (the last line runs into the lower break).

English · Mark M. Mattison

            
Jesus saw some little children nursing. He said to his disciples, "These nursing children can be compared to those who enter the kingdom."
They said to him, "Then we'll enter the kingdom as little children?"
Jesus said to them, "When you make the two into one, and make the inner like the outer and the outer like the inner, and the upper like the lower, and so make the male and the female a single one so that the male won't be male nor the female female; when you make eyes in the place of an eye, a hand in the place of a hand, a foot in the place of a foot, and an image in the place of an image; then you'll enter [the kingdom]."
