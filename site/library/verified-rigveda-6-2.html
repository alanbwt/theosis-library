Introduction

        
Rigveda 6.2 is a hymn of 11 verses addressed to Agni. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 6, the family book of Bharadvāja. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 403v-404v. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1THOU, Agni, even as Mitra, hast a princely glory of thine own. Thou, active Vasu, makest fame increase like full prosperity.
2For, verily, men pray to thee with sacrifices and with songs. To thee the Friendly Courser, seen of all, comes speeding through the air.
3Of one accord men kindle thee Heaven's signal of the sacrifice, When, craving bliss, this race of man invites thee to the solemn rite.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
4Let the man thrive who travails sore, in prayer, for thee the Bountiful. He with the help of lofty Dyaus comes safe through straits of enmity.
5The mortal who with fuel lights thy flame and offers unto thee, Supports a house with many a branch, Agni, to live a hundred years.
6Thy bright smoke lifts itself aloft, and far-extended shines in heaven. For, Purifier! like the Sun thou beamest with thy radiant glow.
7For in men's houses thou must be glorified as a well-loved guest, Gay like an elder in a fort, claiming protection like a son.
8Thou, Agni, like an able steed, art urged by wisdom in the wood. Thou art like wind; food, home art thou, like a young horse that runs astray.
9E'en things imperishable, thou, O Agni, like a gazing ox, Eatest, when hosts, Eternal One! of thee the Mighty rend the woods.

10veṣi | hi | adhvari-yatām | agne | hotā | dame | viśām | sam-ṛdhaḥ | viśpate | kṛṇu | juṣasva | havyam | aṅgiraḥ
11accha | naḥ | mitra-mahaḥ | deva | devān | agne | vocaḥ | su-matim | rodasyoḥ | vīhi | svastim | su-kṣitim | divaḥ | nṝn | dviṣaḥ | aṃhāṃsi | duḥ-itā | tarema | tā | tarema | tava | avasā | tarema

            

            
          


          

            
English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
10Agni, thou enterest as Priest the home of men who sacrifice. Lord of the people, prosper them. Accept the offering, Angiras!
11O Agni, God with Mitra's might, call hither the favour of the Gods from earth and heaven. Bring weal from heaven, that men may dwell securely. May we o'ercome the foe's malign oppressions, may we o'ercome them, through thy help o'ercome them.
