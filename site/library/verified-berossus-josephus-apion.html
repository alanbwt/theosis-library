Introduction

        
In his reply to Apion (c. 95 AD) Josephus set out to prove the antiquity of the Jews from non-Greek historians. After the Egyptian Manetho and the Phoenician archives he turned to Berossus, summarized what Berossus said about the flood, the ark that landed in the mountains of Armenia and the generations after Noah, and then quoted his third book word for word: Nabopolassar sends his son Nebuchadnezzar against a rebel governor in Egypt and Syria; Nebuchadnezzar hurries back across the desert on his father's death, rebuilds and walls Babylon, builds a new palace in fifteen days and a hanging garden for his Median queen; after him come Evil-Merodach, Neriglissar, Labashi-Marduk and Nabonidus, until Cyrus takes Babylon.



The manuscript is Florence, Biblioteca Medicea Laurenziana, Plut. 69.22, a copy of the first book of Against Apion dated to the 10th century by the library (11th by Niese), the principal Greek manuscript of the work (Niese's siglum L). The excerpt runs from fol. 10r to fol. 11v. Each section is one of Niese's numbered sections and points at the page(s) that carry it; the page breaks were read on the images.
