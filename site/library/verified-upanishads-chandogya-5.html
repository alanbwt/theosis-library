Introduction

        
The Chandogya Upanishad belongs to the Samaveda and is one of the two longest and oldest Upanishads. It has eight chapters (prapathakas). This entry gives chapter 5. It tells of the dispute of the senses and the breath (5.1-2), of Svetaketu and King Pravahana Jaivali on the five fires and the paths of the dead (5.3-10), and of King Asvapati Kaikeya teaching six learned householders the universal self, Vaisvanara, and the offering to the breaths (5.11-24).
The page images come from University of Pennsylvania Libraries, Ms. Coll. 390 Item 945, a paper manuscript of 74 leaves (11 x 17 cm) holding the whole Chandogya Upanishad, written in Devanagari, 10 to 11 lines to a side, on blue machine-made paper with watermarks; it is undated (catalogued 18th or 19th century). The scribe numbers the khandas and closes each chapter with a colophon, but does not number paragraphs. Chapter 5 runs from fol. 38v to fol. 47v.
For every page, the first and last words were read on the image and found in the edited text; the page boundaries follow from them. Each section below holds the paragraphs that end on one page, with every page they occupy. The copyist left out paragraphs 5.1.8-14 (his eye passed between repeated words); they are not shown, because no page of this manuscript carries them.

English · F. Max Müller (1879)

                    
1.1He who knows the oldest and the best becomes himself the oldest and the best. Breath indeed is the oldest and the best.
1.2He who knows the richest, becomes himself the richest. Speech indeed is the richest.
1.3He who knows the firm rest, becomes himself firm in this world and in the next. The eye indeed is the firm rest.
1.4He who knows success, his wishes succeed, both his divine and human wishes. The ear indeed is success.
1.5He who knows the home, becomes a home of his people. The mind indeed is the home.
1.6The five senses quarrelled together, who was the best, saying, I am better, I am better.
1.7They went to their father Pragâpati and said: 'Sir, who is the best of us?' He replied: 'He by whose departure the body seems worse than worst, he is the best of you.'
1.15And people do not call them, the tongues, the eyes, the ears, the minds, but the breaths (prâna, the senses). For breath are all these.

English · F. Max Müller (1879)

                    
2.1Breath said: 'What shall be my food?' They answered: 'Whatever there is, even unto dogs and birds.' Therefore this is food for Ana (the breather). His name is clearly Ana. To him who knows this there is nothing that is not (proper) food.
2.2He said: 'What shall be my dress?' They answered: "Water.' Therefore wise people, when they are going to eat food, surround their food before and after with water. He (prâna) thus gains a dress, and is no longer naked.
2.3Satyakâma Gâbâla, after he had communicated this to Gosruti Vaiyâghrapadya, said to him: 'If you were to tell this to a dry stick, branches would grow, and leaves spring from it.'
2.4If a man wishes to reach greatness, let him perform the Dîkshâ (preparatory rite) on the day of the new moon, and then, on the night of the full moon, let him stir a mash of all kinds of herbs with curds and honey, and let him pour ghee on the fire (âvasathya laukika), saying, 'Svâhâ to the oldest and the best.' After that let him throw all that remains (of the ghee) into the mash.
2.5In the same manner let him pour ghee on the fire, saying, 'Svâhâ to the richest.' After that let him throw all that remains together into the mash. In the same manner let him pour ghee on the fire, saying, 'Svâhâ to the firm rest.' After that let him throw all that remains together into the mash. In the same manner let him pour ghee on the fire, saying, 'Svâhâ to success.' After that let him throw all that remains together into the mash.
2.6Then going forward and placing the mash in his hands, he recites: 'Thou (Prâna) art Ama by name, for all this together exists in thee. He is the oldest and best, the king, the sovereign. May he make me the oldest, the best, the king, the sovereign. May I be all this.'
2.7Then he eats with the following Rik verse at every foot: 'We choose that food'—here he swallows—'Of the divine Savitri (prâna)'—here he swallows—'The best and all-supporting food'— here he swallows — "We meditate on the speed of Bhaga (Savitri, prâna)'—here he drinks all.
2.8Having cleansed the vessel, whether it be a kamsa or a kamasa, he sits down behind the fire on a skin or on the bare ground, without speaking or making any other effort. If in his dream he sees a woman, let him know this to be a sign that his sacrifice has succeeded.
2.9On this there is a Sloka: 'If during sacrifices which are to fulfil certain wishes he sees in his dreams a woman, let him know success from this vision in a dream, yea, from this vision in a dream.'
3.1Svetaketu Âruneya went to an assembly of the Pañkâlas. Pravâhana Gaivali said to him: 'Boy, has your father instructed you?' 'Yes, Sir,' he replied.
3.2'Do you know to what place men go from here?' 'No, Sir,' he replied. 'Do you know how they return again?' 'No Sir,' he replied. 'Do you know where the path of Devas and the path of the fathers diverge?' 'No, Sir,' he replied.
3.3"Do you know why that world never becomes full?' 'No, Sir," he replied. 'Do you know why in the fifth libation water is called Man?' 'No, Sir,' he replied.

16.1Then he said to Budila Âsvatarâsvi, 'O Vaiyâghrapadya, whom do you meditate on as the Self?' He replied: 'Water only, venerable king.' He said: 'The Self which you meditate on is the Vaisvânara Self, called Rayi (wealth). Therefore are you wealthy and flourishing.
16.2'You eat food and see your desire, and whoever thus meditates on that Vaisvânara Self, eats food and sees his desire, and has Vedic glory in his house. 'That, however, is but the bladder of the Self, and your bladder would have burst, if you had not come to me.'
17.1Then he said to Auddâlaka Âruni: 'O Gautama, whom do you meditate on as the Self?' He replied: 'The earth only, venerable king.' He said: 'The Self which you meditate on is the Vaisvânara Self, called Pratishthâ (firm rest). Therefore you stand firm with offspring and cattle.
