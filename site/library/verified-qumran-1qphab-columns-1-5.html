Pesher Habakkuk (1QpHab), columns I-V

        
        
Qumran Cave 1 scroll (late 1st century BCE)

        
Israel Museum scroll 1QpHab photographs (Wikimedia Commons, PD-Art) + ETCBC dss transcription (Abegg; CC BY-NC 4.0) + English of W. H. Brownlee, BASOR 112 (1948), public domain · ETCBC dss Text-Fabric transcription (Abegg, Bowley, Cook)

        
      


      

      

        
Introduction

        
The Habakkuk Commentary (Pesher Habakkuk, 1QpHab) is one of the seven scrolls found in Qumran Cave 1 in 1947. It quotes the first two chapters of the book of Habakkuk a phrase at a time and explains each quotation with the word pesher ("its meaning"), applying the prophet's words to the community's own time: the Teacher of Righteousness, the Wicked Priest, the Man of the Lie, and the Kittim, a foreign power usually identified with Rome. The scroll is about 1.48 m long and is kept in the Shrine of the Book at the Israel Museum, Jerusalem (inv. 95.57/28). On the evidence of its handwriting it is usually dated to the late first century BCE.



This entry gives columns I to V of the 13 surviving columns (Habakkuk 1:1-16). Each section is one column. The Hebrew is the scroll's own text with its column and line numbers, from the transcription of Martin G. Abegg Jr. and colleagues in the ETCBC "dss" dataset. Square brackets mark letters lost from the leather and restored by the editors; a dot under a letter marks an uncertain reading; ⟨ ⟩ marks letters written above the line; an ellipsis marks lost letters that are not restored.



The English is the complete translation of the scroll by William H. Brownlee, published in December 1948. Brownlee kept the scroll's columns and printed its line numbers in his margin; they appear here as superscript numbers. In his notation a diagonal line (/) marks the start of a new line of the scroll, words in parentheses are his conjectural restorations, a row of dots (shown here as …) is a lacuna, ? marks traces of letters, # marks letters that can be read but not translated, and words between asterisks were moved across a line division. His quotations of Habakkuk are set in italics.



Gaps: columns VI to XIII (Habakkuk 1:16 to 2:20) are not included, because no photograph of those columns is available under terms that allow reuse here. Columns I and II, and the lower lines of every column, are damaged on the scroll itself.
