Introduction

        
The Book of the Dead, in Egyptian the spells for going forth by day, is a collection of funerary spells written on papyrus rolls and placed in burials from the New Kingdom to the Roman period. Each roll was made for one person, with spells taken from a large repertoire and illustrated with drawings (vignettes). This library gives one complete roll: the papyrus of Iuefankh in the Museo Egizio, Turin (Cat. 1791), written in the Ptolemaic period (332-30 BCE) and coming from Thebes. Richard Lepsius published it in 1842 and divided it into 165 chapters; his numbering is still the standard numbering of the spells, and it is used here.



Spell 124 (Birch: 'The chapter of Going to the Ministers [Chiefs] of Osiris.') is on Lepsius's plates XLV-XLVI and on photograph 18 of the 31 Museo Egizio photographs of the roll.



The Egyptian is the transliteration of the Thesaurus Linguae Aegyptiae (edition by Burkhard Backes), with the column number of the spell before each column; the numbers are those of Lepsius's plates. On the papyrus the text runs in vertical columns of cursive hieroglyphs from right to left along the roll; titles and some words are written in red. Signs restored by the editor are in square brackets, signs the scribe left out in angle brackets, superfluous signs in braces and damaged signs in half brackets. The English is the translation by Samuel Birch, published in 1867 and made from Lepsius's copy of this papyrus, so the English and the Egyptian come from the same manuscript. It was the first complete English translation of the Book of the Dead; many passages are now understood differently. Birch's own square brackets give his alternatives and explanations. The photographs are by the Museo Egizio; each shows a stretch of the roll, and the note of each section says where the spell stands on it.

Images: Museo Egizio, Turin, photographs of Cat. 1791 on Wikimedia Commons (https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Book_of_the_Dead_of_Iuefankh_-_Museo_Egizio_(Turin)_C._1791), e.g. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Book_of_the_Dead_of_Iuefankh,_papyrus_-_Museo_Egizio_Turin_C_1791_p034.jpg (CC0 1.0 (Museo Egizio, as stated on Wikimedia Commons))

            
Text-to-image mapping: Spell order, plate and column numbers from Lepsius, Das Todtenbuch der Aegypter (1842), whose plates reproduce this papyrus and are headed by their chapter numbers; the TLA uses the same numbers. Positions in the notes are measured from the left edge of the photograph.

English · Samuel Birch (1867)

            
CXXIV. The chapter of Going to the Ministers [Chiefs] of Osiris.
Vignette. — Deceased adoring four Genii of the Dead.]
He who dwells Tattu, in Tu, in Uat builds my soul. I have ploughed the fields in person. I have mowed for [as] Khem. What is abominable, I do not eat it. What is filthy, I do not eat it. Heaps of food I have none of it, I do not touch it with my hand, I do not tread on with my sandals, because my bread is of white corn, my drink is of red corn of the Nile.
Says the Ark and its cabin, I have brought them. I eat under the grapes and peaches. I knew what I did with Good- Arms, how I illuminated the white crown, how I twined round it the urei.
Oh Keepers of the Gate of the Gods, who salute the earth, and make peace! Let me bear.... . Akheku has opened to me his arms where the Gods are, and listen to the words of the recipients of light and the Osiris, and the hearts of the Gods pass or listen. I protect myself, and prevail in heaven as those who fly.
Oh Builders for every God and Goddess! let him pass them by me. He has been judged [created] before the Sun, he has been judged [created] before the Gods of the Circle. The Spirit has concealed the heaven from the Gods. What I have by me is bread for them, the Gods. I have gone through the disk, I have come out through Tum, or through Ahu. I have spoken to his divine servants. I have spoken to the Spirits. I have vanquished those attached to darkness. I have sat within the Great Mahur [the Asp], where he dwells who belongs to the day. I have been there with Osiris. I repeat the words he has told me. He has made me understand the words of the Gods. My wise Spirit comes. I have touched truth when I wished it. I am the Spirit more prepared than all Spirits, the image for the mummies of Annu [Heliopolis] Tattu [This], Suten-Khen [Bubastis], Abutu [Abydos], Apu [Khemmo], Senu.
The Osiris has been justified by [or as] every God and Goddess hidden in Hades.
