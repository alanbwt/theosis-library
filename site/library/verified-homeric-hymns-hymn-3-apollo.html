Introduction

        
The Homeric Hymns are 33 hexameter poems to the Greek gods, composed by anonymous poets between about the 7th and the 5th century BCE and handed down under Homer's name. This entry: Homeric Hymn 3, To Apollo. The birth of Apollo on Delos and the founding of his oracle at Delphi, with the slaying of the serpent and the Cretan sailors who become his priests.
This collection has Hymns 1-18 only. The manuscript shown, M, breaks off at Hymn 18, line 4 (fol. 50r; fol. 50v is blank), so the rest of Hymn 18 (lines 5-12) and Hymns 19-33 (to Pan, Hephaestus, Apollo, Poseidon, Zeus, Hestia, the Muses and Apollo, Dionysus, Artemis, Athena, Hestia, Earth, Helios, Selene and the Dioscuri) are not in it and are not given here.
The page photographs are from Leiden, Universiteitsbibliotheek, BPG 33 H (the 'Moscow' manuscript, M; paper, Greece?, first half of the 15th century; found in Moscow in 1777 by C. F. Matthaei), Homeric Hymns on fols. 31r-50r. M is the only manuscript of the Hymn to Demeter. Each section below is one column of a page (a = left, b = right); its note gives the first and last verse as read on the photograph. This entry is on fol. 36r, 36v, 37r, 37v, 38r, 38v, 39r, 39v, 40r, 40v.
Verses of the Loeb text that are not in this manuscript, and so are not shown: Hymn 3: 23-73, 136-138.
The Greek is Evelyn-White's Loeb text (1914) via Perseus, not a transcription of the manuscript; where M is damaged the edition's restorations are shown in square brackets. The English is Evelyn-White's prose translation (1914), in blocks of about five lines, so a block can begin a line or two before or after a column break.

89Now when Leto had sworn and ended her oath, 90Delos was very glad at the birth of the far-shooting lord.
