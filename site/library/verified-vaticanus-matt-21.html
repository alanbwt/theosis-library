It gives the letters as the first scribe wrote them: no accents or breathings, the scribe's own spelling, and the sacred names in their contracted form (θς for θεος, ις for ιησους, κς for κυριος; the overline is not reproduced). Corrections by later hands are not shown. Letters in square brackets are not legible on the page and were restored by the transcribers, a dot under a letter marks an uncertain reading, and [...] marks text lost to damage. Beneath each section, for comparison, is Tischendorf's 8th critical edition (1869). Where the two differ, the transcription is what the manuscript says.
