Introduction

        
The Instruction of Ptahhotep presents itself as the teaching of Ptahhotep, vizier of King Isesi of the 5th Dynasty (24th century BCE), handed on to his son when the vizier asks to retire. It is a series of 37 maxims on conduct (modesty, self-control, justice, speech at the right moment, care of household and dependants), framed by a prologue and a long epilogue on 'hearing', which in Egyptian means both listening and obeying. The oldest complete copy is the Papyrus Prisse, written in the 12th Dynasty around 1850 BCE and now in the Bibliotheque nationale de France (Egyptien 183-194).



In the papyrus the text runs over 16 columns of horizontal hieratic lines, numbered pages 4 to 19 (pages 1-2 hold the end of the Instruction for Kagemni, page 3 was erased). The opening of most maxims is written in red. The papyrus is mounted in frames: page 4-5 on Egyptien 186, page 6 on 187, page 7 on 188, and two pages on each of 189 to 194. Every section below is linked to the photograph of the column or columns that carry it, and its scan note gives the page and line numbers.



The sections follow Battiscombe Gunn's 1906 translation (A, B, maxims 1-37, C, 38-43, D and the colophon). For each Gunn section the Egyptian is the corresponding run of sentences in the Thesaurus Linguae Aegyptiae edition by Peter Dils, with the papyrus page and line number before each line. A second block gives the TLA hieroglyphic transcription in Unicode (a transcription of the hieratic); the few words for which the TLA has no complete hieroglyphic encoding are shown there in transliteration, in italics. Signs supplied by the editor are in angle brackets, superfluous signs in braces, damaged or unclear signs in half brackets, and lost text as [...].



Gunn translated from the Prisse text but did not translate maxim 32, for which he printed only a heading; that section therefore has the Egyptian but no real English. Gunn's renderings are early and some passages are now understood differently.

English · Battiscombe G. Gunn (1906)

                    
The Instruction of the Governor of his City, the Vizier, Ptah-hotep, in the Reign of the King of Upper and Lower Egypt, Isôsi, living for ever, to the end of Time.

        
The Governor of his City, the Vizier, Ptah-hotep, he said: 'O Prince, my Lord, the end of life is at hand; old age descendeth [upon me]; feebleness cometh, and childishness is renewed. He [that is old] lieth down in misery every day. The eyes are small; the ears are deaf. Energy is diminished, the heart hath no rest. The mouth is silent, and he speaketh no word; the heart stoppeth, and he remembereth not yesterday. The bones are painful throughout the body; good turneth unto evil. All taste departeth. These things doeth old age for mankind, being evil in all things. The nose is stopped, and he breatheth not for weakness (?), whether standing or sitting.

        
'Command me, thy servant, therefore, to make over my princely authority [to my son]. Let me speak unto him the words of them that hearken to the counsel of the men of old time; those that hearkened unto the gods. I pray thee, let this thing be done, that sin may be banished from among persons of understanding, that thou may enlighten the lands.'

        
Said the Majesty of this God: 'Instruct him, then, in the words of old time; may he be a wonder unto the children of princes, that they may enter and hearken with him. Make straight all their hearts; and discourse with him, without causing weariness.'
