Introduction

        
A hymn to Ninurta introduces the poem. Anzu, set to guard Enlil's bath, seizes the Tablet of Destinies and flies off; the gods look for a champion, and Adad, Gerra and Shara refuse.
The Epic of Anzu tells how the lion-headed bird Anzu stole the Tablet of Destinies from Enlil, and how the god Ninurta, after other gods refused, defeated Anzu and recovered it. The Standard Babylonian version has three tablets. This page follows the line numbering of the electronic Babylonian Library edition.
How this page is built. Each section is a run of consecutive lines as written on one photographed clay tablet. For every line the eBL edition (https://www.ebl.lmu.de/corpus/L/1/10/SB/I) lists the manuscripts that carry it, with each tablet's own column and line numbers; the tablet chosen for each run is the photographed manuscript with the most signs preserved there. The original text is eBL's transliteration of that tablet's signs (not a modern composite text): square brackets mark broken signs, x an illegible sign, # a damaged sign, ? an uncertain reading, and words in {braces} are determinatives. Superscript numbers are the line numbers of the eBL edition, used in both the Akkadian and the English. The English is B. R. Foster's translation in the eBL edition (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), one line of English per line of the text; italics mark uncertain renderings, as in the source.
Finding a line on the photograph. Each section names the tablet, the face and column, and the tablet's own line numbers (a prime, as in 5', counts from the first preserved line of a broken column). On a tablet with six columns the obverse carries columns i-iii from left to right; the reverse, turned over from bottom to top, carries columns iv-vi from right to left. The photographs often show the obverse above the reverse with the edges around them; a fragment preserves only part of this layout.
This page shows 117 of the 201 lines of the eBL edition (58%), from 4 tablets in the British Museum: K.7257 (Nineveh), K.3454 (Nineveh), K.22880 (Nineveh), BM.38797.
Lines not shown. 84 of the 201 lines of the eBL edition have no witness here with an open photograph: 22-a+32 (only on YBC.9842); a+33 (only on YBC.9842, Di.2258); a+34 (only on YBC.9842); a+35 (only on YBC.9842, Di.2258); a+36 (only on YBC.9842); a+37 (only on YBC.9842, Di.2258); a+38-a+47 (only on YBC.9842, IM.58821); a+48 (only on YBC.9842); a+49-a+54 (only on YBC.9842, Di.2258); a+55-b+57 (only on YBC.9842); b+110-b+124 (no manuscript preserves it); b+167-c+195 (only on YBC.9842); c+198 (only on YBC.9842); c+199-c+201 (only on IM.95180, YBC.9842). Lines that survive only on tablets whose photographs are not openly licensed (for example in the Iraq Museum, Berlin, Istanbul, Ankara, Philadelphia or private collections), or only on tablets whose eBL photograph does not show the lines, are omitted.

English · B. R. Foster (eBL edition)

                    
1Son of the king of the inhabited world, splendid one, beloved of [Ma]mi,

2The mighty one will I ever sing, divine firstborn of [En]lil,

3Ninurta, the splendid one, beloved of Mami,

4The mighty one will I ever praise, divine firstborn of Enlil.

5Born (in) Ekur, leader of the Anu[nna-gods], Eninnu’s hope,

6Who waters paddock, garden, town, street, and city,

7Wave of battles, dancer (in combat), sash of valor,

8Whose tireless onset raging fiends dread,

9Hear the praise of the mighty one’s strength!

10It is he who in his fierceness defeated and fettered the mount of stones,

11Conquering soaring Anzu with his weapon,

12Slaying the bull man in the midst of the sea.

13Doughty, valorous, slaughterer with his weapon,

14Mighty one, impetuous, leader of battle and fray!

15Until then, no daises had been built among the Igigi-gods,
