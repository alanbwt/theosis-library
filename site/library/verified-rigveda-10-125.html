Introduction

        
Rigveda 10.125 is a hymn of 8 verses addressed to Vac (speech). It stands in book (maṇḍala) 10, the last and latest book. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 48 (University of Pennsylvania), a paper manuscript of the eighth aṣṭaka (RV 10.46-10.191) of the Ṛgveda Saṃhitā in Devanāgarī, 158 leaves, 6 lines to the page, with the accents marked in red; the scribe's colophon on fol. 158 gives Saṃvat 1768 (1711 CE); this hymn stands on fol. 112r-113r. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Like most Rigveda manuscripts, Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 48 writes a repeated refrain in short form after its first occurrence (for example kasmai° for kasmai devāya haviṣā vidhema); the Sanskrit shown is the full text of Aufrecht's edition.

Vedic Sanskrit · Aufrecht (accented)

                    
1a̱haṁ ru̱drebhi̱r vasu̍bhiś carāmy a̱ham ā̍di̱tyair u̱ta vi̱śvade̍vaiḥ | a̱ham mi̱trāvaru̍ṇo̱bhā bi̍bharmy a̱ham i̍ndrā̱gnī a̱ham a̱śvino̱bhā ||
2a̱haṁ soma̍m āha̱nasa̍m bibharmy a̱haṁ tvaṣṭā̍ram u̱ta pū̱ṣaṇa̱m bhaga̍m | a̱haṁ da̍dhāmi̱ dravi̍ṇaṁ ha̱viṣma̍te suprā̱vye̱3̱̍ yaja̍mānāya sunva̱te ||

            
          


          

            
English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1I TRAVEL with the Rudras and the Vasus, with the Âdityas and All-Gods I wander. I hold aloft both Varuṇa and Mitra, Indra and Agni, and the Pair of Aṣvins.
2I cherish and sustain high-swelling Soma, and Tvashṭar I support, Pûshan, and Bhaga. I load with wealth the zealous sdcrificer who pours the juice and offers his oblation

Vedic Sanskrit · Aufrecht (accented)

                    
3a̱haṁ rāṣṭrī̍ sa̱ṁgama̍nī̱ vasū̍nāṁ ciki̱tuṣī̍ pratha̱mā ya̱jñiyā̍nām | tām mā̍ de̱vā vy a̍dadhuḥ puru̱trā bhūri̍sthātrā̱m bhūry ā̍ve̱śaya̍ntīm ||
4mayā̱ so anna̍m atti̱ yo vi̱paśya̍ti̱ yaḥ prāṇi̍ti̱ ya ī̍ṁ śṛ̱ṇoty u̱ktam | a̱ma̱ntavo̱ māṁ ta upa̍ kṣiyanti śru̱dhi śru̍ta śraddhi̱vaṁ te̍ vadāmi ||
5a̱ham e̱va sva̱yam i̱daṁ va̍dāmi̱ juṣṭa̍ṁ de̱vebhi̍r u̱ta mānu̍ṣebhiḥ | yaṁ kā̱maye̱ taṁ-ta̍m u̱graṁ kṛ̍ṇomi̱ tam bra̱hmāṇa̱ṁ tam ṛṣi̱ṁ taṁ su̍me̱dhām ||
6a̱haṁ ru̱drāya̱ dhanu̱r ā ta̍nomi brahma̱dviṣe̱ śara̍ve̱ hanta̱vā u̍ | a̱haṁ janā̍ya sa̱mada̍ṁ kṛṇomy a̱haṁ dyāvā̍pṛthi̱vī ā vi̍veśa ||

            
          


          

            
English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
3I am the Queen, the gatherer-up of treasures, most thoughtful, first of those who merit worship. Thus Gods have stablished me in many places with many homes to enter and abide in.
4Through me alone all eat the food that feeds them,– each man who sees, breathes, hears the word outspoken They know it not, but yet they dwell beside me. Hear, one and all, the truth as I declare it.
5I, verily, myself announce and utter the word that Gods and men alike shall welcome. I make the man I love exceeding mighty, make him a sage, a R̥ishi, and a Brahman.
6I bend the bow for Rudra that his arrow may strike and slay the hater of devotion. I rouse and order battle for the people, and I have penetrated Earth and Heaven.

Vedic Sanskrit · Aufrecht (accented)

                    
7a̱haṁ su̍ve pi̱tara̍m asya mū̱rdhan mama̱ yoni̍r a̱psv a1̱̍ntaḥ sa̍mu̱dre | tato̱ vi ti̍ṣṭhe̱ bhuva̱nānu̱ viśvo̱tāmūṁ dyāṁ va̱rṣmaṇopa̍ spṛśāmi ||

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
7On the world's summit I bring forth the Father: my home is in the waters, in the ocean. Thence I extend o'er all existing creatures, and touch even yonder heaven with my forehead.
8I breathe a strong breath like the wind and tempest, the while I hold together all existence. Beyond this wide earth and beyond the heavens I have become so mighty in my grandeur.
