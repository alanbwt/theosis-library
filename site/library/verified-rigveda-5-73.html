Introduction

        
Rigveda 5.73 is a hymn of 10 verses addressed to Asvins. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 5, the family book of the Atri clan. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 388v-389v. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1WHETHER, O Aṣvins, ye this day be far remote or near at hand, In many spots or in mid-air, come hither, Lords of ample wealth.
2These here, who show o'er widest space, bringing full many a wondrous act, Resistless, lovingly I seek, I call the Mightiest to enjoy.
3Another beauteous wheel have ye fixed there to decorate your car. With others through the realms ye roam in might unto the neighbouring tribes.
4That deed of yours that is extolled, Viṣvas! hath all been done with this. Born otherwise, and spotless, ye have entered kinship's bonds with us.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
5When Sûryâ mounted on your car that rolls for ever rapidly, Birds of red hue were round about and burning splendours compassed you.
6Atri bethinks himself of you, O Heroes, with a friendly mind, What time, Nâsatyas, with his mouth he stirs the spotless flame for you.
7Strong is your swiftly moving steed, famed his exertion in the course When by your great deeds, Aṣvins, Chiefs, Atri is brought to us again.
8Lovers of sweetness, Rudras, she who streams with sweetness waits on you. When ye have travelled through the seas men bring you gifts of well-dressed food.
9Aṣvins, with truth they call you Twain bestowers of felicity; At sacrifice most prompt to hear, most gracious ye at sacrifice.
