Introduction

        
Rigveda 6.53 is a hymn of 10 verses addressed to Pusan. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 6, the family book of Bharadvāja. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 472v-473v. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

Manuscript

            
            
            
            
Where on the image
Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41, fol. 472v (numbered 145v by the scribe) (OPenn image 473, upper leaf). Checked on the image: the Rigveda text on this page begins in RV 6.52.16 ‹agnīparjanyau avatam dhiyam me›. English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1LORD of the path, O Pûshan, we have yoked and bound thee to our hymn, Even as a car, to win the prize.
2Bring us the wealth that men require, a manly master of a house, Free-handed with the liberal meed.
3Even him who would not give, do thou, O glowing Pûshan, urge to give, And make the niggard's soul grow soft.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
4Clear paths that we may win the prize; scatter our enemies afar. Strong God, be all our thoughts fulfilled.
5Penetrate with an awl, O Sage, the hearts of avaricious churls, And make them subject to our will.
6Thrust with thine awl, O Pûshan: seek that which the niggard's heart holds dear, And make him subject to our will.
7Tear up and rend in pieces, Sage, the hearts of avaricious churls, And make them subject to our will.
8Thou, glowing Pûshan, carriest an awl that urges men to prayer; Therewith do thou tear up and rend to shreds the heart of every one.
9Thou bearest, glowing Lord! a goad with horny point that guides the cows Thence do we seek thy gift of bliss.
