Introduction

        
The elders advise Gilgamesh. His mother, the goddess Ninsun, prays to Shamash for his protection and adopts Enkidu. The two heroes leave Uruk for the Forest of Cedar.
The Standard Babylonian version of the epic, traditionally ascribed to the scholar Sin-leqi-unninni, was written on twelve tablets. Most surviving copies come from the libraries of Ashurbanipal at Nineveh (7th century BCE) and from Babylon and other Babylonian cities (7th-2nd century BCE). This page gives Tablet III in the line numbering of the electronic Babylonian Library edition by A. R. George (2022 and later revisions).
How this page is built. Each section is a run of consecutive lines as written on one photographed clay tablet. For every line the eBL edition (https://www.ebl.lmu.de/corpus/L/1/4/SB/III) lists the manuscripts that carry it, with each tablet's own column and line numbers; the tablet chosen for each run is the photographed manuscript with the most signs preserved there. The original text is eBL's transliteration of that tablet's signs (not a modern composite text): square brackets mark broken signs, x an illegible sign, # a damaged sign, ? an uncertain reading, and words in {braces} are determinatives. Superscript numbers are the line numbers of the eBL edition, used in both the Akkadian and the English. The English is A. R. George's translation in the eBL edition (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), one line of English per line of the poem; italics mark uncertain renderings, as in the source.
Finding a line on the photograph. Each section names the tablet, the face and column, and the tablet's own line numbers (a prime, as in 5', counts from the first preserved line of a broken column). On a tablet with six columns the obverse carries columns i-iii from left to right; the reverse, turned over from bottom to top, carries columns iv-vi from right to left. The photographs often show the obverse above the reverse with the edges around them; a fragment preserves only part of this layout.
This page shows 164 of the 209 lines of the eBL edition (78%), from 8 tablets in the British Museum: BM.98990 (Nineveh), K.4474 (Nineveh), K.3423 (Nineveh), K.9885 (Nineveh), K.8558 (Nineveh), BM.35079, BM.32960, BM.36093.
Lines not shown. 45 of the 209 lines of the eBL edition have no witness here with an open photograph: 23 (only on BM.34191); 29-30 (only on BM.34191); 59 (only on IM.76873); 85 (only on VAT.19286, IM.76873); 109-116 (only on IM.76873); 122a (only on IM.76873); 133-136 (only on IM.76873); 164-165 (only on K.8573); 166-173 (only on K.8573, BM.34191); 207-222 (only on BM.34191); 244 (no manuscript preserves it). Lines that survive only on tablets whose photographs are not openly licensed (for example in the Iraq Museum, Berlin, Istanbul, Ankara, Philadelphia or private collections), or only on tablets whose eBL photograph does not show the lines, are omitted.

English · A. R. George (eBL edition)

                    
1‘[Come back in safety] to [the quay of] Uruk!

2Do not trust, O Gilgameš, in the fullness of your strength,

3let your eyes be satisfied, strike a blow to rely on!

4“He who goes in front saves (his) comrade,

5he who knew the road protected his friend.”

6Let Enkidu go in front of you,

7he knows the way to the Forest of Cedar!

8He is tried in battles and experienced in combat,

9let Enkidu protect (his) friend and keep safe (his) comrade!

10let him bring his person back to his wives!

11In this our assembly we hereby give the king into your care:

12you will ensure (his) return and give the king into our care.’

13Gilgameš opened his mouth to speak,

14saying to Enkidu:

15‘Come, my friend, let us go to Egal-maḫ,

16into the presence of the great Queen Ninsun!

17Ninsun is clever, she is wise, she knows everything,

18she will set steps of (good) counsel for our feet.’

19They took each other hand in hand,

20Gilgameš and Enkidu, as they went to Egal-maḫ.

21Into the presence of the great Queen Ninsun,

22Gilgameš arose and entered before [the goddess, his mother.]

24‘O Ninsun, I have grown so bold [as to travel]

25the distant path to where Ḫumbaba is.

26I shall face a battle that I do not know,

27I shall ride a road that I do not know.

28I hereby beseech you, give me your blessing, so that I may go,
