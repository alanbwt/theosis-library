Introduction

        
Manetho's third book carried the kings of Egypt from the Twentieth Dynasty to the second Persian conquest. It includes the Tanite and Bubastite houses (Smendes, Psusennes, Sesonchis, Osorthon), Bocchoris of Sais, under whom 'a lamb spoke', the Ethiopian conquerors Sabacon, Sebichos and Taracus, the Saite kings Psammetichus, Nechao II and Amosis, the Persian kings from Cambyses to Darius II, the last native houses of Mendes and Sebennytus (Nectanebes and Teos), and the brief Persian dynasty of Ochus, Arses and Darius III, 'whom Alexander of Macedon overthrew'.



As with the earlier books, the lists survive in the two epitomes of Julius Africanus and Eusebius, which George Syncellus copied side by side about 810 AD. The manuscript is Paris, Bibliothèque nationale de France, grec 1764 (11th century), fols. 10v-11v, where the dynasties are numbered κ'-λα' in the margin; the images are from the BnF microfilm. Each section is one dynasty in one version, in the order of the manuscript, with the folio and the words read on it.
