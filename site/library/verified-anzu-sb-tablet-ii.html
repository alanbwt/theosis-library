Introduction

        
The mother goddess persuades her son Ninurta to fight Anzu. His first attack fails because Anzu uses the power of the Tablet of Destinies; Ea sends him new advice.
The Epic of Anzu tells how the lion-headed bird Anzu stole the Tablet of Destinies from Enlil, and how the god Ninurta, after other gods refused, defeated Anzu and recovered it. The Standard Babylonian version has three tablets. This page follows the line numbering of the electronic Babylonian Library edition.
How this page is built. Each section is a run of consecutive lines as written on one photographed clay tablet. For every line the eBL edition (https://www.ebl.lmu.de/corpus/L/1/10/SB/II) lists the manuscripts that carry it, with each tablet's own column and line numbers; the tablet chosen for each run is the photographed manuscript with the most signs preserved there. The original text is eBL's transliteration of that tablet's signs (not a modern composite text): square brackets mark broken signs, x an illegible sign, # a damaged sign, ? an uncertain reading, and words in {braces} are determinatives. Superscript numbers are the line numbers of the eBL edition, used in both the Akkadian and the English. The English is B. R. Foster's translation in the eBL edition (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), one line of English per line of the text; italics mark uncertain renderings, as in the source.
Finding a line on the photograph. Each section names the tablet, the face and column, and the tablet's own line numbers (a prime, as in 5', counts from the first preserved line of a broken column). On a tablet with six columns the obverse carries columns i-iii from left to right; the reverse, turned over from bottom to top, carries columns iv-vi from right to left. The photographs often show the obverse above the reverse with the edges around them; a fragment preserves only part of this layout.
This page shows 71 of the 154 lines of the eBL edition (46%), from 4 tablets in the British Museum: K.3008 (Nineveh), K.21072 (Nineveh), BM.121060 (Nineveh), K.19368 (Nineveh).
Lines not shown. 83 of the 154 lines of the eBL edition have no witness here with an open photograph: 1 (only on A.144, SU-1951.102.A, IM.95180, YBC.9842); 2-22 (only on A.144, SU-1951.102.A, IM.95180); 23-25 (only on A.144, SU-1951.102.A); 26 (only on A.144, SU-1951.102.A, SU-1952.218); 44-45 (only on A.144, SU-1951.102.A, SU-1952.232); 56-58 (only on A.144, SU-1951.102.A, SU-1952.232, IM.95180); 59 (only on K.18740, SU-1951.102.A, SU-1952.232, IM.95180); 60-64 (only on K.18740, A.144, SU-1951.102.A, SU-1952.232, IM.95180); 83-89 (only on A.144, SU-1951.102.A, SU-1952.232); 90 (no manuscript preserves it); 92 (no manuscript preserves it); 96-99 (only on A.144); 100 (no manuscript preserves it); 101-102 (only on A.144); 103-122 (only on A.144, SU-1951.102.A); 132 (only on A.144, SU-1951.102.A); 146-147 (only on A.144, IM.95180); 148-152 (only on A.144, SU-1951.102.A, IM.95180); 153-154 (only on IM.95180). English · B. R. Foster (eBL edition)

                    
27“Show yourself pre-eminent before the gods, and your name shall be ‘Mighty One’.”

28The warrior heeded his mother’s word,

29Seething with fury, he made his way toward his mountain.

30The lord hitched up seven battles,

31The warrior hitched up against him seven ill winds,

32Seven whirlwinds that made the dust swirl.

33He launched a terrifying assault, made war,

34The gusts were ready at his side for battle.

35Anzu and Ninurta met on the mountainside.

36When Anzu saw him, he began shaking with rage at him,

37He snarled like a storm-demon, his frightful radiance enveloped the mountains.

38He was roaring like a lion, carried away with passion.

39In his rage he cried to the w[arrior],

40“I have carried off all possible divine authority,

41“And I appropriated for myself the responsibilities of all the gods.

42“Who are you who have to come fight with me? Account for yourself!”

43He rushed toward him as his challenge was going out to him,
