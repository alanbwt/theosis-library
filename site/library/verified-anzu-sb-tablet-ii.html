Introduction

        
The mother goddess persuades her son Ninurta to fight Anzu. His first attack fails because Anzu uses the power of the Tablet of Destinies; Ea sends him new advice.
The Epic of Anzu tells how the lion-headed bird Anzu stole the Tablet of Destinies from Enlil, and how the god Ninurta, after other gods refused, defeated Anzu and recovered it. The Standard Babylonian version has three tablets. This page follows the line numbering of the electronic Babylonian Library edition.
How this page is built. Each section is a run of consecutive lines as written on one photographed clay tablet. For every line the eBL edition (https://www.ebl.lmu.de/corpus/L/1/10/SB/II) lists the manuscripts that carry it, with each tablet's own column and line numbers; the tablet chosen for each run is the photographed copy of the work with the most signs preserved there. Commentaries and texts that only quote a line are never chosen. School and excerpt tablets are used only for lines that no photographed library copy preserves as well, and only where they carry the line as text; their sections say so. The original text is eBL's transliteration of that tablet's signs (not a modern composite text): square brackets mark broken signs, x an illegible sign, # a damaged sign, ? an uncertain reading, and words in {braces} are determinatives; the notes give a key to every sign used on this page, and eBL's layout codes are written out in words (for example '(line break)' or '(blank space)'). Superscript numbers are the line numbers of the eBL edition, used in both the Akkadian and the English. The English is B. R. Foster's translation in the eBL edition (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), one line of English per line of the text; italics mark uncertain renderings, as in the source. Where the tablet is broken, the English still translates the whole line of eBL's composite text; each section's note names the lines that are broken on its tablet.
Finding a line on the photograph. Each section names the tablet, the face and column, and the tablet's own line numbers (a prime, as in 5', counts from the first preserved line of a broken column). On a tablet with six columns the obverse carries columns i-iii from left to right; the reverse, turned over from bottom to top, carries columns iv-vi from right to left. The photographs often show the obverse above the reverse with the edges around them; a fragment preserves only part of this layout.
This page shows 72 of the 154 lines of the eBL edition (47%), from 5 tablets in the Yale Peabody Museum and British Museum: YBC.9842, K.3008 (Nineveh), K.21072 (Nineveh), BM.121060 (Nineveh), K.19368 (Nineveh).

27“Show yourself pre-eminent before the gods, and your name shall be ‘Mighty One’.”

28The warrior heeded his mother’s word,

29Seething with fury, he made his way toward his mountain.

30The lord hitched up seven battles,

31The warrior hitched up against him seven ill winds,

33He launched a terrifying assault, made war,

Manuscript

            
            
            
            
Where on the image
British Museum K.21072: column iii, the tablet's own lines 1' to 9'. eBL's corpus edition (https://www.ebl.lmu.de/corpus/L/1/10/SB/II) attributes SB II 123-131 to this manuscript at these column and line numbers. Photograph checked: eight views of a curved fragment about 2 x 3 cm in large script, ink K21072 on the broken back and 81-2-4 on the inscribed surface (eBL notes "Marked, 81-2-4"). About 6 or 7 line-ends per view with a ruling after every second line, as eBL describes; about enough for eBL's 9 + 9 short line-ends in columns ii and iii, though fewer than 19 are clearly countable. On this tablet of lines 124, 126-131 only one to three signs survive and lines 123, 125 are partly broken (square brackets and x in the Akkadian). The English of these lines translates eBL's composite text, so it includes words that are not preserved on this tablet but are restored from other manuscripts or by the editors. Square brackets in the English mark eBL's restorations in its composite text; words that survive on other tablets carry no brackets even where this tablet is broken.
