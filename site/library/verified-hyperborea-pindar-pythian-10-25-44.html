Introduction

        
Pindar's tenth Pythian ode, his earliest datable poem, was written in 498 BCE for Hippocleas of Thessaly, winner of the boys' double foot race at Delphi. After praising the man who sees his son crowned, Pindar reaches the limit of mortal fortune: no one can find the marvellous road to the Hyperboreans, by ship or on foot. Perseus once feasted among them as they sacrificed hecatombs of asses to Apollo, who delights in their festivals; the Muse is never absent from their dances; crowned with golden laurel they feast, and neither disease nor old age touches that holy race, which lives without toil or battle, beyond the reach of Nemesis.



The manuscript is Vatican, Biblioteca Apostolica Vaticana, Vat. gr. 1312 (Pindar B), 12th century, the oldest manuscript of the Pythians. Its leaves are darkened and stained, and in the published images only some of the pages of this ode can be read with confidence. This entry therefore gives the part of the ode that could be placed line by line, 25-44, which holds the whole description of the Hyperboreans, on fol. 164v (last rows), 165r and 165v. The rest of the ode (1-24 and 45-72) is not included.
