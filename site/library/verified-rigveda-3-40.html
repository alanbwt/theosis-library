Introduction

        
Rigveda 3.40 is a hymn of 9 verses addressed to Indra. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 3, the family book of Viśvāmitra. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 226v-227r. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

            §1-5 · RV 3.40.1-5 (aṣṭaka 3, adhyāya 3, varga 1); Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 fol. 226v
            

              
              
              
            

          


          
          

            
Manuscript

            
            
            
            
Where on the image
Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41, fol. 226v (numbered 36v by the scribe) (OPenn image 228, upper leaf). Checked on the image: the Rigveda text on this page begins in RV 3.40.1 ‹indra tvā vṛṣabham vayam›. Lines 1-3 carry the anukramaṇī for aṣṭaka 3, adhyāya 3; 3.40.1 (indra tvā vṛṣabham vayam) begins in line 3 after oṃ. The next page with Rigveda text, fol. 227r (numbered 37r by the scribe), begins in RV 3.40.6 ‹-pāhi naḥ sutam madhoḥ›. Verses 3.40.1-5 end on this page.

2indra̍ kratu̱vida̍ṁ su̱taṁ soma̍ṁ harya puruṣṭuta | pibā vṛ̍ṣasva̱ tātṛ̍pim ||

4indra̱ somā̍ḥ su̱tā i̱me tava̱ pra ya̍nti satpate | kṣaya̍ṁ ca̱ndrāsa̱ inda̍vaḥ ||

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1THEE, Indra, we invoke, the Bull, what time the Soma is expressed. So drink thou of the savoury juice.
2Indra, whom many laud, accept the strength-conferring Soma juice: Quaff, pour down drink that satisfies.
3Indra, with all the Gods promote our wealth-bestowing sacrifice, Thou highly-lauded Lord of men.
4Lord of the brave, to thee proceed these drops of Soma juice expressed, The bright drops to thy dwelling-place.
5Within thy belly, Indra, take juice, Soma the most excellent: Thine are the drops celestial.

          

        

        
        


          

            §6-9 · RV 3.40.6-9 (aṣṭaka 3, adhyāya 3, varga 2); Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 fol. 227r
            

              
              
              
            

          


          
          

            
Manuscript

            
            
            
            
            
            
Where on the image
Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41, fol. 227r (numbered 37r by the scribe) (OPenn image 228, lower leaf). Checked on the image: the Rigveda text on this page begins in RV 3.40.6 ‹-pāhi naḥ sutam madhoḥ›. The next page with Rigveda text, fol. 227v (numbered 37v by the scribe), begins in RV 3.41.6 ‹-mandasva hi andhasaḥ rādhase›. Verses 3.40.6-9 end on this page; 3.40.6 begins on fol. 226v (numbered 36v by the scribe).

7a̱bhi dyu̱mnāni̍ va̱nina̱ indra̍ṁ sacante̱ akṣi̍tā | pī̱tvī soma̍sya vāvṛdhe ||

7abhi | dyumnāni | vaninaḥ | indram | sacante | akṣitā | pītvī | somasya | vavṛdhe

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
6Drink our libation, Lord of hymns: with streams of meath thou art bedewed Our glory, Indra, is thy gift.
7To Indra go the treasures of the worshipper, which never fail: He drinks the Soma and is strong
8From far away, from near at hand, O Vr̥itra-slayer, come to us: Accept the songs we sing to thee.
9When from the space between the near and far thou art invoked by us, Thence, Indra. come thou hitherward.
