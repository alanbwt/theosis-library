Introduction

        
Stephanus page 25 of Plato's Timaeus (25a-25e): Atlantis, its empire and its defeat by Athens; the island sinks into the sea.



Timaeus 20d-25d is Critias' first account of Atlantis: the story Solon was told by a priest at Sais in Egypt about an Athens of 9,000 years before, which defeated an invading power from the island of Atlantis beyond the Pillars of Heracles, after which the island sank into the sea. Plato returns to it at length in the Critias.



The manuscript is Paris, Bibliothèque nationale de France, grec 1807, known to editors as Plato A. It was copied in Constantinople around 850-875 by the scribe of the so-called philosophical collection, and is the oldest surviving medieval manuscript of the Timaeus and Critias (tetralogies VIII-IX: Clitophon, Republic, Timaeus, Critias, Minos, Laws, Epinomis, Letters). Each page has two columns of 44 lines. The Timaeus fills fol. 114r-144v and the Critias fol. 145r-151r.



On the manuscript this page of text runs over fol. 117r col. b to fol. 117v col. b. Each section below is linked to the folio side(s) that carry it. The mapping comes from reading the first words of every column on the images and finding them in Burnet's text: fol. 117r col. b begins ‹-εξαμένη πρῶτον κατῴκισεν. ᾠκεῖτε δὴ οὖν› (Tim. 24d); fol. 117v col. a begins ‹τῆς δὲ Εὐρώπης μέχρι Τυρρηνίας. αὕτη› (Tim. 25b); fol. 117v col. b begins ‹εἰπεῖν, ἀκήκοας· λέγοντος δὲ δὴ χθὲς› (Tim. 25e).



The Greek is John Burnet's critical text (Oxford, 1902), not a transcription of this manuscript; the scribe's spelling, accents and occasional readings differ from it. The English is R. G. Bury's Loeb translation (1929).
