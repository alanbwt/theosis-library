Introduction

        
The Gospel of Thomas is a collection of 114 sayings attributed to Jesus, introduced as the hidden sayings written down by Didymos Judas Thomas. The only complete copy is the second tractate of Nag Hammadi Codex II, a papyrus book in Coptic copied in the 4th century and found near Nag Hammadi in Upper Egypt in 1945; it is now in the Coptic Museum, Cairo (inv. 10544). Greek fragments of the work survive in three Oxyrhynchus papyri (P. Oxy. 1, 654 and 655), which are not shown here.
This entry covers the sayings 63-64, which begin on codex page 44. The Coptic is given line by line; the small numbers are codex page and line (for example 33.5), so each line can be found on the photograph. Page and line breaks follow the Coptic SCRIPTORIUM edition by Paul Dilley, which he made from the photographs of The Facsimile Edition of the Nag Hammadi Codices, the negatives shown here.
Photograph: Codex II page 44, A-Series negative by Basile Psiroukis, Facsimile Edition p. 54, CCDL nha 2930; Nag Hammadi Archive of the Institute for Antiquity and Christianity, Claremont Colleges Digital Library. Checked on the photograph: page 44 line 1 begins '[ⲛⲁ]ⲙ̣ⲩⲥⲧⲏⲣⲓⲟⲛ̣ ⲡ̣ⲉ̣[ⲧ]ⲉ̣̣ⲧ̣ⲉ̣ⲕ̣⳿ⲟⲩⲛⲁⲙ ⲛⲁⲁϥ' and line 35 reads 'ⲧ̣[ⲉ ⲥⲉⲛⲁⲃ]ⲱ̣ⲕ̣ ⲁⲛ⳿ ⲉϩⲟⲩⲛ⳿ ⲉⲛⲧⲟⲡⲟⲥ ⲙⲡⲁⲓ̈ⲱⲧ⳿' (the top line is damaged at the left; the last line begins after a break).

English · Mark M. Mattison

            
Jesus said, "Someone was planning on having guests. When dinner was ready, they sent their servant to call the visitors.
"The servant went to the first and said, 'My master invites you.'
"They said, 'Some merchants owe me money. They're coming tonight. I need to go and give them instructions. Excuse me from the dinner.'
"The servant went to another one and said, 'My master invites you.'
"They said, "I've just bought a house and am needed for the day. I won't have time.'
"The servant went to another one and said, 'My master invites you.'
"They said, 'My friend is getting married and I'm going to make dinner. I can't come. Excuse me from the dinner.'
"The servant went to another one and said, 'My master invites you.'
"They said, "I've just bought a farm and am going to collect the rent. I can't come. Excuse me.'
"The servant went back and told the master, 'The ones you've invited to the dinner have excused themselves.'
"The master said to their servant, 'Go out to the roads and bring whomever you find so that they can have dinner.'
"Buyers and merchants won't [enter] the places of my Father."

          

        

        
      


    

    

    

      
      

        
Notes

        
    
          
          
  1. 
            Coptic: Coptic SCRIPTORIUM edition (Paul C. Dilley), version 6.2.0, CC BY 4.0, shown in the manuscript's own spelling with words grouped as bound groups. Editorial signs are the edition's: square brackets mark letters lost in a gap (restored by the editor, or dots for the number of lost letters), a dot under a letter marks an uncertain reading, round brackets write out a letter abbreviated by a stroke at a line end, angle brackets mark letters added by the editor, curly brackets letters the editor removes, double square brackets letters erased by the scribe.
          
    2. 
          
          
  2. 
            The small numbers in the Coptic are codex page and line (page.line) as marked in the edition. Where a word is divided between two lines on the papyrus, the edition sometimes keeps the word whole on one line, so a line can begin a few letters earlier or later on the photograph (for example 39.33-34, and the page change inside saying 13).

  3.
