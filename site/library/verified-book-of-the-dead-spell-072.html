Introduction

        
The Book of the Dead, in Egyptian the spells for going forth by day, is a collection of funerary spells written on papyrus rolls and placed in burials from the New Kingdom to the Roman period. Each roll was made for one person, with spells taken from a large repertoire and illustrated with drawings (vignettes). This library gives one complete roll: the papyrus of Iuefankh in the Museo Egizio, Turin (Cat. 1791), written in the Ptolemaic period (332-30 BCE) and coming from Thebes. Richard Lepsius published it in 1842 and divided it into 165 chapters; his numbering is still the standard numbering of the spells, and it is used here.



Spell 72 (Birch: 'The chapter of Coming out from the Day, and of passing through the Gateway.') is on Lepsius's plate XXVII and on photograph 11 of the 31 Museo Egizio photographs of the roll.



The Egyptian is the transliteration of the Thesaurus Linguae Aegyptiae (edition by Burkhard Backes), with the column number of the spell before each column; the numbers are those of Lepsius's plates. On the papyrus the text runs in vertical columns of cursive hieroglyphs from right to left along the roll; titles and some words are written in red. Signs restored by the editor are in square brackets, signs the scribe left out in angle brackets, superfluous signs in braces and damaged signs in half brackets. The English is the translation by Samuel Birch, published in 1867 and made from Lepsius's copy of this papyrus, so the English and the Egyptian come from the same manuscript. It was the first complete English translation of the Book of the Dead; many passages are now understood differently. Birch's own square brackets give his alternatives and explanations. The photographs are by the Museo Egizio; each shows a stretch of the roll, and the note of each section says where the spell stands on it.

      


      

        

          How was this verified? (Provenance)

Images: Museo Egizio, Turin, photographs of Cat. 1791 on Wikimedia Commons (https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Book_of_the_Dead_of_Iuefankh_-_Museo_Egizio_(Turin)_C._1791), e.g. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Book_of_the_Dead_of_Iuefankh,_papyrus_-_Museo_Egizio_Turin_C_1791_p034.jpg (CC0 1.0 (Museo Egizio, as stated on Wikimedia Commons))

            
Text-to-image mapping: Spell order, plate and column numbers from Lepsius, Das Todtenbuch der Aegypter (1842), whose plates reproduce this papyrus and are headed by their chapter numbers; the TLA uses the same numbers. Positions in the notes are measured from the left edge of the photograph.

English · Samuel Birch (1867)

            
LXXII. The chapter of Coming out from the Day, and of passing through the Gateway.
[Vignette. — Deceased adoring Osiris and another God.)
On ye Lords of Truth without fault, who are for ever, cycling for eternity! Let me pass to the earth. I am a Spirit in your changes, 1 prevail through your magic spells, I judge through your judgment; save me from the annihilation of this Region of the Two Truths. Give ye me my mouth to speak by it. Food has been given to me in your presence. Because that I know your names. I know the name of that Great God. Ye give incense to his nostril. Tekem is his name, lie has passed through to the Eastern horizon of the heaven, he has passed through to the Western horizon of the heaven. I go forth swiftly [come near] in turn to his going forth swiftly [coming near].
Rub ye out [my sin] in the Purgatory [Meshd\. The wicked do not prevail against me. Do not turn me out of your doors. Do you shut your doors in my face ? My bread is of Tu, my drink of Tep, or of Aner. My arms are bound in the divine place. My father Turn did it for me, he placed my house above the earth; there are corn and barley in it, unknown is their quantity. I made in it the Festival of passing the Soul to my body. I made in it the Festival of Tum for [is said by] my Soul, for [to] my body. Give ye to me meals of food and drink, oxen, geese, clothes, incense, wax, all good and pure things in which the life of a God consists. I establish myself for ever in my transformations that I choose. I sailed along to the fields of the Aahlu. I departed from the fields of llept [the Pools]. I follow the two Truths. I am the Lions.
This book should be known on earth, and made in writing on the coffin. It is the chapter by which he will leave the day in nil the transformations that he wishes, and goes to his abode not turned away. Food, drink, and slices of flesh will be given to him off the altars of Osiris. He goes to the fields of the Aahlu; corn and barley are given to him there, for he is flourishing as he was on earth, and he does all that he chooses, like the Gods there, in garments of truth millions of times.
