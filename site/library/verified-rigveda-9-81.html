Introduction

        
Rigveda 9.81 is a hymn of 5 verses addressed to Soma. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 9, the hymns to Soma Pavamāna. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 709v-710r. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1ONWARD to Indra's throat move, beauteously adorned, the waves of Soma as he purifies himself, When they, brought forward with the lovely curd of kine, effused, have cheered the Hero to bestow his gifts.
2Hither hath Soma flowed unto the beakers, like a chariot-horse, a stallion swift upon his way. Thus, knowing both the generations, he obtains the rights and dues of Gods from yonder and from hence.
3While thou art cleansed, O Soma, scatter wealth on us; Indu, bestow great bounty as a liberal Prince. Giver of life, with wisdom help to opulence; strew not our home possessions far away from us.
4Hither let Pûshan Pavamâna come to us, Varuṇa, Mitra, bountiful, of one accord, The Maruts, Aṣvins, Vâyu, and Br̥ihaspati, Savitar, Tvashṭar, tractable Sarasvatî.

Manuscript

            
            
            
            
            
            
Where on the image
Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41, fol. 710r (numbered 27r by the scribe) (OPenn image 710, lower leaf). Checked on the image: the Rigveda text on this page begins in RV 9.81.5 ‹uru antarikṣam viśve devāḥ›. English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
5Both Heaven and Earth, the all-invigorating Pair, Vidhâtar, Aditi, and Aryaman the God, Bhaga who blesses men, the spacious Firmament,– let all the Gods in Pavamâna take delight.
