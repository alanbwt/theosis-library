Introduction

        
Rigveda 8.40 is a hymn of 12 verses addressed to Indra and Agni. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 8, the book of the Kaṇva and Aṅgiras poets. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 611v-612v. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1INDRA and Agni, surely ye as Conquerors will give us wealth, Whereby in fight we may o'ercome that which is strong and firmly fixed, as Agni burns the woods with wind. Let all the others die away.
2We set no snares to tangle you; Indra we worship and adore, Hero of heroes mightiest. Once may he come unto us with his Steed, come unto us to win us strength, and to complete the sacrifice.
3For, famous Indra-Agni, ye are dwellers in the midst of frays. Sages in wisdom, ye are knit to him who seeketh you as friends. Heroes, bestow on him his wish.
4Nabhâka-like, with sacred song Indra's and Agni's praise I sing, Theirs to whom all this world belongs, this heaven and this mighty earth which bear rich treasure in their lap.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
5To Indra and to Agni send your prayers, as was Nabhâka's wont,– Who oped with sideway opening the sea with its foundations seven-Indra all powerful in his might.
6Tear thou asunder, as of old, like tangles of a creeping plant, Demolish thou the Dâsa's might. May we with Indra's help divide the treasure he hath gathered up.
7What time with this same song these men call Indra-Agni sundry ways, May we with our own heroes quell those who provoke us to the fight, and conquer those who strive with us.
8The Two refulgent with their beams rise and come downward from the sky. By Indra's and by Agni's hest, flowing away, the rivers, run which they released from their restraint.
9O Indra, many are thine aids, many thy ways of guiding us, Lord of the Bay Steeds, Hinva's Son. To a Good Hero come our prayers, which soon shall have accomplishment.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
10Inspire him with your holy hymns, the Hero bright and glorious, Him who with might demolisbeth even the brood of Ṣushṇa, and winneth for us the heavenly streams.
11Inspire him worshipped with fair rites, the glorious Hero truly brave. He brake in pieces Ṣushṇa's brood who still expected not the stroke, and won for us the heavenly streams. Let all the others die away.
12Thus have we sung anew to Indra-Agni, as sang our sires, Angirases, and Mandhâtar. Guard us with triple shelter and preserve us: may we be masters of a store of riches.
