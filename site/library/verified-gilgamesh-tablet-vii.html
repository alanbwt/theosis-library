Introduction

        
Enkidu dreams that the gods have decided he must die. He curses the door he made from the cedar and the trapper and Shamhat, then relents at Shamash's word. He describes a dream of the Netherworld and dies after a long illness.
The Standard Babylonian version of the epic, traditionally ascribed to the scholar Sin-leqi-unninni, was written on twelve tablets. Most surviving copies come from the libraries of Ashurbanipal at Nineveh (7th century BCE) and from Babylon and other Babylonian cities (7th-2nd century BCE). This page gives Tablet VII in the line numbering of the electronic Babylonian Library edition by A. R. George (2022 and later revisions).
How this page is built. Each section is a run of consecutive lines as written on one photographed clay tablet. For every line the eBL edition (https://www.ebl.lmu.de/corpus/L/1/4/SB/VII) lists the manuscripts that carry it, with each tablet's own column and line numbers; the tablet chosen for each run is the photographed manuscript with the most signs preserved there. The original text is eBL's transliteration of that tablet's signs (not a modern composite text): square brackets mark broken signs, x an illegible sign, # a damaged sign, ? an uncertain reading, and words in {braces} are determinatives. Superscript numbers are the line numbers of the eBL edition, used in both the Akkadian and the English. The English is A. R. George's translation in the eBL edition (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), one line of English per line of the poem; italics mark uncertain renderings, as in the source.
Finding a line on the photograph. Each section names the tablet, the face and column, and the tablet's own line numbers (a prime, as in 5', counts from the first preserved line of a broken column). On a tablet with six columns the obverse carries columns i-iii from left to right; the reverse, turned over from bottom to top, carries columns iv-vi from right to left. The photographs often show the obverse above the reverse with the edges around them; a fragment preserves only part of this layout.
This page shows 202 of the 219 lines of the eBL edition (92%), from 14 tablets in the British Museum: K.4579.A (Nineveh), K.3588 (Nineveh), Sm.2132 (Nineveh), Rm-II.399 (Nineveh), BM.46002, K.2589 (Nineveh), BM.34423, K.11659 (Nineveh), K.3389 (Nineveh), BM.35245, K.9196 (Nineveh), K.8590 (Nineveh), 1879,0708.335 (Nineveh), 1879,0708.320 (Nineveh).
Lines not shown. 17 of the 219 lines of the eBL edition have no witness here with an open photograph: 51-55 (only on SU-1951.129.A); 82-90 (only on SU-1951.129.A); 124-125 (no manuscript preserves it); 298 (no manuscript preserves it). Lines that survive only on tablets whose photographs are not openly licensed (for example in the Iraq Museum, Berlin, Istanbul, Ankara, Philadelphia or private collections), or only on tablets whose eBL photograph does not show the lines, are omitted.
