Introduction

        
Rigveda 3.59 is a hymn of 9 verses addressed to Mitra. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 3, the family book of Viśvāmitra. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 246v-247v. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1MITRA, when speaking, stirreth men to labour: Mitra sustaineth both the earth and heaven. Mitra beholdeth men with eyes that close not. To Mitra bring, with holy oil, oblation.
2Foremost be he who brings thee food, O Mitra, who strives to keep thy sacred Law, Âditya. He whom thou helpest ne'er is slain or conquered, on him, from near or far, falls no affliction.
3joying in sacred food and free from sickness, with knees bent lowly on the earth's broad surface, Following closely the Âditya's statute, may we remain in Mitra's gracious favour.
4Auspicious and adorable, this Mitra was born with fair dominion, King, Disposer. May we enjoy the grace of him the Holy, yea, rest in his propitious loving-kindness.
5The great Âditya, to be served with wor. ship, who stirreth men, is gracious to the singer. To Mitra, him most highly to be lauded, offer in fire oblation that he loveth.

7a̱bhi yo ma̍hi̱nā diva̍m mi̱tro ba̱bhūva̍ sa̱prathā̍ḥ | a̱bhi śravo̍bhiḥ pṛthi̱vīm ||

7abhi | yaḥ | mahinā | divam | mitraḥ | babhūva | sa-prathāḥ | abhi | śravaḥ-bhiḥ | pṛthivīm

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
6The gainful grace of Mitra,. God, supporter of the race of man, Gives splendour of most.glorious fame.
7Mitra whose glory spreads afar, he who in might surpasses heaven, Surpasses earth in his renown.
8All the Five Races have repaired to Mitra, ever strong to aid, For he sustaineth all the Gods.
9Mitra to Gods, to living men, to him who strews the holy grass, Gives food fulfilling sacred Law.
