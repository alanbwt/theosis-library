Introduction

        
Rigveda 10.52 is a hymn of 6 verses addressed to continuation of the dialogue. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 10, the last and latest book. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 48 (University of Pennsylvania), a paper manuscript of the eighth aṣṭaka (RV 10.46-10.191) of the Ṛgveda Saṃhitā in Devanāgarī, 158 leaves, 6 lines to the page, with the accents marked in red; the scribe's colophon on fol. 158 gives Saṃvat 1768 (1711 CE); this hymn stands on fol. 8v-9r. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Like most Rigveda manuscripts, Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 48 writes a repeated refrain in short form after its first occurrence (for example kasmai° for kasmai devāya haviṣā vidhema); the Sanskrit shown is the full text of Aufrecht's edition.

Vedic Sanskrit · Aufrecht (accented)

                    
1viśve̍ devāḥ śā̱stana̍ mā̱ yathe̱ha hotā̍ vṛ̱to ma̱navai̱ yan ni̱ṣadya̍ | pra me̍ brūta bhāga̱dheya̱ṁ yathā̍ vo̱ yena̍ pa̱thā ha̱vyam ā vo̱ vahā̍ni ||
2a̱haṁ hotā̱ ny a̍sīda̱ṁ yajī̍yā̱n viśve̍ de̱vā ma̱ruto̍ mā junanti | aha̍r-ahar aśvi̱nādhva̍ryavaṁ vām bra̱hmā sa̱mid bha̍vati̱ sāhu̍tir vām ||

            
          


          

            
English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1INSTRUCT me, all ye Gods, how I, elected your Priest, must seat me here, and how address you. Instruct me how to deal to each his portion, and by what ' path to bring you man's oblation.
2I sit as Priest most skilled in sacrificing: the Maruts and all Deities impel me. Aṣvins, each day yours is the Adhvaryu's duty: Brahman and wood are here: 'tis yours to offer.

Vedic Sanskrit · Aufrecht (accented)

                    
3a̱yaṁ yo hotā̱ kir u̱ sa ya̱masya̱ kam apy ū̍he̱ yat sa̍ma̱ñjanti̍ de̱vāḥ | aha̍r-ahar jāyate mā̱si-mā̱sy athā̍ de̱vā da̍dhire havya̱vāha̍m ||
4māṁ de̱vā da̍dhire havya̱vāha̱m apa̍mluktam ba̱hu kṛ̱cchrā cara̍ntam | a̱gnir vi̱dvān ya̱jñaṁ na̍ḥ kalpayāti̱ pañca̍yāmaṁ tri̱vṛta̍ṁ sa̱ptata̍ntum ||
5ā vo̍ yakṣy amṛta̱tvaṁ su̱vīra̱ṁ yathā̍ vo devā̱ vari̍va̱ḥ karā̍ṇi | ā bā̱hvor vajra̱m indra̍sya dheyā̱m athe̱mā viśvā̱ḥ pṛta̍nā jayāti ||
6trīṇi̍ śa̱tā trī sa̱hasrā̍ṇy a̱gniṁ tri̱ṁśac ca̍ de̱vā nava̍ cāsaparyan | aukṣa̍n ghṛ̱tair astṛ̍ṇan ba̱rhir a̍smā̱ ād id dhotā̍ra̱ṁ ny a̍sādayanta ||

            
          


          

            
English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
3Who is the Priest? Is he the Priest of Yama? On whom is thrust this God-appointed honour? He springs to life each month, each day that passes; so Gods have made him their oblation-bearer.
4The Gods have made me bearer of oblations, who slipped away and passed through many troubles. Wise Agni shall ordain for us the worship, whether five-wayed, threefold, or seven-threaded.
5So will I win you strength and life for ever. O Gods, that I may give you room and freedom. To Indra's arms would I consign the thunder; in all these battles shall he then be victor.
6The Deities three hundred and thirty-nine, have served and honoured Agni, Strewn sacred grass, anointed him with butter, and seated him as Priest, the Gods' Invoker.
