Introduction

        
Rigveda 8.15 is a hymn of 13 verses addressed to Indra. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 8, the book of the Kaṇva and Aṅgiras poets. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 584r-584v. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1SING forth to him whom many men invoke, to him whom many laud. Invite the powerful Indra with your songs of praise.
2Whose lofty might-for doubly strong is he-supports the heavens and earth, And hills and plains and floods and light with manly power.
3Such, Praised by many! thou art King alone thou smitest Vr̥itras dead, To gain, O Indra, spoils of war and high renown.
4We sing this strong and wild delight of thine which conquers in the fray, Which, Caster of the Stone! gives room and shines like gold.
5Wherewith thou also foundest lights for Âyu and for Manu's sake: Now joying in this sacred grass thou beamest forth.
6This day too singers of the hymn praise, as of old, this might of thine: Win thou the waters day by day, thralls of the strong.
7That lofty Indra-power of thine, thy strength and thine intelligence, Thy thunderbolt for which we long, the wish makes keen.
8O Indra, Heaven and Earth augment thy manly power and thy renown; The waters and the mountains stir and urge thee on.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
9Vishṇu the lofty ruling Power, Varuṇa, Mitra sing thy praise: In thee the Marut3' company have great delight.
10O Indra, thou wast born the Lord of men, most liberal of thy gifts: Excellent deeds for evermore are all thine own.
11Ever, alone, O highly-praised, thou sendest Vr̥itras to their rest: None else than Indra executes the mighty deed.
12Though here and there, in varied hymns, Indra, men call on thee for aid, Still with our heroes fight and win the light of heaven.
13Already have all forms of him entered our spacious dwelling-place: For victory stir thou Indra, up, the Lord of Might.
