Introduction

        
Rigveda 4.39 is a hymn of 6 verses addressed to Dadhikra. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 4, the family book of Vāmadeva. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 300v-301r. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1Now give we praise to Dadhikrâs the rapid, and mention in our laud the Earth and Heaven. May the Dawns flushing move me to exertion, and bear me safely over every trouble.
2I praise the mighty Steed who fills my spirit, the Stallion Dadhikrâvan rich in bounties, Whom, swift of foot and shining bright as Agni, ye, Varuṇa and Mitra, gave to Pûrus.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
3Him who hath honoured, when the flame is kindled at break of dawn, the Courser Dadhikrâvan, Him, of one mind with Varuṇa and Mitra may Aditi make free from all transgression.
4When we remember mighty Dadhikrâvan our food and strength, then the blest name of Maruts, Varuṇa, Mitra, we invoke for welfare, and Agni, and the thunder-wielding Indra.
5Both sides invoke him as they call on Indra when they stir forth and turn to sacrificing. To us have Varuṇa and Mitra granted the Courser Dadhikrâs, a guide for mortals.
6So have I glorified with praise strong Dadhikrâvan, conquering Steed. Sweet may he make our mouths; may he prolong the days we have to live.
