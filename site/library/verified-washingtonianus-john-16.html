Introduction

        
John 16 from Codex Washingtonianus (the Freer Gospels, Gregory-Aland 032, Freer Gallery of Art F1906.274), a parchment codex of the four Gospels written in Egypt in the late 4th or early 5th century and bought by Charles Lang Freer from the dealer Ali Arabi at Giza in 1906. It is one of the oldest nearly complete Gospel books in existence. The Gospels stand in the so-called Western order, Matthew, John, Luke, Mark, and the text is famously block-mixed: the copyist drew on several exemplars of different textual character, so the text type changes in blocks within a single Gospel. After Mark 16:14 the codex carries the Freer Logion, a saying found in no other manuscript. Two passages are lost to damage: Mark 15:13-37 and John 14:26-16:6.

Where a verse begins on one page and ends on the next, both pages are listed. For this chapter the page of each verse is taken from the transcription itself, which records where every page begins; CSNTM's index confirms which photograph shows that page.
  2. Greek: the manuscript's own text, from a diplomatic transcription (see Greek text below), with Tischendorf's 8th critical edition (1869) for comparison.
  3. English: the King James Version (1611, public domain), verbatim from bible-api.com. The KJV translates the later Byzantine text, so it will not always match the Greek shown or the reading of the codex.
Not in the codex (lost to damage): John 16:1-6.
Greek text: a diplomatic transcription of Codex Washingtonianus made by the International Greek New Testament Project (IGNTP), transcription of John in GA 032, version 2.1 (2013), published under a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 licence. It gives the letters as the first scribe wrote them: no accents or breathings, the scribe's own spelling, and the sacred names in their contracted form (θς for θεος, ις for ιησους, κς for κυριος; the overline is not reproduced). Corrections by later hands are not shown. Letters in square brackets are not legible on the page and were restored by the transcribers, a dot under a letter marks an uncertain reading, and [...] marks text lost to damage. Beneath each section, for comparison, is Tischendorf's 8th critical edition (1869). Where the two differ, the transcription is what the manuscript says. Each section is shown with the page on which the transcription places its verses.

English · King James Version (1611)

                    
7Nevertheless I tell you the truth; It is expedient for you that I go away: for if I go not away, the Comforter will not come unto you; but if I depart, I will send him unto you. 8And when he is come, he will reprove the world of sin, and of righteousness, and of judgment: 9Of sin, because they believe not on me; 10Of righteousness, because I go to my Father, and ye see me no more; 11Of judgment, because the prince of this world is judged. 12I have yet many things to say unto you, but ye cannot bear them now. 13Howbeit when he, the Spirit of truth, is come, he will guide you into all truth: for he shall not speak of himself; but whatsoever he shall hear,
that shall he speak: and he will shew you things to come.

English · King James Version (1611)

                    
20Verily, verily, I say unto you, That ye shall weep and lament, but the world shall rejoice: and ye shall be sorrowful, but your sorrow shall be turned into joy. 21A woman when she is in travail hath sorrow, because her hour is come: but as soon as she is delivered of the child, she remembereth no more the anguish, for joy that a man is born into the world. 22And ye now therefore have sorrow: but I will see you again, and your heart shall rejoice, and your joy no man taketh from you. 23And in that day ye shall ask me nothing. Verily, verily, I say unto you, Whatsoever ye shall ask the Father in my name, he will give
it
 you. 24Hitherto have ye asked nothing in my name: ask, and ye shall receive, that your joy may be full. 25These things have I spoken unto you in proverbs: but the time cometh, when I shall no more speak unto you in proverbs, but I shall shew you plainly of the Father. 26At that day ye shall ask in my name: and I say not unto you, that I will pray the Father for you: 27For the Father himself loveth you, because ye have loved me, and have believed that I came out from God.
