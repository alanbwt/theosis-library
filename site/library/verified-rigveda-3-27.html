Introduction

        
Rigveda 3.27 is a hymn of 15 verses addressed to Agni. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 3, the family book of Viśvāmitra. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 206v-207v. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1IN ladle dropping oil your food goes in oblation up to heaven, Goes to the Gods in search of bliss.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
2Agni I laud, the Sage inspired, crowner of sacrifice through song, Who listens and gives bounteous gifts.
3O Agni, if we might obtain control of thee the potent God, Then should we overcome our foes.
4Kindled at sacrifices he is Agni, hallower, meet for praise, With flame for hair: to him we seek.
5Immortal Agni, shining far, enrobed with oil, well worshipped, bears The gifts of sacrifice away.
6The priests with ladles lifted up, worshipping here with holy thought, Have brought this Agni for our aid.
7Immortal, Sacrificer, God, with wondrous power he leads the way, Urging the great assembly on.
8Strong, he is set on deeds ofstrength. In sacrifices led in front, As Singer he completes the rite.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
9Excellent, he was made by thought. The Germ of beings have I gained, Yea, and die Sire of active strength.
10Thee have I stablished, Excellent, O strengthened by the sage's prayer, Thee, Agni, longing, nobly bright.
11Agni, the swift and active One, singers, at time of sacrifice, Eagerly kindle with their food.
12Agni the Son of Strength who shines up to the heaven in solemn rites, The wise of heart, I glorify.
13Meet to be lauded and adored, showing in beauty through the dark, Agni, the Strong, is kindled well.
14Agni is kindled as a bull, like a horsebearer of the Gods: Men with oblations worship him.
15Thee will we kindle as a bull, we who are Bulls ourselves, O Bull. Thee, Agni, shining mightily.
