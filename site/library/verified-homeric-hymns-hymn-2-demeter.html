Introduction

        
The Homeric Hymns are 33 hexameter poems to the Greek gods, composed by anonymous poets between about the 7th and the 5th century BCE and handed down under Homer's name. This entry: Homeric Hymn 2, To Demeter. Hades carries off Persephone; Demeter searches for her, nurses the child Demophon at Eleusis, withholds the harvest, and wins her daughter back for two thirds of each year. The hymn ends with the founding of the Eleusinian Mysteries. This manuscript is its only witness.
This collection has Hymns 1-18 only. The manuscript shown, M, breaks off at Hymn 18, line 4 (fol. 50r; fol. 50v is blank), so the rest of Hymn 18 (lines 5-12) and Hymns 19-33 (to Pan, Hephaestus, Apollo, Poseidon, Zeus, Hestia, the Muses and Apollo, Dionysus, Artemis, Athena, Hestia, Earth, Helios, Selene and the Dioscuri) are not in it and are not given here.

English · H. G. Evelyn-White (1914)

            
1I begin to sing of rich-haired Demeter, awful goddess —of her and her trim-ankled daughter whom Aidoneus rapt away, given to him by all-seeing Zeus the loud-thunderer. Apart from Demeter, lady of the golden sword and glorious fruits, 5she was playing with the deep-bosomed daughters of Oceanus and gathering flowers over a soft meadow, roses and crocuses and beautiful violets, irises also and hyacinths and the narcissus, which Earth made to grow at the will of Zeus and to please the Host of Many, to be a snare for the bloom-like girl — 10a marvellous, radiant flower. It was a thing of awe whether for deathless gods or mortal men to see: from its root grew a hundred blooms and it smelled most sweetly, so that all wide heaven above and the whole earth and the sea's salt swell laughed for joy.
