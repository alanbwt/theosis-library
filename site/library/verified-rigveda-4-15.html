Introduction

        
Rigveda 4.15 is a hymn of 10 verses addressed to Agni. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 4, the family book of Vāmadeva. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 268v-269v. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1AGNI the Herald, like a horse, is led forth at our solemn rite, God among Gods adorable.
2Three times unto our solemn rite comes Agni like a charioteer, Bearing the viands to the Gods.
3Round the oblations hath he paced, Agni the Wise, the Lord of Strength, Giving the offerer precious boons.
4He who is kindled eastward for Sr̥injaya, Devavâta's son, Resplendent, tamer of the foe.
5So mighty be the Agni whom the mortal hero shall command, With sharpened teeth and bountiful.
6Day after day they dress him, as they clean a horse who wins the prize. Dress the red Scion of the Sky.
7When Sahadeva's princely son with two bay horses thought of me, Summoned by him I drew not back.
8And truly those two noble bays I straightway took when offered me, From Sahadeva's princely son.

          

        

        
        


          

            §9-10 · RV 4.15.9-10 (aṣṭaka 3, adhyāya 5, varga 16); Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 fol. 269v
            

              
              
              
            

          


          
          

            
Manuscript

            
            
            
            
Where on the image
Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41, fol. 269v (numbered 78v by the scribe) (OPenn image 270, upper leaf). Checked on the image: the Rigveda text on this page begins in RV 4.15.9 ‹eṣaḥ vām devau aśvinā›. The next page with Rigveda text, fol. 270r (numbered 79r by the scribe), begins in RV 4.16.4 ‹vedi su-dṛśīkam arkaiḥ mahi›. Verses 4.15.9-10 end on this page.

10taṁ yu̱vaṁ de̍vāv aśvinā kumā̱raṁ sā̍hade̱vyam | dī̱rghāyu̍ṣaṁ kṛṇotana ||

            
            

              Compare: Padapāṭha (word-by-word text written in the manuscript; Sansknet e-text via GRETIL)

10tam | yuvam | devau | aśvinā | kumāram | sāha-devyam | dīrgha-āyuṣam | kṛṇotana

            

            
          


          

            
English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
9Long, O ye Aṣvins, may he live, your care, ye Gods, the princely son. Of Sahadeva, Somaka.
10Cause him the youthful prince, the son of Sahadeva, to enjoy Long life, O Aṣvins, O ye Gods.
