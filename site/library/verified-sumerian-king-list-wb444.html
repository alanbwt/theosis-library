Introduction

        
The Sumerian King List names the cities that held kingship in Sumer and the kings who ruled in each, from the time when 'the kingship descended from heaven' to the dynasty of Isin. It begins with eight kings in five cities who ruled for 241,200 years before 'the flood swept over', then lists the dynasties after the Flood, starting with Kish. The list was compiled from older sources and survives on many Old Babylonian manuscripts.
The Weld-Blundell Prism (Ashmolean Museum AN1923.444, W-B 444) is the most complete copy. It is a four-sided clay prism with two columns of text on each face, eight columns in all, written in the Old Babylonian period. The last king it names is Sin-magir of Isin, so it was written in his reign or soon after (about 1800 BCE). A hole runs through the prism from top to bottom, probably for a spindle so that it could be turned. S. Langdon published it in 1923 (OECT 2).
Each section below is one paragraph of the ETCSL translation, headed by its composite line range. The Sumerian is the transliteration of the signs on this prism (CDLI), with each prism line numbered by the composite line it matches and grouped under the face and column where it stands. The photograph for each section is the face of the prism that carries it. The English is the ETCSL translation of the composite text: its numbers follow this prism unless a note names another manuscript.
Gaps. The prism has broken areas at the top of several columns (bracketed in the transliteration). The closing summary of the list (ETCSL lines 378-431, totals of kings and years for each city) is not on this prism and is not shown.

In Zimbir, En-men-dur-ana became king; he ruled for 21000 years. 1 king; he ruled for 21000 years. Then Zimbir fell (?) and the kingship was taken to Šuruppag. In Šuruppag, Ubara-Tutu became king; he ruled for 18600 years. 1 king; he ruled for 18600 years. In 5 cities 8 kings; they ruled for 241200 years. Then the flood swept over.
