Introduction

        
A hymn to Marduk, whose anger is fearful and whose mercy is great. The speaker tells how his gods abandoned him, omens turned against him, the king turned away, and courtiers plotted against him.
Ludlul bel nemeqi ('I will praise the lord of wisdom'), the Poem of the Righteous Sufferer, is a monologue in five tablets by Shubshi-meshre-Shakkan, a nobleman who lost the favour of his king and his gods, was struck by illness, and was restored by Marduk. It was composed in the late second millennium BCE and copied widely in the first millennium. This page follows the line numbering of the electronic Babylonian Library edition.
How this page is built. Each section is a run of consecutive lines as written on one photographed clay tablet. For every line the eBL edition (https://www.ebl.lmu.de/corpus/L/2/2/SB/I) lists the manuscripts that carry it, with each tablet's own column and line numbers; the tablet chosen for each run is the photographed copy of the work with the most signs preserved there. Commentaries and texts that only quote a line are never chosen. School and excerpt tablets are used only for lines that no photographed library copy preserves as well, and only where they carry the line as text; their sections say so. The original text is eBL's transliteration of that tablet's signs (not a modern composite text): square brackets mark broken signs, x an illegible sign, # a damaged sign, ? an uncertain reading, and words in {braces} are determinatives; the notes give a key to every sign used on this page, and eBL's layout codes are written out in words (for example '(line break)' or '(blank space)'). Superscript numbers are the line numbers of the eBL edition, used in both the Akkadian and the English. The English is B. R. Foster's translation in the eBL edition (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), one line of English per line of the text; italics mark uncertain renderings, as in the source. Where the tablet is broken, the English still translates the whole line of eBL's composite text; each section's note names the lines that are broken on its tablet.
Finding a line on the photograph. Each section names the tablet, the face and column, and the tablet's own line numbers (a prime, as in 5', counts from the first preserved line of a broken column). On a tablet with six columns the obverse carries columns i-iii from left to right; the reverse, turned over from bottom to top, carries columns iv-vi from right to left. The photographs often show the obverse above the reverse with the edges around them; a fragment preserves only part of this layout.
This page shows 117 of the 120 lines of the eBL edition (98%), from 8 tablets in the British Museum and Iraq Museum: K.9392 (Nineveh), BM.36320, IM.67628 (Kalḫu), K.10503 (Nineveh), K.9237 (Nineveh), 1880,0617.2642, BM.38790, BM.32208.
Lines not shown. 3 of the 120 lines of the eBL edition are not shown, because no photographed copy of the work used here carries them: 85 (no photograph in eBL: VAT.11100 (Vorderasiatisches Museum, Berlin), SU-1951.10 (Şanlıurfa Museum), BM.73592, VAT.10756 (Vorderasiatisches Museum, Berlin); not in the eBL Fragmentarium (no photograph there): IM.132669; its photograph shows only part of this face, and the lines cannot be located on it: IM.67628); 86 (no photograph in eBL: VAT.11100 (Vorderasiatisches Museum, Berlin), SU-1951.10 (Şanlıurfa Museum), BM.73592; not in the eBL Fragmentarium (no photograph there): IM.132669; commentary: K.3291; quotation in another text: BM.41286); 92 (no photograph in eBL: VAT.11100 (Vorderasiatisches Museum, Berlin), SU-1951.10 (Şanlıurfa Museum), BM.73592, BM.61433; not in the eBL Fragmentarium (no photograph there): IM.132669; its photograph shows only part of this face, and the lines cannot be located on it: IM.67628). A line is shown only on a photographed tablet that carries it as text. Commentaries and other texts that quote a line (letters, rituals, god lists) are not used as witnesses, because they carry it with explanations or inside another text; tablets without a photograph in eBL are not used, because the line could not be shown on its tablet.

1I will praise the lord of wisdom, solicitous god,
