Introduction

        
Rigveda 5.46 is a hymn of 8 verses addressed to all the gods. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 5, the family book of the Atri clan. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 364r-365r. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

1hayo̱ na vi̱dvām̐ a̍yuji sva̱yaṁ dhu̱ri tāṁ va̍hāmi pra̱tara̍ṇīm ava̱syuva̍m | nāsyā̍ vaśmi vi̱muca̱ṁ nāvṛta̱m puna̍r vi̱dvān pa̱thaḥ pu̍rae̱ta ṛ̱ju ne̍ṣati ||

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1WELL knowing I have bound me, horselike, to the pole: I carry that which bears as on and gives us help. I seek for no release, no turning back therefrom. May he who knows the way, the Leader, guide me straight.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
2O Agni, Indra, Varuṇa, and Mitra, give, O ye Gods, and Marut host, and Vishṇu. May both Nâsatyas, Rudra, heavenly Matrons, Pûshan, Sarasvatî, Bhaga, accept us.
3Indra and Agni, Mitra, Varuṇa, Aditi, the Waters, Mountains, Maruts, Sky, and Earth and Heaven, Vishṇu I call, Pûshan, and Brahmaṇaspati, and Bhaga, Ṣaṅsa, Savitar that they may help.
4May Vishṇu also and Vâta who injures none, and Soma granter of possessions give us joy; And may the R̥ibhus and the Aṣvins, Tvashṭar and Vibhvan remember us so that we may have wealth.
5So may the band of Maruts dwelling in the sky, the holy, come to us to sit on sacred grass; Br̥ihaspati and Pûshan grant us sure defence, Varuṇa, Mitra, Aryaman guard and shelter us.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
6And may the Mountains famed in noble eulogies, and the fair-gleaming Rivers keep us safe from harm. May Bhaga the Dispenser come with power and grace, and far-pervading Aditi listen to my call.
7May the Gods' Spouses aid us of their own freewill, aid us to offspring and the winning of the spoil. Grant us protection, O ye gracious Goddesses, ye who are on the earth or in the waters' realm.
8May the Dames, wives of Gods, enjoy our presents, Râṭ, Aṣvinî, Agnâyî, and Indrâṇî. May Rodasî and Varuṇânî hear us, and Goddesses come at the Matrons' season.
