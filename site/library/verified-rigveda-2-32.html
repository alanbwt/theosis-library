Introduction

        
Rigveda 2.32 is a hymn of 8 verses addressed to diverse gods. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 2, the family book of Gṛtsamada. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 174r-174v. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1GRACIOUSLY further, O ye Heaven and Earth, this speech striving to win reward, of me your worshipper. First rank I give to you, Immortal, high extolled! I, fain to win me wealth, to you the mighty Pair.
2Let not man's guile annoy us, secret or by day: give not us up a prey to these calamities. Sever not thou our friendship: think thereon for us. This, with a heart that longs for bliss, we seek from thee.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
3Bring hither with benignant mind the willing Cow teeming with plenteous milk, full, inexhaustible. O thou invoked by many, day by day I urge thee with my word, a charger rapid in his tread.
4With eulogy I call on Râkâ swift to hear may she, auspicious, hear us, and herself observe. With never-breaking needle may she sew her work, and give a hero son most wealthy, meet for praise.
5All thy kind thoughts, O Râkâ, lovely in their form, wherewith thou grantest wealth to him who offers gifts- With these come thou to us this day benevolent, O Blessed One, bestowing food of thousand sorts.
6O broad-tressed Sinîvâlî, thou who art the Sister of the Gods, Accept the offered sacrifice, and, Goddess, grant us progeny.
7With lovely fingers, lovely arms, prolific Mother of many sons- Present the sacred gifts to her, to Sinîvâlî Queen of men.
8Her, Sinîvâlî, her, Gungû, her, Râkâ, her, Sarasvatî, Indrâṇî to mine aid I call, and Varuṇânî for my weal.
