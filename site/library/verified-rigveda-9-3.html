Introduction

        
Rigveda 9.3 is a hymn of 10 verses addressed to Soma. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 9, the hymns to Soma Pavamāna. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 668r-668v. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
2This God, made ready with the hymn, runs swiftly through the winding ways, Inviolable as he flows.
3This God while flowing is adorned, like a bay steed for war, by men Devout and skilled in holy songs.
4He, like a warrior going forth with heroes, as he flows along Is fain to win all precious boons.
5This God, as he is flowing on, speeds like a car and gives his gifts: He lets his voice be heard of all
6Praised by the sacred bards, this God dives into waters, and bestows Rich gifts upon the worshipper.
7Away he rushes with his stream, across the regions, into heaven, And roars as he is flowing on.
8While flowing, meet for sacrifice, he hath gone up to heaven across The regions, irresistible.
9After the 'way of ancient time, this God, pressed out for Deities, Flows tawny to the straining-cloth.
10This Lord of many Holy Laws, even at his birth engendering strength, Effused, flows onward in a stream.
