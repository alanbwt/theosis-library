Introduction

        
Rigveda 8.49 is a hymn of 10 verses addressed to Indra. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 8, the book of the Kaṇva and Aṅgiras poets. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 624r-625r. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

Vedic Sanskrit · Aufrecht (accented)

                    
1a̱bhi pra va̍ḥ su̱rādha̍sa̱m indra̍m arca̱ yathā̍ vi̱de | yo ja̍ri̱tṛbhyo̍ ma̱ghavā̍ purū̱vasu̍ḥ sa̱hasre̍ṇeva̱ śikṣa̍ti ||

            
            

              Compare: Padapāṭha (word-by-word text written in the manuscript; Sansknet e-text via GRETIL)
              
        
1abhi | pra | vaḥ | su-rādhasam | indram | arca | yathā | vide | yaḥ | jaritṛ-bhyaḥ | magha-vā | puru-vasuḥ | sahasreṇa-iva | śikṣati

            

            
          


          

            
English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1TO you will I sing Indra's praise who gives good gifts as well we know; The praise of Maghavan who, rich in treasure, aids his singers with wealth thousandfold.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
2As with a hundred hosts, he rushes boldly on, and for the offerer slays his foes. As from a mountain flow the water-brooks, thus flow his gifts who feedeth many a one.
3The drops effused, the gladdening draughts, O Indra, Lover of the Son As waters seek the lake where they are wont to rest, fill thee, for bounty, Thunderer.
4The matchless draught that strengthens and gives eloquence, the sweetest of the meath drink thou, That in thy joy thou mayst scatter thy gifts o'er us, plenteously, even as the dust.
5Come quickly to our laud, urged on by Soma-pressers like a horse- Laud, Godlike Indra, which milch-kine make sweet for thee: with Kaṇva's sons are gifts for thee.
6With homage have we sought thee as a Hero, strong, preeminent, with unfailing wealth. O Thunderer, as a plenteous spring pours forth its stream, so, Indra, flow our songs to thee.
7If now thou art at sacrifice, or if thou art upon the earth, Come thence, high-thoughted! to our sacrifice with the Swift, come, Mighty with the Mighty Ones.
8The active, fleet-foot, tawny Coursers that are thine are swift to victory, like the Wind, Wherewith thou goest round to visit Manus' seed, wherewith all heaven is visible.

9e̱tāva̍tas ta īmaha̱ indra̍ su̱mnasya̱ goma̍taḥ | yathā̱ prāvo̍ maghava̱n medhyā̍tithi̱ṁ yathā̱ nīpā̍tithi̱ṁ dhane̍ ||
10yathā̱ kaṇve̍ maghavan tra̱sada̍syavi̱ yathā̍ pa̱kthe daśa̍vraje | yathā̱ gośa̍rye̱ asa̍nor ṛ̱jiśva̱nīndra̱ goma̱d dhira̍ṇyavat ||

            
            

              Compare: Padapāṭha (word-by-word text written in the manuscript; Sansknet e-text via GRETIL)
              
        
9etāvataḥ | te | īmahe | indra | sumnasya | go--mataḥ | yathā | pra | āvaḥ | magha-van | medhya-atithim | yathā | nīpa-atithim | dhane
10yathā | kaṇve | magha-van | trasadasyavi | yathā | pakthe | daśa-vraje | yathā | go--śarye | asanoḥ | ṛjiśvani | indra | go--mat | hiraṇya-vat

            

            
          


          

            
English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
9Indra, from thee so great we crave prosperity in wealth of kine, As, Maghavan, thou favouredst Medhyâtithi, and, in the fight, Nîpâtithi.
10As, Maghavan, to Kaṇva, Trasadasyu, and to Paktha and Daṣavraja; As, Indra, to Goṣarya and R̥ijiṣvan, thou vouchsafedst wealth in kine and gold.
