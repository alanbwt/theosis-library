Introduction

        
Rigveda 10.139 is a hymn of 6 verses addressed to Surya. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 10, the last and latest book. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 48 (University of Pennsylvania), a paper manuscript of the eighth aṣṭaka (RV 10.46-10.191) of the Ṛgveda Saṃhitā in Devanāgarī, 158 leaves, 6 lines to the page, with the accents marked in red; the scribe's colophon on fol. 158 gives Saṃvat 1768 (1711 CE); this hymn stands on fol. 126v-127r. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Like most Rigveda manuscripts, Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 48 writes a repeated refrain in short form after its first occurrence (for example kasmai° for kasmai devāya haviṣā vidhema); the Sanskrit shown is the full text of Aufrecht's edition.

Vedic Sanskrit · Aufrecht (accented)

                    
1sūrya̍raśmi̱r hari̍keśaḥ pu̱rastā̍t savi̱tā jyoti̱r ud a̍yā̱m̐ aja̍sram | tasya̍ pū̱ṣā pra̍sa̱ve yā̍ti vi̱dvān sa̱mpaśya̱n viśvā̱ bhuva̍nāni go̱pāḥ ||
2nṛ̱cakṣā̍ e̱ṣa di̱vo madhya̍ āsta āpapri̱vān roda̍sī a̱ntari̍kṣam | sa vi̱śvācī̍r a̱bhi ca̍ṣṭe ghṛ̱tācī̍r anta̱rā pūrva̱m apa̍raṁ ca ke̱tum ||
3rā̱yo bu̱dhnaḥ sa̱ṁgama̍no̱ vasū̍nā̱ṁ viśvā̍ rū̱pābhi ca̍ṣṭe̱ śacī̍bhiḥ | de̱va i̍va savi̱tā sa̱tyadha̱rmendro̱ na ta̍sthau sama̱re dhanā̍nām ||

            
          


          

            
English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1SAVITAR, golden-haired, hath lifted eastward, bright With the sunbeams, his eternal lustre; He in whose energy wise Pûshan marches, surveying all existence like a herdsman.
2Beholding men he sits amid the heaven filling the two world-halves and air's wide region. He looks upon the rich far-spreading pastures between the eastern and the western limit.
3He, root of wealth, the gatherer-up of treasures, looks with his might on every form and figure. Savitar, like a God.whose Law is constant, stands in the battle for the spoil like Indra.

Vedic Sanskrit · Aufrecht (accented)

                    
4vi̱śvāva̍suṁ soma gandha̱rvam āpo̍ dadṛ̱śuṣī̱s tad ṛ̱tenā̱ vy ā̍yan | tad a̱nvavai̱d indro̍ rārahā̱ṇa ā̍sā̱m pari̱ sūrya̍sya pari̱dhīm̐r a̍paśyat ||
5vi̱śvāva̍sur a̱bhi tan no̍ gṛṇātu di̱vyo ga̍ndha̱rvo raja̍so vi̱māna̍ḥ | yad vā̍ ghā sa̱tyam u̱ta yan na vi̱dma dhiyo̍ hinvā̱no dhiya̱ in no̍ avyāḥ ||
6sasni̍m avinda̱c cara̍ṇe na̱dīnā̱m apā̍vṛṇo̱d duro̱ aśma̍vrajānām | prāsā̍ṁ gandha̱rvo a̱mṛtā̍ni voca̱d indro̱ dakṣa̱m pari̍ jānād a̱hīnā̍m ||

            
          


          

            
English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
4Waters from sacrifice came to the Gandharva Viṣvâvasu, O Soma, when they saw him. Indra, approaching quickly, marked their going, and looked around upon the Sun's enclosures.
5This song Viṣvâvasu shall sing us, meter of air's mid-realm celestial Gandharva, That we may know aright both truth and falsehood: may he inspire our thoughts and help our praises.
6In the floods' track he found the bootyseeker: the rocky cow-pen's doors he threw wide open. These, the Gandharva told him, Rowed with Amrit. Indra knew well the puissance of the dragons.
