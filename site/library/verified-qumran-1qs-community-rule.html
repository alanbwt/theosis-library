Introduction

        
The Community Rule (Hebrew Serekh ha-Yahad, 1QS; first published as the "Manual of Discipline") is one of the seven scrolls found in Qumran Cave 1 in 1947. It sets out how a man enters the covenant of the Community (the yahad), the yearly ceremony of blessings and curses, a teaching on the two spirits of truth and perversion that God set in mankind until the time of visitation, the rules for the common life, the sessions of "the Many" and the admission of new members, a penal code, the founding of the council of the Community as a "house of holiness" that prepares "the way" in the wilderness (Isaiah 40:3), and the duties of the wise man, and it closes with a long hymn. Column IX looks forward to "the coming of a Prophet and the anointed ones of Aaron and Israel". The scroll is kept in the Shrine of the Book at the Israel Museum, Jerusalem; on the evidence of its handwriting it is usually dated to about 100-75 BCE.



Each section is one column of the scroll, shown on the Israel Museum's photograph. The Hebrew is the scroll's own text with its column and line numbers, from the transcription of Martin G. Abegg Jr. and colleagues in the ETCBC "dss" dataset. Square brackets mark letters lost from the leather and restored by the editors; a dot under a letter marks an uncertain reading; ⟨ ⟩ marks letters written above the line by the scribe; {{ }} marks letters the scribe cancelled; {x} <y> marks a slip of the scribe that the editors delete ({x}) and correct (<y>), and braces alone (VI 5-6) mark words written twice by mistake; an ellipsis marks lost letters that are not restored; four dots stand in the scroll in place of the divine name (VIII 14).



The English is the complete translation of the scroll by William H. Brownlee, published by the American Schools of Oriental Research in 1951. Brownlee followed the scroll line by line and marked each line division with a vertical stroke (|) and the end of a column with a double stroke (||); the superscript numbers placed at his strokes give the line numbers of the scroll, the same as in the Hebrew. In his notation square brackets enclose words he supplied or restored, parentheses enclose parenthetical words of the Hebrew, and words between asterisks belong in the Hebrew to the line before. Words he printed in italics are written twice in the scroll; words struck through are words the scribe cancelled and replaced.



Gaps: the scroll once began with at least one further column, now lost, and the top lines of column I are damaged; the lowest lines of most columns are broken along the torn lower edge. Two shorter works once sewn to the end of the same scroll, the Rule of the Congregation (1QSa) and the Rule of Blessings (1QSb), are separate manuscripts and are not included here.

English · W. H. Brownlee (1951)

            
1the straying of spirit understanding,
And to make murmurers wise through instruction;
And to respond humbly before the haughty of spirit,
And with broken spirit to men of | 2injustice*—
Who point with the finger and speak iniquity,
And are envious of wealth!
For as for me, my justification belongs to God;
And in His hand is the perfection of my way,
Together with the uprightness of my heart. |
3Through His righteousness my transgression shall be blotted out;
For from the fountain of His knowledge He has opened my light,
And mine eye has beheld the wonders He has done,
And my heart is illumined with the Mystery to come. |
4Eternal Being is the support of my right hand;
Upon the Rock of strength is the way of my treading;
It shall not tremble before aught,
For God’s faithfulness [or, truth] is itself | 5the rock I tread.
And His might is the support of my right hand.
And from the fountain of His righteousness [flows] my justification,
A light in my heart—. .
From His marvellous mysteries in Eternal Being. |
6My eye has beheld that wisdom
Which was hidden from men of knowledge,
And that prudent purpose [which was hidden] from the sons of men:
A fountain of righteousness and reservoir | 7of strength
As well as a spring of glory [which were concealed] from the assembly of flesh.
To those whom God chose He has given them as an eternal possession;
And He has given to them an inheritance in the lot of | 8the holy ones;
And with the sons of Heaven He has associated their assembly [or Conclave] for a community council;
Their assembly will be in the Holy Abode as an eternal planting,
During every | 9period that will be.
But I belong to wicked humanity
And to the assembly of perverse flesh.
My iniquities, my transgression, my sin
(Together with the perversities of my heart) |
10Belong to the assembly of worms and of things that move in darkness.
For a man’s way is [not] his own;
A man does not direct his steps;
For to God belongs the decision,
And His counsel | 11is perfection of way;
And by His knowledge everything has been brought into being.
And everything that is, He established by His purpose;
And apart from Him, nothing is done.
And I, if | 12I totter,
God’s dependable mercy is my salvation forever;
And if I stumble in the guilt of flesh,
My justification through God’s righteousness will stand everlastingly! |
13And if He begin my affliction,
Even from the pit He will draw out my soul,
And He will direct my steps in the way.
In His compassion He has brought me near,
And in His dependable mercy He will bring | 14my justification.
In His steadfast righteousness He has justified me
And in His great goodness He will pardon [or, atone for] all my iniquities;
And in His righteousness He will cleanse me from | 15man’s *impurity*,
And from the sin of the children of men.
TO PRAISE GOD FOR HIS RIGHTEOUSNESS AND THE MOST HIGH FOR HIS GLORY:
Blessed art Thou, O my God!
Who openest | 16the heart of Thy servant *unto knowledge*,
Directing in righteousness all his deeds,
And causing the son of Thy handmaid to stand,
Just as Thou wert pleased that the elect of mankind
Should stand | 17before Thee forever.
For without Thee a way can not be perfect,
And apart from Thy will naught can be accomplished!
Thou has taught | 18all knowledge;
And everything that has come to pass [has been] by Thy will.
For there is none other besides Thee
To reply to Thy counsel,
And to understand | 19all Thy holy purpose,
And to gaze into the depth of Thy mysteries,
And to comprehend all Thy marvels,
As well as the strength of | 20Thy might;
And who is able to bear Thy glory?
For what, indeed, is He,
The son of man among Thy miraculous works?
21And [what is] that born of woman, in Thy estimation?
For, as for him, from the dust is his kneading;
And the bread of worms is his final dwelling;
For he is something shaped, only | 22fashioned clay
Whose longing is for the dust.
What will clay reply, or that which is shaped by hand?
And what counsel does he understand?
