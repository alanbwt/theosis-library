Introduction

        
Rigveda 10.140 is a hymn of 6 verses addressed to Agni. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 10, the last and latest book. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 48 (University of Pennsylvania), a paper manuscript of the eighth aṣṭaka (RV 10.46-10.191) of the Ṛgveda Saṃhitā in Devanāgarī, 158 leaves, 6 lines to the page, with the accents marked in red; the scribe's colophon on fol. 158 gives Saṃvat 1768 (1711 CE); this hymn stands on fol. 127r-127v. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Like most Rigveda manuscripts, Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 48 writes a repeated refrain in short form after its first occurrence (for example kasmai° for kasmai devāya haviṣā vidhema); the Sanskrit shown is the full text of Aufrecht's edition.

Vedic Sanskrit · Aufrecht (accented)

                    
1agne̱ tava̱ śravo̱ vayo̱ mahi̍ bhrājante a̱rcayo̍ vibhāvaso | bṛha̍dbhāno̱ śava̍sā̱ vāja̍m u̱kthya1̱̍ṁ dadhā̍si dā̱śuṣe̍ kave ||

            
          


          

            
English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1AGNI, life-power and fame are thine: thy fires blaze mightily, thou rich in wealth of beams! Sage, passing bright, thou givest to the worshipper, with strength, the food that merits laud.

          

        

        
        


          

            §2-6 · RV 10.140.2-6 (aṣṭaka 8, adhyāya 7, varga 28); Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 48 fol. 127v

Vedic Sanskrit · Aufrecht (accented)

                    
2pā̱va̱kava̍rcāḥ śu̱krava̍rcā̱ anū̍navarcā̱ ud i̍yarṣi bhā̱nunā̍ | pu̱tro mā̱tarā̍ vi̱cara̱nn upā̍vasi pṛ̱ṇakṣi̱ roda̍sī u̱bhe ||
3ūrjo̍ napāj jātavedaḥ suśa̱stibhi̱r manda̍sva dhī̱tibhi̍r hi̱taḥ | tve iṣa̱ḥ saṁ da̍dhu̱r bhūri̍varpasaś ci̱trota̍yo vā̱majā̍tāḥ ||
4i̱ra̱jyann a̍gne prathayasva ja̱ntubhi̍r a̱sme rāyo̍ amartya | sa da̍rśa̱tasya̱ vapu̍ṣo̱ vi rā̍jasi pṛ̱ṇakṣi̍ sāna̱siṁ kratu̍m ||
5i̱ṣka̱rtāra̍m adhva̱rasya̱ prace̍tasa̱ṁ kṣaya̍nta̱ṁ rādha̍so ma̱haḥ | rā̱tiṁ vā̱masya̍ su̱bhagā̍m ma̱hīm iṣa̱ṁ dadhā̍si sāna̱siṁ ra̱yim ||
6ṛ̱tāvā̍nam mahi̱ṣaṁ vi̱śvada̍rśatam a̱gniṁ su̱mnāya̍ dadhire pu̱ro janā̍ḥ | śrutka̍rṇaṁ sa̱pratha̍stamaṁ tvā gi̱rā daivya̱m mānu̍ṣā yu̱gā ||

            
          


          

            
English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
2With brilliant, purifying sheen, with perfect sheen thou liftest up thyself in light. Thou, visiting both thy Mothers, aidest them as Son: thou joinest close the earth and heaven.
3O Jâtavedas, Son of Strength, rejoice thyself, gracious, in our fair hymns and songs. In thee are treasured various forms of strengthening food, born nobly and of wondrous help.
4Agni, spread forth, as Ruler, over living things: give wealth to us, Immortal God. Thou shinest out from beauty fair to look upon: thou leadest us to conquering power.
5To him, the wise, who orders sacrifice, who hath great riches un der his control, Thou givest blest award of good, and plenteous food, givest him wealth that conquers all.
6The men have set before them them for their welfare Agni, strong, visible to all, the Holy. Thee, Godlike One, with ears to hear, most famous, men's generations magnify with praise-songs.
