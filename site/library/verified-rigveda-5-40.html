Introduction

        
Rigveda 5.40 is a hymn of 9 verses addressed to Indra, Surya and Atri. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 5, the family book of the Atri clan. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 352v-353v. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1COME thou to what the stones have pressed, drink Soma, O thou Soma's Lord, Indra best Vr̥itra-slayer Strong One, with the Strong.
2Strong is the stone, the draught is strong, strong is this Soma that is pressed, Indra, best Vr̥itra-slayer, Strong One with the Strong.
3As strong I call on thee the Strong, O Thunder-armed, with various aids, Indra, best Vr̥itra-slayer, Strong One with the Strong.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
4Impetuous, Thunderer, Strong, quelling the mighty, King, potent, Vr̥itra-slayer, Soma-drinker, May he come hither with his yoked Bay Horses; may Indra gladden him at the noon libation.
5O Sûrya, when the Asura's descendant Svarbhânu, pierced thee through and through with darkness, All creatures looked like one who is bewildered, who knoweth not the place where he is standing.
6What time thou smotest down Svarbhânu's magic that spread itself beneath the sky, O Indra, By his fourth sacred prayer Atri discovered Sûrya concealed in gloom that stayed his function.
7Let not the oppressor with this dread, through anger swallow me up, for I am thine, O Atri. Mitra art thou, the sender of true blessings: thou and King Varuṇa be both my helpers.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
8The Brahman Atri, as he set the press-stones, serving the Gods with praise and adoration, Established in the heaven the eye of Sûrya, and caused Svarbhânu's magic arts to vanish.
9The Atris found the Sun again, him whom Svarbhânu of the brood Of Asuras had pierced with gloom. This none besides had power to do.
