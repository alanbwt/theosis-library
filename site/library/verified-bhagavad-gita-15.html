Introduction

        
The Bhagavad Gita ("Song of the Lord") is a dialogue of 700 verses between the prince Arjuna and Krishna, set on the battlefield of Kurukshetra in book 6 (Bhishmaparvan) of the Mahabharata. Its date of composition is debated; many scholars place it in the last centuries BCE. This is chapter 15, The Yoga of Attaining the Supreme Spirit (20 verses as numbered in the manuscript and in Besant's edition).



The pages shown are from a complete Sanskrit manuscript of the Gita at the University of Pennsylvania (Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 559), 90 paper leaves of 7 lines in Devanagari, with speaker headings, colophons and verse-end marks in red. Its final colophon (fol. 90r) dates the copy to Saṃvat 1851, the third day of the bright half of Caitra (1794 CE). Chapter 15 runs from fol. 71r to the chapter colophon on fol. 73v.



Each section below is one manuscript page: the verses whose text ends on that page, located by reading the scribe's verse numbers and the first and last words on the image. When a verse begins at the foot of one page and ends on the next, both pages are shown. The Sanskrit and English are from Annie Besant's edition and translation (4th edition, 1922), which prints each verse in Devanagari with its English; the manuscript's spelling differs from the printed text in small details.

Sanskrit · Devanagari (Besant 1922)

            
1श्रीभगवानुवाच ।
ऊर्ध्वमूलमधःशाखमश्वत्थं प्राहुरव्ययम् ।
छन्दांसि यस्य पर्णानि यस्तं वेद स वेदवित् ॥ १ ॥

            
          


          

            
English · Annie Besant (1922)

            
1The Blessed Lord said:
With roots above, branches below, the Asvattha is said to be indestructible; the leaves of it are hymns; he who knoweth it is a Veda-knower.

Sanskrit · Devanagari (Besant 1922)

            
2अधश्चोर्ध्वं प्रसृतास्तस्य शाखाः गुणप्रवृद्धा विषयप्रवालाः ।
अधश्च मूलान्यनुसन्ततानि कर्मानुबन्धीनि मनुष्यलोके ॥ २ ॥
3न रूपमस्येह तथोपलभ्यते नान्तो न चादिर्न च संप्रतिष्ठा ।
अश्वत्थमेनं सुविरूढमूलमसङ्गशस्त्रेण दृढेन छित्त्वा ॥ ३ ॥
4ततः पदं तत्परिमार्गितव्यम्
यस्मिन्गता न निवर्तन्ति भूयः ।
तमेव चाद्यं पुरुषं प्रपद्ये
यतः प्रवृत्तिः प्रसृता पुराणी ॥ ४ ॥
5निर्मानमोहा जितसङ्गदोषाः
अध्यात्मनित्या विनिवृत्तकामाः ।
द्वन्द्वैर्विमुक्ताः सुखदुःखसंज्ञै-
र्गच्छन्त्यमूढाः पदमव्ययं तत् ॥ ५ ॥

            
          


          

            
English · Annie Besant (1922)

            
2Downwards and upwards spread the branches of it, nourished by the qualities; the objects of the senses its buds; and its roots grow downwards, the bonds of action in the world of men.
3Nor here may be acquired knowledge of its form, nor its end, nor its origin, nor its rooting-place; this strongly rooted Asvattha having been cut down by the unswerving weapon of non-attachment,
4That path beyond may be sought, treading which there is no return. I go indeed to that Primal Man, whence the ancient energy forth-streamed.
5Without pride and delusion, victorious over the vice of attachment, dwelling constantly in the Self, desire pacified, liberated from the pairs of opposites known as pleasure and pain, they tread, undeluded, that indestructible path.

Sanskrit · Devanagari (Besant 1922)

            
6न तद्भासयते सूर्यो न शशाङ्को न पावकः ।
यद्गत्वा न निवर्तन्ते तद्धाम परमं मम ॥ ६ ॥
7ममैवांशो जीवलोके जीवभूतः सनातनः ।
मनःषष्ठानीन्द्रियाणि प्रकृतिस्थानि कर्षति ॥ ७ ॥
8शरीरं यदवाप्नोति यच्चाप्युत्क्रामतीश्वरः ।
गृहित्वैतानि संयाति वायुर्गन्धानिवाशयात् ॥ ८ ॥
9श्रोत्रं चक्षुः स्पर्शनं च रसनं घ्राणमेव च ।
अधिष्ठाय मनश्चायं विषयानुपसेवते ॥ ९ ॥

            
          


          

            
English · Annie Besant (1922)

            
6Nor doth the sun lighten there, nor moon, nor fire; having gone thither they return not; that is My supreme abode.
7A portion of Mine own Self, transformed in the world of life into an immortal Spirit, draweth round itself the senses of which the mind is the sixth, veiled in matter.
8When the Lord acquireth a body and when He abandoneth it, He seizeth these and goeth with them, as the wind takes fragrances from their retreats
9Enshrined in the ear, the eye, the touch, the taste and the smell, and in the mind also, He enjoyeth the objects of the senses

Sanskrit · Devanagari (Besant 1922)

            
10उत्क्रामन्तं स्थितं वापि भुञ्जानं वा गुणान्वितम् ।
विमूढा नानुपश्यन्ति पश्यन्ति ज्ञानचक्षुषः ॥ १० ॥
11यतन्तो योगिनश्चैनं पश्यन्त्यात्मन्यवस्थितम् ।
यतन्तोऽप्यकृतात्मानो नैनं पश्यन्त्यचेतसः ॥ ११ ॥
12यदादित्यगतं तेजो जगद्भासयतेऽखिलम् ।
यच्चन्द्रमसि यच्चाग्नौ तत्तेजो विद्धि मामकम् ॥ १२ ॥
13गामाविश्य च भूतानि धारयाम्यहमोजसा ।
पुष्णामि चौषधीः सर्वाः सोमो भूत्वा रसात्मकः ॥ १३ ॥

            
          


          

            
English · Annie Besant (1922)

            
10The deluded do not perceive (Him) when He departeth or stayeth or enjoyeth, swayed by the qualities; the wisdom-eyed perceive.
11Yogîs also, struggling, perceive Him, established in the Self; but though struggling, the unintelligent perceive Him not, their selves untrained.
12That splendour issuing from the sun that enlighteneth the whole world, that which is in the moon and in fire, that splendour know as from Me.
13Permeating the soil, I support beings by my vital energy, and having become the delicious Soma I nourish all plants.

          

        

        
        


          

            §14-17 · Bhagavad Gita 15.14-17; Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 559, fol. 72v and fol. 73r

Sanskrit · Devanagari (Besant 1922)

            
14अहं वैश्वानरो भूत्वा प्राणिनां देहमाश्रितः ।
प्राणापानसमायुक्तः पचाम्यन्नं चतुर्विधम् ॥ १४ ॥
15सर्वस्य चाहं हृदि सन्निविष्टो मत्तः स्मृतिर्ज्ञानमपोहनं च ।
वेदैश्च सर्वैरहमेव वेद्यो वेदान्तकृद्वेदविदेव चाहम् ॥ १५ ॥
16द्वाविमौ पुरुषौ लोके क्षरश्चाक्षर एव च ।
क्षरः सर्वाणि भूतानि कूटस्थोऽक्षर उच्यते ॥ १६ ॥
17उत्तमः पुरुषस्त्वन्यः परमात्मेत्युदाहृतः ।
यो लोकत्रयमाविश्य बिभर्त्यव्यय ईश्वरः ॥ १७ ॥

            
          


          

            
English · Annie Besant (1922)

            
14I, having become the Fire of Life, take possession of the bodies of breathing things, and united with the life-breaths, I digest the four kinds of food.
15And I am seated in the hearts of all, and from Me memory and wisdom and their absence. And that which is to be known in all the Vedas am I; and I indeed the Veda-knower and the author of the Vedanta.
16There are two Energies in this world, the destructible and the indestructible; the destructible is all beings, the unchanging is called the indestructible.
17The highest Energy is verily Another, declared as the Supreme Self, He who, pervading all, sustaineth the three worlds, the indestructible Lord.

Sanskrit · Devanagari (Besant 1922)

            
18यस्मात्क्षरमतीतोऽहमक्षरादपि चोत्तमः ।
अतोऽस्मि लोके वेदे च प्रथितः पुरुषोत्तमः ॥ १८ ॥
19यो मामेवमसंमूढो जानाति पुरुषोत्तमम् ।
स सर्वविद्भजति मां सर्वभावेन भारत ॥ १९ ॥
20इति गुह्यतमं शास्त्रमिदमुक्तं मयाऽनघ ।
एतद्‌बुध्वा बुद्धिमान् स्यात्कृतकृत्यश्च भारत ॥ २० ॥

            
          


          

            
English · Annie Besant (1922)

            
18Since I excel the destructible, and am more excellent also than the indestructible, in the world and in the Veda I am proclaimed the Supreme Spirit.
19He who undeluded knoweth Me thus as the Supreme Spirit, he, all-knowing, worshippeth Me with his whole being, O Bhârata.
20Thus by Me this most secret teaching hath been told O sinless one. This known, he hath become illuminated and hath finished his work, O Bhârata.

Notes

        
    
          
          
  1. 
            Sanskrit and English: Annie Besant, The Bhagavad-Gita, 4th edition (Madras: G. A. Natesan, 1922), transcription validated on English Wikisource. Besant's footnotes are not reproduced.
          
    2. 
          
          
  2. 
            Verse numbers follow Besant and the manuscript, which agree throughout: chapter 13 counts Arjuna's opening question ('prakṛtiṃ puruṣaṃ caiva...') as verse 1, so it has 35 verses and the whole text 701.
          
    3. 
          
          
  3. 
            K. T. Telang's translation (Sacred Books of the East 8, 1882) was the first choice for the English, but it is continuous prose without verse divisions, so it cannot be matched to manuscript pages verse by verse; Besant's verse-numbered translation is used instead.
          
    4. 
          
          
  4. 
            The last section of each chapter is the chapter colophon. Besant prints most Sanskrit colophons in shortened form (marked with ॰) while his English gives the full formula; the manuscript writes the colophon out in full, and its chapter names differ in places from Besant's (see the scan note).
