Introduction

        
The Nigali Sagar (Nigliva) pillar lies in the Nepal Terai about thirteen miles (21 km) north-west of Lumbini; it is not in its original place, and only two pieces of its shaft survive. Its inscription records that Ashoka enlarged to double its size the stupa of the Buddha Konakamana (Kanakamuni, one of the Buddhas held to have preceded Gautama) fourteen years after his consecration, and later came to worship there. It is the oldest datable witness to the belief in earlier Buddhas. The beginnings of lines 3 and 4 are broken away; Hultzsch's English supplies the lost words in square brackets from the Rummindei inscription.
Sources. Original text and English: E. Hultzsch, Inscriptions of Asoka. New Edition (Corpus Inscriptionum Indicarum, vol. I), Oxford: Clarendon Press for the Government of India, 1925, transcribed from the page images of the archive.org scan (public domain). Images: Hultzsch's collotype plates of inked impressions (estampages) of the Nigali Sagar (Nigliva) pillar, taken on the stone for the Archaeological Survey of India. An impression is a contact copy pressed on paper from the inscribed surface itself, not a drawing; each section's image is the part of the plate that carries that text, with the plate's own numerals and line numbers in the margins.
Reading the text. Line numbers are Hultzsch's lines on the stone. Letters in square brackets are damaged or uncertain on the stone; dots mark lost letters. The capital letters in brackets, (A), (B) ..., are Hultzsch's sentence divisions and are the same in the transliteration and the English. Hultzsch's footnotes, which record other scholars' readings and his corrections, are not reproduced.

Ashokan Prakrit · Hultzsch transliteration

                    
1(A) Devānaṁpiyena Piyadasina lājina chodasa-vasā[bh]i[si]t[e]n[a]
2Budhasa Konākamanasa thube dutiyaṁ vaḍhite
3(B) . . . . . . . . sābhisitena cha atana āgācha mahīyite
4. . . . . . . . . . pāpite

            
          


          

            
English · E. Hultzsch (1925)

            
(A) When king Dēvānāṁpriya Priyadarśin had been anointed fourteen years, he enlarged the Stūpa of the Buddha Kōnākamana to the double (of its original size).
(B) And when he had been anointed [twenty] years, he came himself and worshipped (this spot) [and] caused [a stone pillar to be set up].
