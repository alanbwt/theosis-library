Introduction

        
Rigveda 10.81 is a hymn of 7 verses addressed to Visvakarman. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 10, the last and latest book. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 48 (University of Pennsylvania), a paper manuscript of the eighth aṣṭaka (RV 10.46-10.191) of the Ṛgveda Saṃhitā in Devanāgarī, 158 leaves, 6 lines to the page, with the accents marked in red; the scribe's colophon on fol. 158 gives Saṃvat 1768 (1711 CE); this hymn stands on fol. 47r-48r. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Like most Rigveda manuscripts, Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 48 writes a repeated refrain in short form after its first occurrence (for example kasmai° for kasmai devāya haviṣā vidhema); the Sanskrit shown is the full text of Aufrecht's edition.

            §1-3 · RV 10.81.1-3 (aṣṭaka 8, adhyāya 3, varga 16); Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 48 fol. 47r
            

              
              
              
            

          


          
          

            
Manuscript

            
            
            
            
Where on the image
Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 48, fol. 47r (numbered 46r by the scribe) (OPenn image 46, lower leaf). Checked on the image: the Rigveda text on this page begins in RV 10.80.7 ‹-tatakṣur agnim mahām avocāmā›. The next page with Rigveda text, fol. 47v (numbered 46v by the scribe), begins in RV 10.81.4 ‹sa vṛkṣa āsa yato›. Verses 10.81.1-3 end on this page.

          

          

          

            
Vedic Sanskrit · Aufrecht (accented)

                    
1ya i̱mā viśvā̱ bhuva̍nāni̱ juhva̱d ṛṣi̱r hotā̱ ny asī̍dat pi̱tā na̍ḥ | sa ā̱śiṣā̱ dravi̍ṇam i̱cchamā̍naḥ prathama̱cchad ava̍rā̱m̐ ā vi̍veśa ||
2kiṁ svi̍d āsīd adhi̱ṣṭhāna̍m ā̱rambha̍ṇaṁ kata̱mat svi̍t ka̱thāsī̍t | yato̱ bhūmi̍ṁ ja̱naya̍n vi̱śvaka̍rmā̱ vi dyām aurṇo̍n mahi̱nā vi̱śvaca̍kṣāḥ ||
3vi̱śvata̍ścakṣur u̱ta vi̱śvato̍mukho vi̱śvato̍bāhur u̱ta vi̱śvata̍spāt | sam bā̱hubhyā̱ṁ dhama̍ti̱ sam pata̍trai̱r dyāvā̱bhūmī̍ ja̱naya̍n de̱va eka̍ḥ ||

            
          


          

            
English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1HE who sate down as Hotar-priest, the R̥ishi, our Father, offering up all things existing,– He, seeking through his wish a great possession, came among men on earth as archetypal.
2What was the place whereon he took his station? What was it that supported him? How was it? Whence Viṣvakarman, seeing all, producing the earth, with mighty power disclosed the heavens.
3He who hath eyes on all sides round about him, a mouth on all sides, arms and feet on all sides, He, the Sole God, producing earth and heaven, weldeth them, with his arms as wings, together.

Vedic Sanskrit · Aufrecht (accented)

                    
4kiṁ svi̱d vana̱ṁ ka u̱ sa vṛ̱kṣa ā̍sa̱ yato̱ dyāvā̍pṛthi̱vī ni̍ṣṭata̱kṣuḥ | manī̍ṣiṇo̱ mana̍sā pṛ̱cchated u̱ tad yad a̱dhyati̍ṣṭha̱d bhuva̍nāni dhā̱raya̍n ||
5yā te̱ dhāmā̍ni para̱māṇi̱ yāva̱mā yā ma̍dhya̱mā vi̍śvakarmann u̱temā | śikṣā̱ sakhi̍bhyo ha̱viṣi̍ svadhāvaḥ sva̱yaṁ ya̍jasva ta̱nva̍ṁ vṛdhā̱naḥ ||
6viśva̍karman ha̱viṣā̍ vāvṛdhā̱naḥ sva̱yaṁ ya̍jasva pṛthi̱vīm u̱ta dyām | muhya̍ntv a̱nye a̱bhito̱ janā̍sa i̱hāsmāka̍m ma̱ghavā̍ sū̱rir a̍stu ||

            
          


          

            
English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
4What was the tree, what wood in sooth produced it, from which they fashioned out the earth and heaven? Ye thoughtful men inquire within your spirit whereon he stood when he established all things.
5Nine highest, lowest, sacrificial natures, and these thy mid-most here, O Viṣvakarman, Teach thou thy friends at sacrifice, O Blessed, and come thyself, exalted, to our worship.
6Bring thou thyself, exalted with oblation, O Viṣvakarman, Earth and Heaven to worship. Let other men around us live in folly here let us have a rich and liberal patron.

Vedic Sanskrit · Aufrecht (accented)

                    
7vā̱cas pati̍ṁ vi̱śvaka̍rmāṇam ū̱taye̍ mano̱juva̱ṁ vāje̍ a̱dyā hu̍vema | sa no̱ viśvā̍ni̱ hava̍nāni joṣad vi̱śvaśa̍mbhū̱r ava̍se sā̱dhuka̍rmā ||

            
          


          

            
English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
7Let us invoke to-day, to aid our labour, the Lord of Speech, the thought-swift Viṣvakarman. May he hear kindly all our invocations who gives all bliss for aid, whose works are righteous.
