Introduction

        
The Book of the Dead, in Egyptian the spells for going forth by day, is a collection of funerary spells written on papyrus rolls and placed in burials from the New Kingdom to the Roman period. Each roll was made for one person, with spells taken from a large repertoire and illustrated with drawings (vignettes). This library gives one complete roll: the papyrus of Iuefankh in the Museo Egizio, Turin (Cat. 1791), written in the Ptolemaic period (332-30 BCE) and coming from Thebes. Richard Lepsius published it in 1842 and divided it into 165 chapters; his numbering is still the standard numbering of the spells, and it is used here.



Spell 79 (Birch: 'The chapter of Making the Change into the oldest of the') is on Lepsius's plate XXX and on photograph 12 of the 31 Museo Egizio photographs of the roll.



The Egyptian is the transliteration of the Thesaurus Linguae Aegyptiae (edition by Burkhard Backes), with the column number of the spell before each column; the numbers are those of Lepsius's plates. On the papyrus the text runs in vertical columns of cursive hieroglyphs from right to left along the roll; titles and some words are written in red. Signs restored by the editor are in square brackets, signs the scribe left out in angle brackets, superfluous signs in braces and damaged signs in half brackets. The English is the translation by Samuel Birch, published in 1867 and made from Lepsius's copy of this papyrus, so the English and the Egyptian come from the same manuscript. It was the first complete English translation of the Book of the Dead; many passages are now understood differently. Birch's own square brackets give his alternatives and explanations. The photographs are by the Museo Egizio; each shows a stretch of the roll, and the note of each section says where the spell stands on it.

English · Samuel Birch (1867)

            
LXXIX. The chapter of Making the Change into the oldest of the
Chiefs.
[Vignette. — Deceased adoring three Gods.]
I am Tum, maker of the Heaven, creator of beings, coming forth from the world, making all the generations of existences, giving birth to the Gods, creating himself, Lord of Life supplying the Gods. Oh ye supporters, Lords of Things, pure, hiding your places! Oh ye avengers, Lords of Eternity, hiding the secret shapes of the shrine, not knowing wherefore they are! Oh ye avengers, Lords of the place of the Gods, turning the libations of the inundations in the West in the midst of the Firmament! Let me come to ye. Jam pure, I am divine, am spiritualised, I am strong, I am become a soul, I prevail. I bring to you frankincense, balls of natron. I have rubbed the venom from your mouth. I have come, I have rubbed all the evil out of your heart. I have caught the sins which ye keep. I have brought good things to ye, I have touched truth. I know ye, I know your name, I know your shapes. Those who wish me no good, he knows them. I am created as ye [have been]. I rise God from men. The Gods live, they see it. I prevail as ye do with that God taller than his box [place]. He has come; the Gods rejoice; the Goddess-wives rejoice at him [when] they see him. I have come to ye. I have risen from your place, I have sat in my place in the horizon. I have received food off the table, and drunk libations at the eventide. I have come to those who are in the horizon with joy; glory has been given to me by those who are in the Gate in this mortal body. I rejoice at that Great God, Lord of the Palace; the Gods rejoice when they see him at his good coming forth from the belly, born of his mother the Firmament.
