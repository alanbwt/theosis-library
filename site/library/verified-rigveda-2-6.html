Introduction

        
Rigveda 2.6 is a hymn of 8 verses addressed to Agni. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 2, the family book of Gṛtsamada. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 154r-154v. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41, fol. 154r (numbered 12r by the scribe) (OPenn image 155, lower leaf). Checked on the image: the Rigveda text on this page begins in RV 2.5.4 ‹vayāḥ-iva anu rohate›. English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1AGNI, accept this flaming brand, this waiting with my prayer on thee: Hear graciously these songs of praise.
2With this hymn let us honour thee, seeker of horses, Son of Strength, With this fair hymn, thou nobly born.
3As such, lover of song, with songs, wealth-lover, giver of our wealth! With reverence let us worship thee.
4Be thou for us a liberal Prince, giver and Lord of precious things. Drive those who hate us far away.
5Such as thou art, give rain from heaven, give strength which no man may resist: Give food exceeding plentiful.

Manuscript

            
            
            
            
            
            
Where on the image
Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41, fol. 154v (numbered 12v by the scribe) (OPenn image 156, upper leaf). Checked on the image: the Rigveda text on this page begins in RV 2.6.6 ‹yaviṣṭha dūta naḥ girā›. The next page with Rigveda text, fol. 155r (numbered 1r by the scribe), begins in RV 2.8.2 ‹cāru-pratīkaḥ āhutaḥ›. Verses 2.6.6-8 end on this page; 2.6.6 begins on fol. 154r (numbered 12r by the scribe).

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
6To him who lauds thee, craving help, most youthful envoy! through our song, Most holy Herald! come thou nigh.
7Between both races, Agni, Sage, well skilled thou passest to and fro, As envoy friendly to mankind.
8Befriend us thou as knowing all. Sage, duly worship thou the Gods, And seat thee on this sacred grass.
