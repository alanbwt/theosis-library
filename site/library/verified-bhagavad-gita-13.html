Introduction

        
The Bhagavad Gita ("Song of the Lord") is a dialogue of 700 verses between the prince Arjuna and Krishna, set on the battlefield of Kurukshetra in book 6 (Bhishmaparvan) of the Mahabharata. Its date of composition is debated; many scholars place it in the last centuries BCE. This is chapter 13, The Yoga of the Distinction between the Field and the Knower of the Field (35 verses as numbered in the manuscript and in Besant's edition).



The pages shown are from a complete Sanskrit manuscript of the Gita at the University of Pennsylvania (Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 559), 90 paper leaves of 7 lines in Devanagari, with speaker headings, colophons and verse-end marks in red. Its final colophon (fol. 90r) dates the copy to Saṃvat 1851, the third day of the bright half of Caitra (1794 CE). Chapter 13 runs from fol. 63v to the chapter colophon on fol. 67v.



Each section below is one manuscript page: the verses whose text ends on that page, located by reading the scribe's verse numbers and the first and last words on the image. When a verse begins at the foot of one page and ends on the next, both pages are shown. The Sanskrit and English are from Annie Besant's edition and translation (4th edition, 1922), which prints each verse in Devanagari with its English; the manuscript's spelling differs from the printed text in small details.

Sanskrit · Devanagari (Besant 1922)

            
1अर्जुन उवाच ।
प्रकृतिं पुरुषं चैव क्षेत्रं क्षेत्रज्ञमेव च ।
एतद्वेदितुमिच्छामि ज्ञानं ज्ञेयं च केशव ॥ १ ॥
2श्रीभगवानुवाच ।
इदं शरीरं कौन्तेय क्षेत्रमित्यभिधीयते ।
एतद्यो वेत्ति तं प्राहुः क्षेत्रज्ञ इति तद्विदः ॥ २ ॥

            
          


          

            
English · Annie Besant (1922)

            
1Arjuna said:
Matter and Spirit, even the Field and the Knower of the Field, wisdom and that which ought to be known, these I fain would learn, O Keshava.
2The Blessed Lord said:
This body, son of Kuntî, is called the Field; that which knoweth it is called the Knower of the Field by the Sages.

12Constancy in the wisdom of the Self, understanding of the object of essential wisdom; that is declared to be the Wisdom; all against it is ignorance.

15Shining with all sense-faculties without any senses; unattached supporting everything and free from qualities enjoying qualities;

19Thus the Field, Wisdom and the Object of Wisdom, have been briefly told. My devotee, thus knowing, enters into My Being.
20Know thou that Matter and Spirit are both without beginning; and know thou also that modifications and qualities are all Matter-born.

English · Annie Besant (1922)

            
33As the omnipresent ether is not affected, by reason of its subtlety, so seated everywhere in the body the Self is not affected.
34As the one sun illumineth the whole earth, so the Lord of the Field illumineth the whole Field, O Bhârata.
35They who by the eyes of Wisdom perceive this difference between the Field and the Knower of the Field, and the liberation of beings from Matter they go to the Supreme.
