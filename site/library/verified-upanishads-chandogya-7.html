Introduction

        
The Chandogya Upanishad belongs to the Samaveda and is one of the two longest and oldest Upanishads. It has eight chapters (prapathakas). This entry gives chapter 7. Narada, learned in all the Vedas and sciences, comes to Sanatkumara for help against sorrow. Sanatkumara leads him step by step from name, speech and mind up to breath and to the infinite (bhuman), which is happiness: 'where one sees nothing else, hears nothing else, knows nothing else, that is the infinite'.
The page images come from University of Pennsylvania Libraries, Ms. Coll. 390 Item 945, a paper manuscript of 74 leaves (11 x 17 cm) holding the whole Chandogya Upanishad, written in Devanagari, 10 to 11 lines to a side, on blue machine-made paper with watermarks; it is undated (catalogued 18th or 19th century). The scribe numbers the khandas and closes each chapter with a colophon, but does not number paragraphs. Chapter 7 runs from fol. 56r to fol. 65r.
For every page, the first and last words were read on the image and found in the edited text; the page boundaries follow from them. Each section below holds the paragraphs that end on one page, with every page they occupy.

English · F. Max Müller (1879)

                    
1.1Nârada approached Sanatkumâra and said, 'Teach me, Sir!' Sanatkumâra said to him: 'Please to tell me what you know; afterward I shall tell you what is beyond.'
1.2Nârada said: 'I know the Rig-veda, Sir, the Yagur-veda, the Sâma-veda, as the fourth the Âtharvana, as the fifth the Itihâsa-purâna (the Bhârata); the Veda of the Vedas (grammar); the Pitrya (the rules for the sacrifices for the ancestors); the Râsi (the science of numbers); the Daiva (the science of portents); the Nidhi (the science of time); the Vâkovâkya (logic); the Ekâyana (ethics); the Devavidyâ (etymology); the Brahma-vidyâ (pronunciation, sikshâ, ceremonial, kalpa, prosody, khandas); the Bhûta-vidyâ (the science of demons); the Kshatravidyâ (the science of weapons); the Nakshatra-vidyâ (astronomy); the Sarpa and Devagana-vidyâ (the science of serpents or poisons, and the sciences of the genii, such as the making of perfumes, dancing, singing, playing, and other fine arts). All this I know, Sir.

3.1'Mind (manas) is better than speech. For as the closed fist holds two amalaka or two kola or two aksha fruits, thus does mind hold speech and name. 4.1'Will (saṅkalpa) is better than mind. For when a man wills, then he thinks in his mind, then he sends forth speech, and he sends it forth in a name. In a name the sacred hymns are contained, in the sacred hymns all sacrifices.

This is will. Meditate on will.

Meditate on consideration.

15.2'For if one says anything unbecoming to a father, mother, brother, sister, tutor or Brâhmana, then people say, Shame on thee! thou hast offended thy father, mother, brother, sister, tutor, or a Brâhmana.
