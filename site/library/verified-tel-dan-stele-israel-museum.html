Tel Dan stele (House of David inscription), Israel Museum

        
        
A king of Aram-Damascus, probably Hazael (9th century BCE)

        
Israel Museum, Jerusalem: photograph by Gary Todd (CC0) + Biran and Naveh's text and Biran's 1995 translation as given in English Wikipedia (CC BY-SA 4.0) · A. Biran and J. Naveh, IEJ 43 (1993) and 45 (1995), via Wikipedia

        
      


      

      

        
Introduction

        
The Tel Dan stele is a broken basalt monument in Old Aramaic, set up at Dan in northern Israel in the second half of the 9th century BCE by a king of Aram-Damascus, usually identified as Hazael. Fragment A was found in 1993 and fragments B1 and B2 in 1994 in the excavations of Avraham Biran. The speaker says that the king of Israel had entered his father's land, that the god Hadad made him king, and that he killed the king of Israel and the king of the 'House of David'. The phrase bytdwd in line 9 is the oldest mention of the dynasty of David outside the Old Testament. Biran and Naveh restore the names as Joram son of Ahab, king of Israel, and Ahaziah son of Joram, of the House of David, the two kings killed by Jehu in 2 Kings 9; the restorations and the join of the fragments are debated. The stele is in the Israel Museum, Jerusalem.
Each line is given in three forms: the Phoenician (Old Aramaic) letters as transcribed by Biran and Naveh, the same letters in Hebrew square script, and a romanization. Dots divide words as on the stone; square brackets are restorations. The English is Biran's translation of 1995. Lines 10-13 survive only in their first few words.

            §1-7 · Tel Dan stele (KAI 310) lines 1-7: the death of the king's father; Hadad makes him king; seventy kings slain
            

              
              
              
            

          


          
          

            
Manuscript

            
            
            
            
Where on the image
Lines 1-7 of the thirteen lines on the inscribed face, which the photograph shows whole with all three fragments: fragment A on the right carries the right-hand part of every line (the text runs right to left), fragments B1 and B2 on the left carry the left-hand ends of lines 1-8 in Biran and Naveh's join. Checked on the image: thirteen lines on fragment A, line 9 beginning k.bytdwd with the letters of bytdwd picked out in white by the museum. Square brackets mark Biran and Naveh's restorations, which are not on the stone; their join of fragment B to fragment A, followed in the museum display, is not accepted by every scholar.
