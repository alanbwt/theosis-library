Introduction

        
Rigveda 9.114 is a hymn of 4 verses addressed to Soma. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 9, the hymns to Soma Pavamāna. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 742r-742v. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

Manuscript

            
            
            
            
Where on the image
Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41, fol. 742r (numbered 59r by the scribe) (OPenn image 742, lower leaf). Checked on the image: the Rigveda text on this page begins in RV 9.113.6 ‹-some mahīyate somena ānandam›. The next page with Rigveda text, fol. 742v (numbered 59v by the scribe), begins in RV 9.114.3 ‹devāḥ ādityāḥ ye sapta›. Verses 9.114.1-2 end on this page.

          

          

          

            
Vedic Sanskrit · Aufrecht (accented)

                    
1ya indo̱ḥ pava̍māna̱syānu̱ dhāmā̱ny akra̍mīt | tam ā̍huḥ supra̱jā iti̱ yas te̍ so̱māvi̍dha̱n mana̱ indrā̍yendo̱ pari̍ srava ||
2ṛṣe̍ mantra̱kṛtā̱ṁ stomai̱ḥ kaśya̍podva̱rdhaya̱n gira̍ḥ | soma̍ṁ namasya̱ rājā̍na̱ṁ yo ja̱jñe vī̱rudhā̱m pati̱r indrā̍yendo̱ pari̍ srava ||

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1THE man who waIketh as the Laws of Indu Pavamâna bid,– Men call him rich in children, him, O Soma, who hath met thy thought. Flow, Indu, flow for Indra's sake.
2Kaṣyapa, R̥ishi, lifting up thy voice with hymn-composers' lauds, Pay reverence to King Soma born the Sovran Ruler of the plants. Flow, Indu, flow for Indra's sake.

          

        

        
        


          

            §3-4 · RV 9.114.3-4 (aṣṭaka 7, adhyāya 5, varga 28); Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 fol. 742v
            

              
              
              
            

          


          
          

            
Manuscript

            
            
            
            
            
            
Where on the image
Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41, fol. 742v (numbered 59v by the scribe) (OPenn image 743, upper leaf). Checked on the image: the Rigveda text on this page begins in RV 9.114.3 ‹devāḥ ādityāḥ ye sapta›. The next page with Rigveda text, fol. 743r (numbered 60r by the scribe), begins in RV 10.1.4 ‹anya-rūpāḥ asi tvam vikṣu›. 4yat | te | rājan | śṛtam | haviḥ | tena | soma | abhi | rakṣa | naḥ | arāti-vā | mā | naḥ | tārīt | mo iti | ca | naḥ | kim | cana | āmamat | indrāya | indo iti | pari | srava

            

            
          


          

            
English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
3Seven regions have their several Suns; the ministering priests are seven; Seven are the Âditya Deities,– with these, O Soma, guard thou us. Flow, Indu, flow for Indra's sake.
4Guard us with this oblation which, King Soma, hath been dressed for thee. Let not malignity conquer us, let nothing evil do us harm. Flow, Indu, flow for Indra's sake.
