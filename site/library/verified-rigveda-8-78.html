Introduction

        
Rigveda 8.78 is a hymn of 10 verses addressed to Indra. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 8, the book of the Kaṇva and Aṅgiras poets. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 646v-647v. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1BRING us a thousand, Indra, as our guerdon for the Soma juice: Hundreds of kine, O Hero, bring.
2Bring cattle, bring us ornament, bring us embellishment and steeds, Give us, besides, two rings of gold.
3And, Bold One, bring in ample store rich jewels to adorn the ear, For thou, Good Lord, art far renowned.
4None other is there for the priest, Hero! but thou, to give him gifts, To win much spoil and prosper him.
5Indra can never be brought low, Ṣakra can never be subdued: He heareth and beholdeth all.
6He spieth out the wrath of man, he who can never be deceived: Ere blame can come he marketh it.
7He hath his stomach full of might, the Vritra-slayer, Conqueror, The Soma-drinker, ordering all.
8In thee all treasures are combined, Soma all blessed things in thee, Uninjured, easy to bestow.
9To thee speeds forth my hope that craves the gift of corn, and kine and gold, Yea, craving horses, speeds to thee.
