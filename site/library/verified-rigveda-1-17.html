Introduction

        
Rigveda 1.17 is a hymn of 9 verses addressed to Indra and Varuna. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 1, a collection of hymns by many families of poets. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 9r-9v. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1I CRAVE help from the Imperial Lords, from Indra-Varuṇa; may they Both favour one of us like me.
2Guardians of men, ye ever come with ready succour at the call Of every singer such as I.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
3Sate you, according to your wish, O Indra-Varuṇa, with wealth: Fain would we have you nearest us.
4May we be sharers of the powers, sharers of the benevolence Of you who give strength bounteously.
5Indra and Varuṇa, among givers of thousands, meet for praise, Are Powers who merit highest laud.
6Through their protection may we gain great store of wealth, and heap it up Enough and still to spare, be ours.
7O Indra-Varuṇa, on you for wealth in many a form I call: Still keep ye us victorious.
8O Indra-Varuṇa, – through our songs that seek to win you to ourselves, Give us at once your sheltering help.
9O Indra-Varuṇa, to you may fair praise which I offer come, joint eulogy which ye dignify.
