Introduction

        
Ninurta defeats and kills Anzu, recovers the Tablet of Destinies, and is praised by the gods with many names.
The Epic of Anzu tells how the lion-headed bird Anzu stole the Tablet of Destinies from Enlil, and how the god Ninurta, after other gods refused, defeated Anzu and recovered it. The Standard Babylonian version has three tablets. This page follows the line numbering of the electronic Babylonian Library edition.
How this page is built. Each section is a run of consecutive lines as written on one photographed clay tablet. For every line the eBL edition (https://www.ebl.lmu.de/corpus/L/1/10/SB/III) lists the manuscripts that carry it, with each tablet's own column and line numbers; the tablet chosen for each run is the photographed manuscript with the most signs preserved there. The original text is eBL's transliteration of that tablet's signs (not a modern composite text): square brackets mark broken signs, x an illegible sign, # a damaged sign, ? an uncertain reading, and words in {braces} are determinatives. Superscript numbers are the line numbers of the eBL edition, used in both the Akkadian and the English. The English is B. R. Foster's translation in the eBL edition (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), one line of English per line of the text; italics mark uncertain renderings, as in the source.
Finding a line on the photograph. Each section names the tablet, the face and column, and the tablet's own line numbers (a prime, as in 5', counts from the first preserved line of a broken column). On a tablet with six columns the obverse carries columns i-iii from left to right; the reverse, turned over from bottom to top, carries columns iv-vi from right to left. The photographs often show the obverse above the reverse with the edges around them; a fragment preserves only part of this layout.
This page shows 83 of the 155 lines of the eBL edition (54%), from 3 tablets in the British Museum: K.7923 (Nineveh), BM.38886, BM.36072.
Lines not shown. 72 of the 155 lines of the eBL edition have no witness here with an open photograph: 1-5 (only on IM.95180); 22-40 (only on IM.95180); b+103 (only on IM.95180); b+104-b+117 (only on IM.95180, Ant.11000); b+118-b+131 (only on IM.95180); b+150-b+167 (only on IM.95180); b+168 (no manuscript preserves it). Lines that survive only on tablets whose photographs are not openly licensed (for example in the Iraq Museum, Berlin, Istanbul, Ankara, Philadelphia or private collections), or only on tablets whose eBL photograph does not show the lines, are omitted.

English · B. R. Foster (eBL edition)

                    
6A storm was [spreading out] to all four directions,

7Weapons were struck and struck in deathblows, a frightful combat,

8Both were bathed in the sweat of battle.

9Then Anzu grew tired, in the brunt of the storm he dropped his wings,

10The lord took his darts toward the back,

11He cut off his pinions, scattered them right and left.

12Once Anzu saw his feathers, that took away his (magic) words.

13When he cried “Feather to wing!” the shaft whistled toward him,

14The arrow passed through his bosom,

15He made his dart pass through pinion and wing,

16The arrow passed through his heart and lungs.

17He slew the mountains, brought mayhem upon and overran their inmost reaches,

18Ninurta slew the mountains, brought mayhem upon and overran their inmost reaches,

19His fury overran the wide earth,

20He overran the inmost reaches of the mountains, he killed wicked Anzu!

21The warrior Ninurta took back the tablet of the gods’ destinies in his hand,
