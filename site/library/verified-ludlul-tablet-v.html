Introduction

        
The restored man passes through the gates of Esagil in Babylon, gives thanks and offerings, and the people of Babylon praise Marduk.
Ludlul bel nemeqi ('I will praise the lord of wisdom'), the Poem of the Righteous Sufferer, is a monologue in five tablets by Shubshi-meshre-Shakkan, a nobleman who lost the favour of his king and his gods, was struck by illness, and was restored by Marduk. It was composed in the late second millennium BCE and copied widely in the first millennium. This page follows the line numbering of the electronic Babylonian Library edition.
How this page is built. Each section is a run of consecutive lines as written on one photographed clay tablet. For every line the eBL edition (https://www.ebl.lmu.de/corpus/L/2/2/SB/V) lists the manuscripts that carry it, with each tablet's own column and line numbers; the tablet chosen for each run is the photographed copy of the work with the most signs preserved there. Commentaries and texts that only quote a line are never chosen. School and excerpt tablets are used only for lines that no photographed library copy preserves as well, and only where they carry the line as text; their sections say so. The original text is eBL's transliteration of that tablet's signs (not a modern composite text): square brackets mark broken signs, x an illegible sign, # a damaged sign, ? an uncertain reading, and words in {braces} are determinatives; the notes give a key to every sign used on this page, and eBL's layout codes are written out in words (for example '(line break)' or '(blank space)'). Superscript numbers are the line numbers of the eBL edition, used in both the Akkadian and the English. The English is B. R. Foster's translation in the eBL edition (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), one line of English per line of the text; italics mark uncertain renderings, as in the source. Where the tablet is broken, the English still translates the whole line of eBL's composite text; each section's note names the lines that are broken on its tablet.
Finding a line on the photograph. Each section names the tablet, the face and column, and the tablet's own line numbers (a prime, as in 5', counts from the first preserved line of a broken column). On a tablet with six columns the obverse carries columns i-iii from left to right; the reverse, turned over from bottom to top, carries columns iv-vi from right to left. The photographs often show the obverse above the reverse with the edges around them; a fragment preserves only part of this layout.
This page shows 71 of the 120 lines of the eBL edition (59%), from 2 tablets in the British Museum: BM.34650, BM.32208.
Lines not shown. 49 of the 120 lines of the eBL edition are not shown, because no photographed copy of the work used here carries them: 23 (no photograph in eBL: BM.77253; commentary: K.3291); 24 (no photograph in eBL: BM.77253); 54 (no photograph in eBL: VAT.9303 (Vorderasiatisches Museum, Berlin), VAT.9442 (Vorderasiatisches Museum, Berlin), BM.74201, Ashm-1924.1795 (Ashmolean Museum)); 55 (no photograph in eBL: VAT.9303 (Vorderasiatisches Museum, Berlin), VAT.9442 (Vorderasiatisches Museum, Berlin), BM.74201); 56 (no photograph in eBL: VAT.9303 (Vorderasiatisches Museum, Berlin), VAT.9442 (Vorderasiatisches Museum, Berlin)); 57-60 (no photograph in eBL: VAT.9303 (Vorderasiatisches Museum, Berlin), VAT.9442 (Vorderasiatisches Museum, Berlin), BM.74201); 61-63 (no photograph in eBL: VAT.9442 (Vorderasiatisches Museum, Berlin)); 64 (no photograph in eBL: VAT.9303 (Vorderasiatisches Museum, Berlin), VAT.9442 (Vorderasiatisches Museum, Berlin)); 65-67 (no photograph in eBL: VAT.9303 (Vorderasiatisches Museum, Berlin)); 68-69 (no photograph in eBL: VAT.9303 (Vorderasiatisches Museum, Berlin), VAT.10538 (Vorderasiatisches Museum, Berlin)); 70 (no photograph in eBL: VAT.9303 (Vorderasiatisches Museum, Berlin)); 71-80 (no photograph in eBL: VAT.9303 (Vorderasiatisches Museum, Berlin), VAT.10538 (Vorderasiatisches Museum, Berlin), SU-1952.212 (Museum of Anatolian Civilizations, Ankara)); 81-82 (no photograph in eBL: VAT.9303 (Vorderasiatisches Museum, Berlin), VAT.10538 (Vorderasiatisches Museum, Berlin), BM.77253, SU-1952.212 (Museum of Anatolian Civilizations, Ankara)); 83-86 (no photograph in eBL: VAT.9303 (Vorderasiatisches Museum, Berlin), VAT.10538 (Vorderasiatisches Museum, Berlin), BM.77253); 87-90 (no photograph in eBL: VAT.10538 (Vorderasiatisches Museum, Berlin), BM.77253); 91-100 (no photograph in eBL: VAT.unn.1 (Vorderasiatisches Museum, Berlin), BM.77253). A line is shown only on a photographed tablet that carries it as text. Commentaries and other texts that quote a line (letters, rituals, god lists) are not used as witnesses, because they carry it with explanations or inside another text; tablets without a photograph in eBL are not used, because the line could not be shown on its tablet.

English · B. R. Foster (eBL edition)

                    
25The street […]

26He had me […] pass through the main square of the city,

27He had me […] come in through the main gate (of the temple),

28[…] Marduk increased,

29[…] Zarpanitu increased.

30Had it not been that the lord released me,

31My life would soon have been snuffed out, Oh yes!

32I would have been sent down to the netherworld, Oh yes!

33I would have departed as a ghost, Oh yes!

34Had it not been that the lord let me abide,

40[With] prostration and supplication […] to Esagila.

41I who came back here from the grave came in through the “Gate of [Sunrise],”

42[In the] “Gate of Prosperity” prosperity came my way,

44[In the] “Gate of Well-being” I was vouchsafed well-being,

45In the “Gate of Life” I was granted life,

46In the “Gate of Sunrise” I was reckoned among the living,

47In the “Gate of Splendid Wonderment” my signs were plain to see.

48In the “Gate of Release from Guilt” I was released from my bond,

49In the “Gate of Pure Water” I was sprinkled with purifying water,

50In the “Gate of Deliberation” my mouth made inquiry,

51In the “Gate of Relief from Sighing” my sighing was relieved.

52In the “Gate of Conciliation” I appeared with Marduk,

53In the “Gate of Joy” I kissed the foot of Zarpanitu.
