Introduction

        
P72 is a papyrus codex copied in Egypt in the 3rd or early 4th century. It holds the oldest known copies of 1 Peter, 2 Peter and Jude. The pages are numbered by the scribe. 1-2 Peter (papyrus pages 1-36) are Papyrus Bodmer VIII, given by Martin Bodmer to Pope Paul VI in 1969 and now in the Vatican Library; Jude (pages 62-68) is Papyrus Bodmer VII, still at the Fondation Martin Bodmer, Cologny (Geneva). Both belong to the Bodmer "Miscellaneous" or "Composite" codex, which also contains the Nativity of Mary, the apocryphal correspondence of Paul with the Corinthians, the 11th Ode of Solomon, Melito's sermon On the Pascha, a hymn fragment and other texts. 1-2 Peter seem to have formed a separate booklet before being bound with the rest.



Each section below is one papyrus page: the photograph, the NTVMR diplomatic transcription of that page line by line, and the King James Version of every verse on it. The scribe's spelling is irregular (ει for ι, ε for αι, and so on) and is kept as written.



This entry covers papyrus pages 28-32 (2 Peter 2:2-3:3). The photographs are from the Vatican Library (Pap. Bodmer VIII).

Notes

        
    
          
          
  1. 
            Greek: the NTVMR diplomatic transcription, line by line as on the papyrus. Square brackets enclose letters that are lost or illegible and were supplied by the transcribers; a dot under a letter marks it as uncertain; [...] marks a gap. Nomina sacra and numerals keep the scribe's overline. Spelling is the scribe's (no accents or breathings). Words broken at a line end stay broken as on the papyrus. Corrections are not shown: the text is the first hand.
          
    2. 
          
          
  2. 
            English: the King James Version (1611), whole verses, for every verse that has text on the page. A verse that begins on one page and ends on the next is given in full on both. The KJV translates a later form of the Greek text, so its wording does not always match the papyrus; material differences (such as verses the papyrus does not have) are noted in the section.
