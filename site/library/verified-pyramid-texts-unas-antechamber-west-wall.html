Introduction

        
The Pyramid Texts are the oldest large body of Egyptian religious writing: spells, called utterances, for the resurrection of the dead king, his protection, his meals and his ascent to the sky, cut in vertical columns of hieroglyphs on the walls of the burial apartments of late Old Kingdom pyramids. The pyramid of Unas at Saqqara, the last king of the 5th Dynasty (about 2375-2345 BCE), is the first to carry them. Its sarcophagus chamber, the passage to the antechamber, the antechamber and the corridor hold 649 numbered columns of text; this library gives every column, wall by wall, in seventeen entries (227 different utterances, some of them inscribed more than once).



This entry is the west wall of the antechamber: 37 columns of hieroglyphs, Sethe's W 416-452, which follow each other from the right-hand (north) edge of the photograph. It holds the utterances 254, 255, 256, 257, 258. The leftmost column (W 452) begins PT 260, which is given in full in the entry for the south wall.



Each section is one utterance. The Egyptian is the transliteration of the Thesaurus Linguae Aegyptiae (edition by Doris Topmann), with Sethe's Unas column number (W) before each column; a number ending in 'a' marks the offering label at the foot of a column. Signs restored by the editor are in square brackets, signs the scribe left out in angle brackets, damaged signs in half brackets. The English is Samuel A. B. Mercer's translation of 1952. Mercer translates Sethe's composite text made from all the pyramids that have an utterance, so his wording can include clauses that are not in the Unas copy, and his section numbers (134a, 134b ...) are Sethe's paragraph numbers, not column numbers; he writes 'N.' where the wall has the king's name. The photograph shows the whole wall; each section's note gives its columns counted from the edge where the text on that wall begins.
