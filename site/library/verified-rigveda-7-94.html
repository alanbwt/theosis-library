Introduction

        
Rigveda 7.94 is a hymn of 12 verses addressed to Indra and Agni. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 7, the family book of Vasiṣṭha. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 550v-551r. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

Manuscript

            
            
            
            
Where on the image
Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41, fol. 550v (numbered 71v by the scribe) (OPenn image 551, upper leaf). Checked on the image: the Rigveda text on this page begins in RV 7.93.6 ‹imām oṃ iti su›. English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1As rain from out the cloud, for you, Indra and Agni, from my soul This noblest praise hath been produced.
2Do ye, O Indra-Agni, hear the singer's call: accept his songs. Ye Rulers, grant his heart's desire.
3Give us not up to poverty, ye Heroes, Indra-Agni, nor To slander and reproach of men.
4To Indra and to Agni we bring reverence, high and holy hymn, And, craving help, softwords with prayer.
5For all these holy singers here implore these Twain to succour them, And priests that they may win them strength.

          

        

        
        


          

            §6-12 · RV 7.94.6-12 (aṣṭaka 5, adhyāya 6, varga 17); Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 fol. 551r

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
6Eager to laudyou, we with songs invoke you, bearing sacred food, Fain for success in sacrifice.
7Indra and Agni, come to us with favour, ye who conquer men: Let not the wicked master us.
8At no time let the injurious blow of hostile mortal fall on us: O Indra-Agni, shelter us.
9Whatever wealth we crave of you, in gold, in cattle, or in steeds, That, Indra-Agni, let us gain;
10When heroes prompt in worship call Indra and Agni, Lords of steeds, Beside the Soma juice effused.
11Call hither with the song and lauds those who best slay the foemen, those Who take delight in hymns of praise.
12Slay ye the wicked man whose thought is evil of the demon kind. Slay him who stays the waters, slay the Serpent with your deadly dart.
