Introduction

        
Rigveda 1.46 is a hymn of 15 verses addressed to Asvins. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 1, a collection of hymns by many families of poets. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 31v-32r. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1Now Morning with her earliest light shines forth, dear Daughter of the Sky: High, Aṣvins, I extol your praise,
2Sons of the Sea, mighty to save discoverers of riches, ye Gods with deep thought who find out wealth.
3Your giant coursers hasten on over the region all in flames, – When your car flies with winged steeds.
4He, liberal, lover of the flood, Lord of the House, the vigilant, Chiefs! with oblations feeds you full.
5Ye have regard unto our hymns, Nâsatyas, thinking of our words: Drink boldly of the Soma juice.

          

        

        
        


          

            §6-15 · RV 1.46.6-15 (aṣṭaka 1, adhyāya 3, varga 34); Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 fol. 32r

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
6Vouchsafe to us, O Aṣvin Pair, such strength as, with attendant light, May through the darkness carry us.
7Come in the ship of these our hymns to bear you to the hither shore O Aṣvins, harness ye the car.
8The heaven's wide vessel is your own on the flood's shore your chariot waits Drops, with the hymn, have been prepared.
9Kaṇvas, the drops are in the heaven; the wealth is at the waters' place: Where will ye manifest your form?
10Light came to lighten up the branch, the Sun appeared as it were gold: And with its-tongue shone forth the dark.
11The path of sacrifice was made to travel to the farther goal: The road of heaven was manifest.
12The singer of their praise awaits whatever grace the Aṣvins give, who save when Soma gladdens them.
13Ye dwellers with Vivasvan come, auspicious, as to Manu erst; come to the Soma and our praise.
14O circumambient Aṣvins, Dawn follows the brightness of your way: Approve with beams our solemn rites.
15Drink ye of our libations, grant protection, O ye Aṣvins Twain, With aids which none may interrupt.
