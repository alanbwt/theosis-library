Introduction

        
Stephanus page 17 of Plato's Timaeus (17a-17d): Socrates counts the guests and recalls yesterday's discussion of the best city.



The manuscript is Paris, Bibliothèque nationale de France, grec 1807, known to editors as Plato A. It was copied in Constantinople around 850-875 by the scribe of the so-called philosophical collection, and is the oldest surviving medieval manuscript of the Timaeus and Critias (tetralogies VIII-IX: Clitophon, Republic, Timaeus, Critias, Minos, Laws, Epinomis, Letters). Each page has two columns of 44 lines. The Timaeus fills fol. 114r-144v and the Critias fol. 145r-151r.



On the manuscript this page of text runs over fol. 114r col. b to fol. 114v col. a. Each section below is linked to the folio side(s) that carry it. The mapping comes from reading the first words of every column on the images and finding them in Burnet's text: fol. 114r col. b begins ‹εἷς, δύο, τρεῖς· ὁ δὲ δὴ› (Tim. 17a); fol. 114v col. a begins ‹πόλεως, εἴτε τις ἔξωθεν ἢ καὶ› (Tim. 17d).



The Greek is John Burnet's critical text (Oxford, 1902), not a transcription of this manuscript; the scribe's spelling, accents and occasional readings differ from it. The English is R. G. Bury's Loeb translation (1929).

            §17a · Plato, Timaeus 17a; Paris gr. 1807, fol. 114r col. b
            

              
              
              
            

          


          
          

            
Manuscript

            
            
            
            
Where on the image
Tim. 17a stands in Paris gr. 1807, fol. 114r col. b. Checked on the image (column openings read on the BnF photographs and found in Burnet's text, quoted in Burnet's spelling): fol. 114r col. b begins ‹εἷς, δύο, τρεῖς· ὁ δὲ δὴ› (Tim. 17a).

          

          

          

            
Greek · Burnet

            
ΣΩ. εἷς, δύο, τρεῖς· ὁ δὲ δὴ τέταρτος ἡμῖν, ὦ φίλε Τίμαιε, ποῦ τῶν χθὲς μὲν δαιτυμόνων, τὰ νῦν δὲ ἑστιατόρων;


ΤΙ. ἀσθένειά τις αὐτῷ συνέπεσεν, ὦ Σώκρατες· οὐ γὰρ ἂν ἑκὼν τῆσδε ἀπελείπετο τῆς συνουσίας.


ΣΩ. οὐκοῦν σὸν τῶνδέ τε ἔργον καὶ τὸ ὑπὲρ τοῦ ἀπόντος ἀναπληροῦν μέρος;

            
          


          

            
English · R. G. Bury (1929)

            
Soc. One, two, three,—but where, my dear Timaeus, is the fourth of our guests of yesterday, our hosts of today?


Tim. Some sickness has befallen him, Socrates; for he would never have stayed away from our gathering of his own free will.


Soc. Then the task of filling the place of the absent one falls upon you and our friends here, does it not?


Tim. Undoubtedly, and we shall do our best not to come short;

          

        

        
        


          

            §17b · Plato, Timaeus 17b; Paris gr. 1807, fol. 114r col. b
            

              
              
              
            

          


          
          

            
Manuscript

            
            
            
            
Where on the image
Tim. 17b stands in Paris gr. 1807, fol. 114r col. b. Checked on the image (column openings read on the BnF photographs and found in Burnet's text, quoted in Burnet's spelling): fol. 114r col. b begins ‹εἷς, δύο, τρεῖς· ὁ δὲ δὴ› (Tim. 17a).

          

          

          

            
Greek · Burnet

            
ΤΙ. πάνυ μὲν οὖν, καὶ κατὰ δύναμίν γε οὐδὲν ἐλλείψομεν· οὐδὲ γὰρ ἂν εἴη δίκαιον, χθὲς ὑπὸ σοῦ ξενισθέντας οἷς ἦν πρέπον ξενίοις, μὴ οὐ προθύμως σὲ τοὺς λοιποὺς ἡμῶν ἀνταφεστιᾶν.


ΣΩ. ἆρʼ οὖν μέμνησθε ὅσα ὑμῖν καὶ περὶ ὧν ἐπέταξα εἰπεῖν;


ΤΙ. τὰ μὲν μεμνήμεθα, ὅσα δὲ μή, σὺ παρὼν ὑπομνήσεις· μᾶλλον δέ, εἰ μή τί σοι χαλεπόν, ἐξ ἀρχῆς διὰ βραχέων πάλιν ἐπάνελθε αὐτά, ἵνα βεβαιωθῇ μᾶλλον παρʼ ἡμῖν.

            
          


          

            
English · R. G. Bury (1929)

            
for indeed it would not be at all right, after the splendid hospitality we received from you yesterday, if we—that is, those who are left of us—failed to entertain you cordially in return.


Soc. Well, then, do you remember the extent and character of the subjects which I proposed for your discussion?


Tim. In part we do remember them; and of what we have forgotten you are present to remind us. Or rather, if it is not a trouble, recount them again briefly from the beginning, so as to fix them more firmly in our minds.

ΤΙ. ναί.

Soc. Did we not begin by dividing off the class of land-workers in it, and all other crafts, from the class of its defenders?


Tim. Yes.
