Introduction

        
Rigveda 8.83 is a hymn of 9 verses addressed to all the gods. It stands in book (maṇḍala) 8, the book of the Kaṇva and Aṅgiras poets. The Rigveda is the oldest collection of Sanskrit hymns, composed in the second half of the second millennium BCE and handed down orally for many centuries before it was written down.
The manuscript shown is Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41 (University of Pennsylvania), an undated paper manuscript of the padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text, in which every word is written separately and compounds are split) of the whole Ṛgveda in Devanāgarī, 884 leaves in fascicles, 7-9 lines to the page, accents in red; this hymn stands on fol. 649v-650r. Each section below gives the verses that end on one page; the page boundaries were read on the images (the opening words of every page), not counted out. Where the manuscript is a padapāṭha (the word-by-word recitation text), the section shows Aufrecht's continuous (saṃhitā) text first and, as the edition text, the padapāṭha of the same verses, which is the form written on the page.

Manuscript

            
            
            
            
Where on the image
Penn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 41, fol. 649v (numbered 71v by the scribe) (OPenn image 650, upper leaf). Checked on the image: the Rigveda text on this page begins in RV 8.82.2 ‹-sutāsaḥ mādayiṣṇavaḥ piba dadhṛk›. The next page with Rigveda text, fol. 650r (numbered 72r by the scribe), begins in RV 8.83.4 ‹vāmam varuṇa śaṃsyam vāmam›. Verses 8.83.1-3 end on this page.

1de̱vānā̱m id avo̍ ma̱hat tad ā vṛ̍ṇīmahe va̱yam | vṛṣṇā̍m a̱smabhya̍m ū̱taye̍ ||

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
1WE choose unto ourselves that high protection of the Mighty Gods That it may help and succour us.
2May they be ever our allies, Varuṇa, Mitra, Aryaman, Far-seeing Gods who prosper us.
3Ye furtherers of holy Law, transport us safe o'er many woes, As over water-floods in ships.

English · Ralph T. H. Griffith (1889-1897)

                    
4Dear wealth be Aryaman to us, Varuṇa dear wealth meet for praise: Dear wealth we choose unto ourselves.
5For Sovrans of dear wealth are ye, Âdityas, not of sinner's wealth, Ye sapient Gods who slay the foe.
6We in our homes, ye Bounteous Ones, and while we journey on the road, Invoke you, Gods, to prosper us.
7Regard us, Indra, Vishṇu, here, ye Aṣvins and the Marut host, Us who are kith and kin to you.
8Ye Bounteous Ones, from time of old we here set forth our brotherhood, Our kinship in the Mother's womb.
9Then come with Indra for your chief, as early day, ye Bounteous Gods Yea, I address you now for this.
